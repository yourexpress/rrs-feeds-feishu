Tabular-to-image encoding methods, which transform tabular data into image-like representations, have shown promising results in predictive tasks. Despite their benefits, these methods follow a centralized paradigm, in which the data are collected and models are trained on a central device. This raises privacy and security concerns, particularly the risk of data leakage, unauthorized data collection and access to users' private information. Federated Learning (FL) provides a decentralized framework in which multiple clients jointly optimize a shared model without directly sharing training data. We introduce Fed-IGTD, a horizontal FL-based approach in which clients share a common feature space and upload only local statistics, from which the server reconstructs the global correlation matrix and obtains a feature-to-pixel mapping to train a convolutional neural network via Federated Averaging (FedAvg). We studied two differential privacy (DP) mechanisms: (i) a one-shot Gaussian mechanism protecting the correlation-aggregation step; and (ii) a client-level DP-FedAvg variant protecting the training with privacy loss composed via a Renyi-DP accountant.
