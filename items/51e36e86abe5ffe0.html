Flash floods in Hanoi submerge roads within minutes of heavy rain onset, yet public flood advisories remain retrospective. Hydrodynamic models could supply forward-looking depth guidance, but they are typically run offline for historical events, and prior studies have not evaluated whether physics-based simulation, spatial serving, and vehicle-aware routing can operate together within a fixed refresh cycle using a cloud solver and a single commodity server. This paper evaluates an end-to-end prototype of an automated flood digital twin designed to meet a 15-minute refresh cycle under data scarcity. The architecture organizes execution into decoupled, event-driven workflows that chain weather-gated 3Di simulations through hydraulic restart states, serve flood depths through a dual-track spatial layer, and publish road-edge depths via standardized output contracts. Evaluated on a 57-run storm replay in the My Dinh and Tay Mo sector of Hanoi driven by satellite rainfall estimates, every cycle completed within the 15-minute deadline, publishing a ready edge table at a median of 537 s after slot start.
