Knowledge tracing (KT) models can estimate item parameters through an item response decoder trained solely for next-response prediction, yet predictive accuracy does not guarantee parameter recovery. We study 2PL, GPCM, and NRM decoders paired with LSTM, Transformer, and DKVMN encoders. Response-model derivatives show why discrimination and option slope updates depend directly on the scale and spread of estimated ability. Affine logit identities show how item-dependent shifts and rescaling in estimated ability are absorbed into item parameters without changing response probabilities. In simulations, difficulty, mean threshold, and correct-option intercept recovery remain high under shuffled order across representation widths, whereas discrimination and keyed option slope contrast recovery require wider item representations and degrade further under fixed item order.
