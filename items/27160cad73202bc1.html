Moving operands to a processor can cost more than the arithmetic performed on them, and for small binary similarity and search workloads data movement can dominate. We present HADES, a programmable processing-near-memory engine built around one shared 32x32-bit 2R1W register-file macro that holds both data and program. Twelve 16-bit opcodes, three working registers and a 32-slot program express ten search workloads in 8 to 19 slots, and SCANP and SCANSEL shorten dual-row and selected scans. A post-layout 2x2 Tiny Tapeout implementation closes timing at 40 MHz with 26,968 square microns of standard cells beside a 15,744 square microns macro. All HADES results come from RTL or gate-level simulation of this implementation, and HADES energy is estimated. Against ESP8266 and ESP32 microcontrollers running plain C, and with an ideal host, HADES answers a resident query 10x to 32x and 2.2x to 11x faster, respectively. End to end, it wins at the evaluated batch sizes of B = 16 and B = 256 against both boards and loses the single-query (B = 1) runs to the ESP32. An Arduino Nano runs a restricted subset.
