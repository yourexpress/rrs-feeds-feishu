Sparse attention speeds up long-context decoding by reading few keys of the KV cache, but existing methods do not control the error of a query, and on real KV caches their errors have heavy tails. We show that perturbing the score of every key with an independent Exp(1) variable turns threshold retrieval into sampling keys with probability proportional to their attention weights. Lowering the threshold along a geometric grid gives nested samples, a Horvitz--Thompson estimate of the output at every level, and confidence bounds that hold simultaneously over all levels, so each head stops at the first level whose certificate meets the target error $\varepsilon$. With fresh perturbations at every decoding step, each certificate fails with probability at most $\delta$ during generation. At a suitable threshold, the estimator reads $O(\varepsilon^{-2}\log(1/\delta))$ values regardless of the context length, and no adaptive method reads fewer, up to constants. A 4-bit scan with deterministic bounds, shared across a grouped-query-attention group, generates the candidates exactly but reads every key.
