Qubit readout, or qubit state discrimination, remains one of the slowest and most error-prone operations in superconducting quantum processors, making fast, accurate, and hardware-efficient classification essential for scalable quantum computing. Conventional discriminators integrate the I/Q record into a single point, discarding evidence a relaxing qubit leaves behind; full-resolution feed-forward classifiers recover it but are too costly to deploy under this constraint. Recurrent classifiers retain full resolution far more cheaply, but their loop-carried hidden state cannot fit a single AI Engine (AIE) tile's register capacity and spills to data memory, adding latency that grows with sequence length. We present a hardware-aware LSTM accelerator for an AMD Versal device that resolves this by partitioning at the dependency boundary - independent input projections on the AIE array, the loop-carried update in programmable logic (PL) - keeping pace with a 64 ns input cadence under a 1,000 ns budget.
