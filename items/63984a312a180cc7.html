Safety risks from fine-tuning are commonly characterized by the harmfulness of the training data:harmful fine-tuning is considered dangerous, while benign fine-tuning is often assumed to preservealignment. We argue that this view misses a more fundamental factor-the behavioral transition inducedby supervision. Harmful fine-tuning and safety-degrading benign fine-tuning can both be understoodas learning the same refusal-to-compliance transition, but on different data distributions. We studywhether such a transition learned on benign inputs can generalize to harmful requests. We constructbenign examples that an aligned model initially tends to refuse and pair them with their originalharmless responses, creating a discrepancy between the model's initial refusal and the supervisedanswer. Fine-tuning on these examples increases harmful compliance, but only when two conditionshold together: the discrepancy is large enough, and it cannot be resolved through a narrow surface-levelshortcut.
