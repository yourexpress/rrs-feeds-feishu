What the Loss Curve Misses: FP4 Pretraining Slows the Acquisition of Rare Facts

arXiv cs.LG · 2026-11-09 · 论文PDF / 每日精选

FP4 预训练实证：损失曲线相同时罕见事实习得变慢——NVFP4 需 1.32-1.52x 曝光次数才达到 BF16 的召回（验证损失仅差 0.5-2%）

Low-precision pretraining is usually judged by validation loss. We ask whether matching loss also means learning rare facts as efficiently. We train transformers from scratch with emulated 4-bit matrix multiplications on synthetic biographies with controlled exposure counts, on their own and mixed into web text. NVFP4 needs 1.32$\times$ and 1.52$\times$ as many exposures as BF16 to match its recall while validation loss differs by only 0.5% and 2%, and 1.26$\times$ and 1.30$\times$ when both models are evaluated with BF16 matrix multiplications. Under our primary readout, the exposure cost is about 1.2$\times$ at matched loss. Zero-mean gradient noise reproduces the cost of quantizing only the backward pass, which barely moves the loss. In a controlled experiment at twice the NVFP4 error, noise fed only to Adam's second-moment estimate acts like a lower learning rate and its cost fades, whereas noise in the update lowers its signal-to-noise ratio and carries about two thirds of the cost through the end of training.
