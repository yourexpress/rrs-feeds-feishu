Wiring and bandwidth bottlenecks between a quantum processor and room-temperature electronics can be reduced by placing digital control at the cryogenic stage, but commercial foundry standard-cell libraries are not specified for operation at 4 K. A cryogenic recharacterization flow is therefore developed for a commercial 22 nm FD-SOI foundry standard-cell library using commercially available cryogenic compact-device models, while retaining the original cell circuits, layouts, logic functions, and implementation semantics and remaining compatible with the commercial synthesis, place-and-route, and signoff methodology used at room temperature. Body-bias dependence is quantified over 38,640 matched slew/load points at zero, +/-0.9 V, and +/-1.8 V body bias. Relative to full bias, zero-bias operation increases the median rising and falling delays by 38.6x and 33.0x, while approximately 90% of transitions exceed the foundry-defined maximum-transition envelope. The 0.8 V-to-0.4 V level shifter is even more sensitive to body bias: its worst-case falling delay decreases from 30.6 ns at zero bias to 0.56 ns at +/-1.8 V.
