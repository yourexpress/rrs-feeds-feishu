It was recently shown (arXiv:2610.09618) that the infimum of the approximation ratios of deterministic constant-round LOCAL algorithms for minimum dominating set on planar graphs is 7. The $(7+\varepsilon)$-approximation given there runs in $T_0+O((c_0/\varepsilon)^κ)$ rounds with $κ=\ln 3/\ln(24/23)\approx 25.81$, and it is asked there whether this exponent can be substantially reduced. We replace the heaviest-edge star selection in its clustering step by a random-centre rule: every part becomes a centre independently with probability $p$, and every non-centre joins its heaviest centre neighbour. For planar graphs this gives $κ<5.89$ with $p=3/8$ (three fair coins per part and level) and $κ<6.7$ with $p=1/2$. An analysis that counts only the join edges cannot go below $5.74$. For every proper minor-closed class of density $a$, the new exponent is never worse, and with about $\frac12\log_2 a$ fair coins per part and level it is smaller by a factor that tends to $8$ as the density grows. This is optimal for the rule when $a$ is an integer and the class contains all $K_{a,n}$. The planar bounds hold verbatim on the torus, the projective plane and the Klein bottle.
