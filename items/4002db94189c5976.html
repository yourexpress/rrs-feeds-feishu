Zero-Gated Cross-Attention

arXiv (cs.LG) · 2026-10-31 · 论文PDF

Injecting non-text modalities into frozen language models often introduces early optimization instability, making it difficult to separate architectural gains from transient training dynamics. We introduce Inductively Gated Cross-Attention (IGCA), a zero-initialized cross-attention bridge that preserves pretrained model behavior at initialization while enabling gradual multimodal conditioning. Integrated across six decoder layers of frozen Falcon-40B, open_llama_30b, and Bloom-7b1 backbones (with 40,404,961,288 trainable adapter parameters used during training), IGCA acts as an exact algebraic no-op at initialization with a maximum output discrepancy of 0.00e+00. Over 59,500 training steps on Falcon-40B, the training loss decreases from 3.9403 to 0.0007 (with final ten step losses of [0.0001, 0.0003, 0.0005, 0.0002, 0.0005, 0.0011, 0.0005, 0.0006, 0.0005, 0.0007]). On held-out evaluation, the best gate-on checkpoint achieves a loss of 2.2201 compared to 4.3469 for the gate-off baseline -- a 48.9% reduction (2.1268 nats) -- while average held-out loss improves from 4.3423 to 2.2498 (1.9075 nats) with a mean gate magnitude of $\vert{}\tanh(α)\vert{} = 0.0713$.
