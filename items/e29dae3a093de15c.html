Encoding and Propagation of Grammatical Number in Large Language Models

arXiv · 2026-11-06 · 论文PDF

Sparse autoencoders (SAEs) have become an increasingly popular means of translating Large Language Model (LLM) hidden state vectors into a human-comprehensible list of features or symbols. As such, SAEs may provide insight into an LLM's knowledge representation and internal ``mental'' grammar - topics of pragmatic interest for mechanistic interpretation of AI behavior and of theoretical interest for the study of in silico representations of human language. Language models with fewer than 100M parameters, trained on small corpora, nonetheless exhibit strong grammatical competence and discourse coherence, despite producing content of limited reliability. We use SAEs that we trained on one such model (Pythia-70M) to conduct an in-depth examination of how a single grammatical phenomenon, number agreement, is represented and processed in an LLM.
