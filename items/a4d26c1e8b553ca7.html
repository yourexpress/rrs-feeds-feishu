Precision-Aware Learning Rate Transfer Across Block Count in LLM Post-Training

arXiv · 2026-11-23 · 论文PDF

The post-training of pretrained large language models, including supervised finetuning (SFT), direct preference optimization (DPO), and reinforcement learning with verifiable reward (RLVR), is very sensitive to the learning rate, and tuning it through grid search is costly in both computation and memory. In this work, we propose \textbf{TipSweep}, a method to transfer the learning rate across the number of trainable transformer blocks. To do so efficiently, we search for the best learning rate by training only the last $k_0$ blocks (in our experiments, typically $k_0=1$), and provide scaling rules that predict the optimal learning rate when training any set of $k$ blocks, including when $k=L$ (the full transformer). Across experiments with models ranging from 1 to 7 billion parameters, we observe that the optimal learning rate follows precision-dependent scaling rules: the optimal rate scales approximately as $k^{-1}$ with float32 (FP32) master weights, but as $k^{-1/2}$ when updates are applied directly to bfloat16 (BF16) weights without a master copy.
