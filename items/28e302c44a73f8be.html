When Does Jacobian Fine-Tuning Stabilize Neural PDE Emulators?

arXiv (cs.LG) · 2026-10-30 · 论文PDF

Neural emulators of chaotic PDEs are trained on one-step prediction but deployed autoregressively, where their errors compound. Jacobian-based regularisers aim to curb this growth, but in a chaotic system part of the growth is physical and cannot be trained away. We separate the two sources of this error with a first-order decomposition. We compare the emulator's error with a counterfactual that propagates the same one-step mistakes through the solver's Jacobian, giving an amplification ratio: the factor by which the model's own dynamics amplify its errors. We study 21 configurations across four PDEs, five architectures and two model sizes (8-10k and 33k parameters), with errors measured at one Lyapunov time. Jacobian fine-tuning reduced the error by more than 10% for 1 of 34 seeds whose amplification ratio was at most 1.25, and for all 20 seeds whose ratio was at least four; for the latter, the error fell by a factor of 8 (small models) and 55 (33k models) in geometric mean over seeds. The gain approximately equals the one-step accuracy cost of fine-tuning divided by the amplification ratio.
