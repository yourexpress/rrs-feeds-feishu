What matters in classical machine learning for non-intrusive load monitoring? A factorial benchmark on REFIT with validation on unseen houses

arXiv (cs.LG) · 2026-10-31 · 论文PDF

Published classical machine-learning results for appliance ON/OFF classification in nonintrusive load monitoring (NILM) range from F1-type scores of 0.53 to 0.98, and the sources of this spread have not been separated. This study separates them with a factorial benchmark on REFIT: seven models, two feature sets and four class-imbalance strategies for five appliances (270 fitted configurations), each evaluated by pooled nested cross-validation and on houses unseen during training, with robustness checks. An analysis of variance shows that the appliance explains 38% of the variance in F1, the feature set 13% and the model only 8%. Engineered rolling-window features raised the mean F1 score by 52% under both protocols. Other conclusions changed on unseen houses: the support-weighted F1 score of the best pooled configurations fell from 0.548 to 0.447, the differences between the models selected by pooled validation were no longer significant, and the benefit of class weighting over no correction rose from 32% to 69%.
