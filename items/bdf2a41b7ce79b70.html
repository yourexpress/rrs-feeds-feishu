Graph diffusion models are competitive approaches for generating complex graph-structured data. However, when such distributions have strict structural constraints, diffusion models may fail to capture them exactly, resulting in the generation of invalid graphs. In this paper, we propose a method for grounding graph diffusion models in domain-specific knowledge via First Order Logic (FOL), leveraging its expressive and interpretable formalism to impose structural constraints on the generative process. We incorporate domain knowledge using classifier guidance, a technique that conditions the diffusion model's distribution on external variables to exert control over the sampled data. During the generative process, classifier guidance steers the denoising trajectory to optimize an objective function, which we define as a fuzzy and differentiable relaxation of the logical rules. Our methodology requires only the specification of constraints in FOL, whose satisfaction is encouraged at inference time to guide a pre-trained generative model.
