Building heterogeneous multistep workflows in automated microscopy: reward design and knowledge transfer for autonomous structure property discovery

arXiv cs.LG · 2026-11-08 · 论文PDF

Autonomous microscopy can turn fast acquisition into scientific progress when it decides not only where to measure, but also what information to acquire, at what scan rate, and when to recalibrate the instrument. We develop a budget constrained framework for such multistep structure-property workflows. It combines explicit scientific rewards, action costs, instrument observation models, transfer of pre experiment knowledge into the online campaign, and evaluation kept independent of the controller. The transferred knowledge includes predictor accuracy and computational cost, empirical learner maturity functions that weight model-derived rewards, and drift characteristics established before controller training. Using a real electron energy-loss spectroscopy dataset, we run two complementary campaigns. In the first, the controller chooses between imaging and spectroscopy, image size, and scan speed. In the second, the instrument drifts and the controller also decides when to recalibrate. The campaigns show that response discovery and structure-property learning favor different surrogate models and acquisition strategies.
