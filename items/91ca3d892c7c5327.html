Calibration and Conformal Prediction for Medical Question Answering in Arabic and English: Evidence from Two Non-Parallel Benchmarks

arXiv cs.LG · 2026-11-12 · 论文PDF

Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly tested on medical exam questions, but accuracy alone does not show when an answer can be trusted. I study the calibration and uncertainty of two open-weight models, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and Jais-adapted-7B-chat (both run in 4-bit precision), on 2,816 English multiple-choice questions from MedMCQA and 4,927 Arabic multiple-choice questions from MedAraBench. Accuracy was 0.561 (English) and 0.430 (Arabic) for Qwen, and 0.369 and 0.308 for Jais. Raw option probabilities were overconfident in all four settings (expected calibration error 0.242 to 0.486), and a single temperature parameter removed 82% to 93% of that error. Split conformal prediction reached its nominal coverage in all 24 settings I tested (within 0.006), but the sets were large when accuracy was low: at 90% coverage, temperature-scaled sets contained on average 63% (Qwen, English), 78% (Qwen, Arabic), 86% (Jais, English) and 89% (Jais, Arabic) of the options. The margin between the top two option probabilities separated correct from incorrect answers best for Qwen on English (AUROC 0.734) and worst for Jais on Arabic (0.579).
