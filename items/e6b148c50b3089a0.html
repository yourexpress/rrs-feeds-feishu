ApproxBrain: Certified Sufficiency Frontiers in Connectome-Constrained Neural Systems

arXiv · 2026-11-06 · 论文PDF

Background. A connectome specifies the substrate available to a nervous system, not how much of it a given computation requires. Reported compact sufficient circuits are point estimates: one subset, one task, one fidelity threshold, one parameterisation. Methods. We formalise computational sufficiency as a cost-indexed family of neuron subsets conditioned on a task and a tolerance pair (permitted degradation epsilon, permitted failure fraction rho), separate task adequacy from phenotype fidelity, and certify candidates on held-out parameter draws with one-sided bounds rather than reporting a single minimum. We measure three quantities a single circuit cannot express: non-decomposability, degeneracy, and an intervention-derived role taxonomy. Results. On a released walking simulator, the published 148-neuron circuit is certified sufficient with margin (mean Dtask = 0.017 over 32 held-out draws, no failure), no 146- or 147-neuron subset of it is sufficient, and removing any one of its three interneurons destroys adequacy.
