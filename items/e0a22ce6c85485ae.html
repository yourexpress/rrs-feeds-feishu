Modeling multi-scale dynamics in urban road networks is essential for intelligent transportation systems but is often hindered by the computational cost of long-range historical context and the challenge of fusing heterogeneous modalities. Existing spatio-temporal frameworks face a trade-off between sequence length and memory overhead, while frequently neglecting the transient effects of exogenous factors such as road incidents and weather. To address these limitations, we propose SpecLST, a frequency-enhanced long-short-term spatio-temporal multimodal framework. SpecLST leverages a fast Fourier transform-based frequency-domain representation module to efficiently encode multi-week historical dependencies, bypassing the memory bottlenecks of standard attention mechanisms over raw long sequences. Heterogeneous context is integrated through attention-based fusion, followed by a sparse Mixture-of-Experts backbone with graph augmentation for spatio-temporal representation learning. Additionally, a frequency-enhanced residual head isolates high-frequency transients, improving sensitivity to abrupt traffic anomalies.
