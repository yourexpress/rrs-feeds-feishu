Bridging the Semantic Gap from Natural Language Specifications to RTL with Layered Formal Guidance

arXiv · 2026-11-23 · 论文PDF

While large language models (LLMs) have shown promise in generating register-transfer level (RTL) code from natural-language specifications, their reliability remains limited on designs with complex control logic and temporal behavior. This stems from a fundamental semantic gap: high-level natural language often leaves implementation-critical details underspecified, forcing LLMs to infer unstated assumptions. This paper presents Layered Formal Guidance, a novel framework that bridges this semantic gap by using formal properties as an intermediate semantic layer between natural-language specifications and RTL generation. Our framework applies formal guidance across three layers: property-driven specification augmentation before generation, property-consistency checking after generation, and counterexample-guided refinement after checking. Experiments on RTLLM benchmarks and various open-source IP designs demonstrate that our framework achieves higher functional correctness and lower sampled output-trace diversity.
