Class-Conditional Conformal Prediction for Signal Temporal Logic Inference with Many Classes

Signal Temporal Logic (STL) inference provides interpretable formulas for time series classification. For multiclass classification, however, accurate predictions do not necessarily ensure that the inferred formulas are satisfied consistently with the true class. We introduce the positive satisfaction rate and codeword satisfaction rate to quantify satisfaction of required formulas and consistency with the complete class codeword, respectively. We develop a two-stage procedure that first learns discriminative formulas using a margin-based objective and then refines predicate thresholds toward a small positive robustness target while fixing the formula structure and temporal intervals. Satisfaction and ranking penalties discourage violations of required formulas and loss of class separation. The decoding loss is used as a nonconformity score and calibrated separately for each class, providing finite-sample class-conditional coverage under intra-class exchangeability. Experiments on naval, synthetic, and robot manipulation trajectories demonstrate discrepancies between classification accuracy and formula satisfaction.
