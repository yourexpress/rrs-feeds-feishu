How Sparse Probability Maps Shape Mixture-of-Experts Routing

arXiv cs.LG · 2026-10-06 · 论文PDF

Mixture-of-experts (MoE) routers typically apply softmax to the router scores and keep the top-K experts, making every token use exactly K experts. Sparsity-inducing probability maps such as sparsemax, alpha-entmax and normmax can adaptively assign exact zeros to selected experts, and therefore appear to offer token-dependent expert participation, even when using the same top-K machinery. In this work, we study whether and how this sparsity survives training. We train matched 300M and 1B top-2 MoE language models with softmax, 1.5-entmax, sparsemax and 2-normmax, and find that the maps behave very differently once trained: at 1B, entmax discards 30% less probability mass than softmax while almost never dropping a selected expert, sparsemax retains the most mass, and normmax routes 21% of tokens to a single expert.
