AutoC2V: Synthesis-Guided LLM Training for QoR-Optimized C++-to-Verilog Generation

arXiv cs.AR · 2026-11-07 · 论文PDF

High-level synthesis (HLS) can translate C/C++ kernels into register-transfer level (RTL) designs, yet generating high-quality hardware still relies heavily on manual pragma tuning and repeated synthesis evaluation. We propose AutoC2V, a C++-to-Verilog generation framework driven by real synthesis feedback that enables large language models (LLMs) to directly generate RTL with improved quality of results (QoR) from synthesizable C++ kernels. We construct training data from real synthesis results, including synthesizable C++ source code, the corresponding Verilog RTL, QoR metrics, and optimization objectives. Using this dataset, we align the model toward synthesis-aware RTL generation through Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). To reduce the high cost of repeatedly invoking real synthesis during GRPO, we design the QoR Prediction Reward Model (QoR RM), which predicts latency and resource metrics by encoding RTL into long-context semantic representations, thereby providing low-cost and continuous QoR reward signals for policy optimization.
