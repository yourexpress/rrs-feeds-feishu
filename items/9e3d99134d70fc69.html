This work investigates the application of the Membership Inference Test (MINT) to large language models (LLMs) fine-tuned with LoRA, aiming to determine whether specific samples were used during training and address concerns about data privacy, leakage, and transparency. Building on our previous LoRA-MINT framework, which relies exclusively on perplexity under a strict black-box setting, we propose an approach based on intermediate model representations, including activations and parameter-dependent signals. These representations enable a MINT classifier to distinguish training from non-training samples without requiring full access to model parameters. We evaluated the method on four LLMs (Qwen, Phi-4, DeepSeek-V3, and LLaMA 3.2-3B) fine-tuned on three datasets covering medical, mathematical, and academic reasoning tasks. Results show consistent performance across architectures and domains, achieving scores between 95% and 98%, with robustness to variations in model configurations and data distributions.
