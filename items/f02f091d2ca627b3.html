On the Power of Alternating Local Certification with Constant-Size Certificates

We study alternation hierarchies for distributed decision in anonymous networks with constant-size certificates. A prover and a disprover alternately assign certificates to nodes over a constant number of rounds, after which each node decides whether to accept based on its own input and the certificates observed in its neighborhood. An instance is accepted if all nodes accept. Unlike previous work on distributed alternation hierarchies, which considered restricted local computation (Reiter, PODC 2024) or logarithmic-size certificates (Feuilloley et al., ICALP 2016), we focus on constant-size certificates and the role of neighborhood information in determining expressive power. We consider three models: SET, where nodes observe only the set of certificate sequences of their neighbors; MULTISET, where multiplicities are also visible; and PORTS, where neighbors can additionally be distinguished by local port numbers. We establish that SET is strictly weaker than MULTISET, which is in turn strictly weaker than PORTS, and that all three alternation hierarchies are strict at every level.
