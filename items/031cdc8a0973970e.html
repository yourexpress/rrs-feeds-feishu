Raman spectroscopy generates large volumes of unlabeled data that are difficult to exploit with conventional supervised learning. This work introduces a multi-domain self-supervised learning framework based on a masked autoencoder (MAE) for learning transferable Raman spectral representations from heterogeneous biological, chemical, forensic, and materials datasets. The proposed pretraining strategy incorporates physics-aware objectives by combining masked intensity reconstruction with a sparse Voigt-profile decoder that models spectral peak structure through differentiable peak centers, widths, and amplitudes. A denoising objective is further included to improve robustness to spectral corruption. The learned encoder is evaluated on substance classification, component identification, concentration estimation, and spectral denoising under linear probing, attention-based probing, end-to-end fine-tuning, and training from scratch. Experimental results show that self-supervised pretraining is most beneficial for challenging tasks with limited labeled data.
