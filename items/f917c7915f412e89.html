Can Latent Reasoning Drive LLM Outputs Without a Trace?

arXiv · 2026-11-23 · 论文PDF

LLMs typically solve complex tasks with chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning, which is also the main tool for monitoring them for misaligned behavior. However, standard transformers can also solve tasks with complete latent reasoning (CLR), carrying out the whole task internally and outputting only the final answer. Theory bounds the depth of CLR, the number of sequential steps it can carry out, and recent work measured it to be shallow. In this work, we ask whether CLR is deep enough for a model to pursue a hidden objective that requires reasoning. Such a model would generate a CoT that appears to solve the given task, while a latent multi-step computation decides its answer based on the hidden objective. First, we show that a simple auxiliary loss allows models to solve a multi-step task latently at least 5x deeper than previously reported. Then, in two common tasks, decision-making and code generation, we train a model to pursue such a hidden objective, which decides a loan or plants a vulnerability.
