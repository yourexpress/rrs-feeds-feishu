A Positional Realizability Theory of Length Generalization in Single-Head Attention

arXiv (cs.LG) · 2026-10-30 · 论文PDF

Why does length generalization (LG) depend so sharply on the positional encoding (PE)? Relative schemes extrapolate fixed offsets, absolute schemes fix positions, and neither handles growing offsets. We give a realizability theory for a deliberately controlled abstraction, a single positional-only attention head, that derives this well-documented empirical pattern and says precisely where it holds and breaks. The device is a simulation transfer operator $T_L$ that re-evaluates a selected length-$L_0$-realizing positional kernel on the longer grid, with a scalar $α_L \in [0,1]$ read off before any training from $(Φ, t_n)$ alone. Here $α_L$ serves as the computable witness of that taxonomy's derivation, not as a stronger empirical predictor.
