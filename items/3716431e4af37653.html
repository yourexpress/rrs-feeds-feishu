Analog compute-in-memory (CIM) enables energy-efficient AI inference by performing in-situ matrix multiplication. The growing demand for deploying larger models motivates chiplet integration to expand CIM capacity. However, simulation abstractions based on local operator costs and aggregate communication metrics obscure the impact of weight-mapping topology when CIM system scales up. We develop a phase analysis that quantifies how strongly mapping topology affects system performance across hardware configurations and how mapping optimization translates into latency gains. To evaluate and exploit this design space, we present Isobar, a simulation platform integrating CIM decompositions, heterogeneous execution, and resource modeling. Validated against silicon measurements and cycle-accurate simulation, Isobar provides reliable cost estimates that enable effective mapping search across model and hardware configurations. For Gemma-7B prefill, Isobar-guided mapping reduces communication time by 23.3% and end-to-end latency by 18.6% relative to the strongest baseline.
