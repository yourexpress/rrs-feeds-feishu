Graceful degradation is a key mechanism employed by large-scale systems to handle overload conditions. Its importance has grown further as GPU-intensive LLM inference introduces persistent capacity constraints that cannot be addressed through provisioning alone. Common degradation approaches rely on service-level mechanisms such as load shedding and circuit breakers, or on feature-level switches (some manually controlled) to preserve availability and quality. While effective at protecting services, such approaches lead to untestable product states and thereby unpredictable product quality. We present HiLO (High Load Optimizer), an automated product-level degradation system designed to handle graceful degradation at scale. HiLO maps observed load to a product state lattice: a small set of pre-validated product states, partially ordered by degradation severity. Dependencies are resolved at design time enabling deterministic and continuous control without requiring runtime dependency discovery.
