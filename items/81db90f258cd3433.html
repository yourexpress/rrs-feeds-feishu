When Are Predictions Enough? An Evaluation Protocol for Frozen Expert Composition

arXiv cs.LG · 2026-11-09 · 论文PDF

Practitioners often combine independently trained models while keeping their weights frozen. These experts are commonly combined through their final predictions, although their internal features may retain useful information that those predictions omit. We ask when combining features improves on combining predictions, and how to make that comparison fair. We introduce a reusable evaluation protocol, apply it to 21 frozen deepfake detectors from DF40 (Yan et al., 2024), and audit how evaluation choices change the conclusions. The protocol uses strong prediction baselines, compares simple and larger feature combiners, varies architecture and training data separately, and reports results by held-out generator with train/test overlap controls. Under leave-one-generator-out evaluation, logistic regression on the features of six architecture-diverse experts improves macro AUROC by 0.081 over the best tested prediction combiner in each fold, with gains on seven of eight generators.
