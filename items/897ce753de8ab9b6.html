Privacy-Preserving Text Inference for Health-Misinformation Classification and Semantic Retrieval

arXiv · 2026-11-06 · 论文PDF

Health-misinformation analysis presents a dual challenge: misleading claims can affect public well-being, while systems designed to detect them may expose sensitive user-submitted text. This motivates privacy-preserving inference for public-health applications in which user queries may contain confidential medical information and trained models and reference databases are maintained on organizational servers. We design a privacy-preserving text inference framework based on CKKS homomorphic encryption for health-misinformation classification and semantic retrieval. The classification pipeline fuses BioBERT embeddings, PCA-reduced MPNet embeddings, and SVD-reduced TF-IDF features, while the retrieval pipeline combines MPNet embeddings with ISCC-SCT semantic fingerprints through late fusion. We evaluate predictive performance and benchmark plaintext versus ciphertext-based execution. The hybrid classifier achieved an accuracy of 0.971 and a macro-F1 score of 0.950, with identical reported metrics under plaintext and ciphertext-based inference.
