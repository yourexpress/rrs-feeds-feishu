FireGate: Bounding Behaviour Change Before Training in Continual RL Fine-Tuning

arXiv cs.LG · 2026-11-09 · 论文PDF

Fine-tuning a deployed language model on one task after another can change behaviour that should stay fixed, such as earlier skills, general chat or refusals. Today, such forgetting is found only after training, on the benchmarks someone chose to run. This work bounds, before training, how often its outputs change on chosen prompt distributions. FireGate places each task's update, such as a new LoRA, behind a gate, which is a classifier on the model's frozen lower layers that decides for each prompt whether the update is used. Where no gate fires, the model computes exactly what the old one computes. As a result, on any prompt distribution, every bounded behaviour score changes by at most the gates' firing rate there over a whole task stream. Calibrating each gate on unlabeled prompts of what must not change fixes that rate before training, with a PAC guarantee, whatever weights training produces. Forgetting is thus bounded by the gate's false-alarm rate, and its price is the gate's power: no update, gated or not, that changes as few protected outputs can change new-task outputs more often than the best test of that level tells new-task prompts from protected ones.
