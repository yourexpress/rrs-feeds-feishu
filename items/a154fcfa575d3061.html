Rethinking Reasoning Effort Training in Large Language Models: On-Policy Distillation or Reinforcement Learning?

arXiv · 2026-11-24 · 论文PDF

Large language models have shown strong capabilities on complex tasks, yet how to control their reasoning effort remains a significant challenge for practical deployment. This paper provides a systematic investigation of reasoning-effort training, contrasting reinforcement learning (RL) with on-policy distillation (OPD). We find that compressing reasoning and separating effort tiers are governed by different signals. Length-penalized RL reliably compresses a single policy, reducing thinking on AIME25 by 70\% without accuracy loss, but collapses the tiers when they are trained jointly. Multi-teacher OPD separates them: across 33 benchmarks, low and medium use 58\% and 43\% fewer thinking tokens than high, while high improves on the reference policy by 3.1 points. We trace this difference to the training signal. A sequence-level reward cannot tell tier-specific behavior from shared behavior, whereas OPD supplies a tier-specific target at every visited state, including the decision to stop.
