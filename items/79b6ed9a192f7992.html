Approximate Adjoint Models for Memory and Compute-Efficient Online Learning of Hybrid Models

Differentiable programming enables online calibration of numerical models by minimizing errors accumulated along simulated trajectories. This includes estimating physical parameters and training neural sub-models in hybrid configurations. Gradients are typically computed using adjoint methods, or backpropagation through time (BPTT), whose computational and memory costs grow with the length of the training window. This limits the duration and resolution of hybrid-model training, a major bottleneck for realistic geophysical applications. Approaches like truncated BPTT reduces memory use by discarding sensitivities across truncation boundaries, introducing gradient bias and neglecting long-range dependencies. We propose a class of gradient approximation methods that preserve the adjoint recurrence over the full trajectory while approximating only the pullback operators within it. Exact pullbacks are computed by automatic differentiation at sparse anchor states only. Between anchors, sensitivities propagate either through the identity (Zero-order) or through a reused, frozen pullback (Pullback Reuse).
