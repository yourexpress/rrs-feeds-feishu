How Much Gradient Does Truncated Backprop Lose in Looped Transformers?

arXiv · 2026-10-20 · 论文PDF

Looped transformers reuse one block of layers many times and are often trained by backpropagating through only the last k loops.

Looped transformers reuse one block of layers many times and are often trained by backpropagating through only the last k loops. We measure how much of the weight gradient this throws away, exactly and for every k from one backward pass, on nine looped language models, and report three findings. First, on a trained model this lost gradient is set near the output: once the loop count is well above k, it barely changes with the loop count (within about 0.03 from 32 to 64 loops on the three models we tested, as a pre-registered test predicted for Parcae). Where the backward signal shrinks, $ρ^k$, with $ρ$ the signal's fitted shrink per loop, approximates it at moderate loop counts but overstates it at long ones, because the fitted $ρ$ drifts up as the fit takes in more loops. On Ouro the signal grows, and keeping two of four loops still loses 81% of the final output's gradient. Second, the lost gradient falls during fine-tuning, by 0.07-0.22 within 300 steps on three of four models, on held-out problems too.
