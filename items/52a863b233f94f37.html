Disentangling Monitor Hardware Artifacts and Smoothing Lag for Shift-Robust Intraoperative EEG and Hemodynamic Risk Calibration

Commercial intraoperative electroencephalography (EEG) and blood pressure monitors introduce cyclic impedance pulses, smoothing delays, direct-current (DC) drift, and cuff holds that confound risk forecasting and uncertainty calibration. We developed a causal signal-conditioning and shift-robust conformal calibration framework across 5,439 public anesthesia procedures. Streaming filters blank 10.13-min 64-Hz impedance checks, apply unclipped post-notch conditioning, separate 63-s suppression-ratio lag, and distinguish three-channel cuff inflations from dropouts across propofol-remifentanil total intravenous anesthesia (TIVA; N = 3,214), volatile anesthesia (N = 2,046), European endoscopy sedation (N = 171), and DC-coupled surgery (N = 8). De-impedanced cortical tree ensembles were compared against deep sequence models, paired with patient-cluster Subgroup-Covariate-Weighted Conformal Risk Control (CRC) and lag-buffered online Conformal Proportional-Integral-Derivative (PID) tracking. Pre-filter blanking removed 97.90% and 97.60% of false flatline conflicts while preserving 99.23% and 98.96% of deep suppression.
