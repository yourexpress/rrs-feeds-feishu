Critic-free methods are widely used in reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) for long chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning, avoiding difficult and costly critic training. Common alternatives lack token-level credit assignment and typically rely on grouped rollouts to estimate prompt-specific baselines. A critic can address both limitations, but long-CoT critic training is hindered by insufficient coverage of trajectories with diverse lengths and early gradients dominated by the newly initialized value head. We introduce S3AC (Stabilized, Selective, and Scalable Actor-Critic) for LLM reasoning, built around a practical critic-training recipe. The trained critic provides token-specific advantages and prompt-specific baselines. Using the prompt-level baseline to estimate difficulty, we propose Difficulty-Aware Prompt Sampling (DAPS). DAPS combines this estimate with outcome history to select prompts before generation, reducing rollout waste without offline difficulty initialization.
