NIMIS: A Structure-Agnostic, Chemically Informed Slot-Attention Framework for Demixing 1D $^1$H-NMR Mixture Spectra

The products of chemical reactions are usually complex mixtures that must be separated into their components before interpretable $^1$H-NMR spectra can be obtained. Direct analysis of the spectra of crude mixtures would accelerate reaction analysis and facilitate the exploration of new chemical space. We present NIMIS, a structure-agnostic, attention-based deep learning framework that groups the peaks of a 1D $^1$H-NMR mixture spectrum according to the component from which they originate. J-coupling information is introduced directly into the attention mechanism of a multi-head Transformer encoder, and the resulting contextualised peak embeddings are separated into components by slot attention. NIMIS was trained on 158,005 synthetic mixtures constructed from experimental spectra in the NMRexp database, covering molecules of up to 20 heavy atoms composed of C, H, N, O, S, F, Cl, Br and I. On held-out molecules it reaches a peak-to-component assignment accuracy of 82.28% for noise-free input and 77.34% with 10% integral noise, with a mean NIMIS score of 0.83 for correct assignments.
