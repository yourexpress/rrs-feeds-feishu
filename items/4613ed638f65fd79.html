Computing in the network (COIN) supports latency-sensitive Metaverse services, but blockchain commitment of persistent shared-state updates competes with application execution for limited resources. We consider the joint problem of deciding which updates to commit, how much Byzantine redundancy to provision, and where to execute Metaverse application tasks. We formulate this problem under resource, latency, and practical Byzantine fault tolerance (PBFT) constraints, accounting for update persistence and semantic significance. We develop a hierarchical redundancy-aware PBFT (R-PBFT) approach that decomposes the decision process into slot-wise computation offloading and sequential update-admission and redundancy control. We formulate the former as an identical-interest game and address the latter using a feasibility-filtered double deep Q-network (DDQN). Analytical downlink allocation accounts for the energy and delivery-feasibility effects of consensus latency. For fixed slot state and redundancy, we establish the game's exact-potential property and finite termination of strict sequential improvements.
