Pathology foundation models are usually compared by their performance on downstream tasks such as classification and retrieval. While such benchmarks measure how useful a model is, they provide no insight into the biology it represents. We introduce BioScore, a benchmark that quantifies how well an image encoder captures cell identities, with spatial transcriptomics of the same tissue as the reference. A sparse autoencoder splits the encoder's patch embeddings into sparse features, which we cluster into a fixed number of groups. BioScore then measures, for each of 52 cell identities defined by marker genes, how specifically one group follows the identity's expression over the tissue. Because the autoencoder training is fully unsupervised, a match shows that the model represents the cell identity from the image alone. We score 8 pathology foundation models and 3 natural-image models. Every pathology model scores above every natural-image model (0.299--0.371 \vs 0.214--0.232), and paired tests separate three tiers among the pathology models.
