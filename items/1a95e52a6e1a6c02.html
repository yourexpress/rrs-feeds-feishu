Personalized public-health targeting must capture heterogeneous intervention benefits while remaining interpretable under resource constraints. Flexible causal-effect models can be hard to operationalize, while coarse subgroup policies may lose individualized effect information. We study budget-constrained hypothetical state interventions in observational data without a logged historical decision policy. For obesity, elevated glucose, and lifetime smoking-history states, causal-structure-informed pretreatment covariates are prespecified, with capacity capped at 70% of those observed in the adverse state. We evaluate causal distillation where Causal Forest and Bayesian Causal Forest teachers estimate conditional average treatment effects (CATEs), which decision tree and random forest students approximate. We compare individualized policies with unsupervised K-means subgroup policies and supervised causal-tree policies with leaf counts matched to K-means cluster counts. Observational policies are assessed on held-out data using a doubly robust estimator, bootstrap uncertainty, and decision-focused teacher-student fidelity measures.
