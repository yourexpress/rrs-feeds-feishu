Superscalar Loop Processors

arXiv (cs.AR) · 2026-11-01 · 论文PDF

To sustain the computational demands of modern ML and HPC workloads, programmable accelerators integrate large arrays of processing elements (PEs), typically built around single-issue in-order cores to maximize integration density and energy efficiency. As a result, each PE's instruction throughput is upper-bounded at one instruction per cycle (IPC), often suffering from significant underutilization of its functional units (FUs). Conventional superscalar processors can improve FU utilization by increasing IPC, but they typically incur substantial area and energy overheads. This work proposes the Superscalar Loop Processor (SLP) architecture, a lightweight approach to superscalar execution that targets regular, compute-intensive loop kernels. We present the SLP execution model, which exploits the static structure of these workloads to enable efficient multiple-issue execution without the complexity of dynamic out-of-order scheduling. We demonstrate the SLP architecture through an open-source prototype implementation, Schnizo, developed as an enhancement of a tiny RV32I core with floating-point support.
