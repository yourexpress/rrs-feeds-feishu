Artificial intelligence faces two growing challenges: computational bottlenecks and energy inefficiency inherent to the von Neumann architecture. Neuromorphic computing offers a promising alternative, yet its immature ecosystem still relies on conventional systems for data supply, requiring efficient conversion between tensor and spike dataflow. We observe a clear mismatch, where data conversion is slow while downstream computation is fast. As data size grows quadratically with large language models, this mismatch becomes a major bottleneck. In this work, we examine the data-conversion components that appear on the critical path of training and inference in neuromorphic LLMs. The work presents FlashT2S (Flash Tensor-to-Spike): GPU-Accelerated Tensor-to-Spike Conversion Operators, which applies architecture-aware and data-centric optimizations to reduce conversion latency. Experimental results show that FlashT2S delivers an average speedup of 850.78x over the native operators across individual operators and 802.83x in realistic workloads.
