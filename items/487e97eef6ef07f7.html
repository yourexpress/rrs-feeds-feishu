Published implementations of the Poincaré convolution in Hyperbolic Neural Networks++ (HNN++) map every overlapping image patch to the Poincaré ball before evaluating its score. We show that the clipped score depends on each patch only through two statistics, its squared norm and its inner products with the filters, which a native convolution and a single-channel box filter compute directly. The factorized layer therefore evaluates the specified score exactly, without constructing ball-valued patches, and its single-precision outputs and gradients agree with double-precision evaluations at least as closely as those of the explicit reference. On an RTX A6000 under two versions of JAX, complete training updates of a Poincaré U-Net and a Poincaré ResNet-20 are 1.5 to 1.8 times faster than the faster of two explicit implementations that map patches to the ball (1.2 to 1.4 times against an explicit implementation that skips this mapping); on a V100, the gain over the gather-based one persists as the ResNet-20 is widened eightfold.
