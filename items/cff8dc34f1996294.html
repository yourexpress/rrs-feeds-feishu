Recent advances in artificial intelligence (AI) are reshaping smart glasses from egocentric capture and display devices into platforms for wearable intelligence. Smart glasses increasingly serve as wearable AI systems that connect first-person observation with real-time assistance under strict form-factor constraints. We frame this transition through the lens of \emph{AI smart glasses} and define them as a system-level concept in which egocentric sensing, resource-aware computing, intelligent reasoning, multimodal interaction, and real-world application constraints are co-designed for personalized assistance in the physical world. To systematically study this perspective, we organize the survey around four connected dimensions. First, we examine the hardware foundation that bounds sensing, computation, feedback delivery, and sustained deployment. Second, we study wearable intelligence, where egocentric signals are transformed into perceptual, contextual, and agentic capabilities. Third, we discuss interaction design, through which users request, receive, correct, and regulate assistance during ongoing activity.
