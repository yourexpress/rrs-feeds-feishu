Mitigating SM-Level Load Imbalance in 3D Gaussian Splatting on GPUs

arXiv cs.DC · 2026-11-09 · 论文PDF

3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) enables photorealistic, real-time rendering on commodity GPUs and is increasingly adopted in generative world models and autonomous driving simulators. However, existing optimizations rely on algorithmic changes that often require retraining, or on specialized accelerators that lack flexibility across evolving 3DGS variants. In this paper, we identify a fundamental inefficiency in GPU-based 3DGS rendering: severe SM-level load imbalance caused by uneven workloads across screen tiles. This imbalance creates a long execution tail, leaving most SMs idle while a few process computationally intensive tiles, particularly in large-scale urban scenes. To address this, we present a software-defined GPU scheduling approach that mitigates the execution tail. Our approach uses a persistent kernel to schedule heavy tiles first and selectively chunks outlier tiles across multiple CTAs. A lightweight per-frame policy determines when and how much to chunk using tile statistics already available in the rendering pipeline.
