We completely characterize the solvability profiles of tasks for two autonomous mobile robots in the plane under variable disorientation, chirality, and rigid movements. The four standard capability models and the schedulers considered here have a known quotient order Q of thirteen computational classes. A task's solvability profile is the set of classes in which it is solvable, and is necessarily an upset of Q. We prove that every upset is realizable and construct a minimum exact disjunctive basis of thirteen concrete tasks. The basis contains one task for each principal upset. For every antichain of Q, the disjunction of the corresponding tasks has profile exactly equal to the union of their profiles. This exactness is not implied by the individual profiles: an algorithm may satisfy different disjuncts in different executions. We establish it by constructing, for every algorithm in each maximal class outside the target union, a single admissible fair execution violating all selected predicates simultaneously.
