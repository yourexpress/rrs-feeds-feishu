Clustering-Based Artificial Tree Ensembles for Random Forest Approximation

arXiv cs.LG · 2026-11-13 · 论文PDF

聚类人工树集成用于随机森林近似

Random Forests (RFs) offer strong prediction performance but are difficult to interpret. Most representative trees (MRTs) and artificial representative trees (ARTs) provide surrogate explanations, but a single tree may miss patterns within a forest. We investigate benefits and limitations of clustering-based representative tree ensembles for RF surrogate modeling and assess the contribution of similarity-based clustering relative to random tree partitions. Using linear and nonlinear simulations and regression benchmarks, we evaluate fidelity, prediction accuracy, complexity, and prediction stability against equally sized ensembles of top-ranked MRTs and randomly selected trees. Increasing ensemble size generally improves fidelity, accuracy, and prediction stability. ART ensembles show higher fidelity in simulations and often greater prediction stability for small ensembles. Compared with random tree partitions, similarity-based clustering generally improves ART fidelity in simulations and often on benchmarks.
