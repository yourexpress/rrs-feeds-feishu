Acoustic Anomaly Detection for Early Identification of Drill Jamming

arXiv · 2026-11-06 · 论文PDF

In the context of Industry 4.0, predictive maintenance is essential to minimize unplanned downtime and ensure operational safety. In machining processes, drill jamming is a critical failure that compromises both workpiece quality and tool integrity. This work proposes an acoustic anomaly detection system using ultrasonic sensors to identify pre-failure patterns in Computer Numerical Control (CNC) machining. We release a public dataset of time- and frequency-domain acoustic features from 17 drills, and benchmark semi-supervised autoencoder-based normality modelling against supervised XGBoost classification using Leave-One-Drill-Out validation. The results demonstrate that the Autoencoder is highly effective for industrial safety, achieving a Recall of 1.00 and a consolidated F1-score of 0.81. Furthermore, the threshold-independent analysis shows that the Autoencoder better separates normal and pre-failure acoustic patterns, achieving a consolidated AUC of 0.8294, compared with 0.7559 for the supervised model under inter-tool covariate shift.
