Tokenized Reward Systems for Federated Learning: The Privacy vs. Fairness Tradeoff

arXiv cs.LG · 2026-11-11 · 论文PDF

Federated learning (FL) systems increasingly rely on blockchain based reward mechanisms to incentivize participation while preserving data privacy. We design and empirically evaluate two complementary Solidity smart contracts for FL reward distribution: the first one that allocates rewards proportionally to a publicly disclosed quality score, and a privacy preserving variant that hides individual quality scores behind a zero-knowledge (ZK) proof of a threshold condition. Both contracts mint a non transferable, use-and-burn token maintaining a trustless incentive structure. We evaluate the two designs through MNIST training with adversarial behavior, including Sybil attacks, zero gradient and random noise, across three independent random seeds. Our results reveal a fundamental tradeoff between cryptographic privacy and incentive fairness. The privacy preserving architecture strictly degrades system robustness, doubling the adversary's average reward capture while introducing severe outcome variance.
