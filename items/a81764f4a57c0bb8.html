Test-time scaling can improve large language model reasoning by generating and combining multiple candidate responses. In sampling-based methods, the inference budget is often described by the number of generated candidates, N. However, N tells us how many candidates are generated, not how they are executed. The same candidate budget can be produced in one batched generation call or split across several sequential calls with smaller batch sizes. We first study the effect of increasing N on reasoning accuracy using Phi-3-mini and Qwen2.5-1.5B on 500 GSM8K prompts. As expected, increasing N from 1 to 8 improves accuracy by 8.4 percentage points for Phi-3-mini and 18.4 points for Qwen2.5-1.5B. However, accuracy alone does not show the systems cost of using a larger candidate budget. We therefore fix N = 8 and compare four generation schedules: 1x8, 2x4, 4x2, and 8x1, where axb denotes a generation calls with b candidates per call. We measure latency, throughput, GPU-hours, and gross GPU-device energy while keeping the total candidate count fixed.
