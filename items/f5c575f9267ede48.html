Last-mile functional coverage closure for modern chip designs remains costly because a mature UVM environment exposes many controls while direct hits on the remaining bins are sparse. We present a non-intrusive workflow that closes pre-defined bins in an existing, human-authored functional coverage model; it does not synthesize verification objectives from the design specification. The workflow first uses abstract-syntax-tree (AST) parsing and cone-of-influence (COI) tracing to map each residual target to existing testbench knobs. Under a matched 20-simulation budget, Bayesian optimization (BO) is compared with continued constrained-random verification (CRV) and random search over the same AST/COI-reduced space. Formal reachability then removes only targets supported by complete unreachability proofs, after which retained targets are grouped by formal difficulty and processed by group-specific BO under a second matched 20-simulation budget. Cleanup and grouping never add numerator bins; only simulator coverage artifacts do.
