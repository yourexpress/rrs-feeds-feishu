MuonGS: Pretraining LLMs with Relaxed Weight-Norm Control

Weight decay and explicit weight-norm constraints improve optimizer performance in language model pretraining, yet a unified understanding of why such control helps and how much is needed remains incomplete. We analyze norm control in Muon over the entire training trajectory using the \emph{accumulated effective learning rate}, $C_T=\sum_{t<T}η_t/\|W_t\|_F$, where $η_t$ is the nominal learning rate and $η_t/\|W_t\|_F$ is the effective learning rate relative to the weight norm. For bounded update directions, accumulation over any training interval bounds relative weight displacement. We show that vanilla Muon can accumulate only logarithmically, potentially limiting exploration and helping explain its slower empirical progress, whereas MuonW and MuonH guarantee at least linear accumulation under the stated conditions. Going beyond bounded-norm control, we show that sufficiently slow norm growth guarantees faster accumulation than the logarithmic worst case, opening up less restrictive optimizer designs.
