While universal approximation theorems theoretically underpin the representation capabilities of machine learning models, they have two shortcomings: They do not focus on efficient representations and they tend to be insensitive to depth and other heuristically important properties. In this work we show that these two shortcomings are intertwined. We prove that machine learning models which are deeper or have higher-order interactions can efficiently represent a larger set of functions, i.e. have larger efficient reach. It follows that deep Boltzmann machines (DBMs) have maximal efficient reach, restricted Boltzmann machines (RBMs) sub-maximal, and k-body generalizations of RBMs form a hierarchy between the two, even though all are universal. More generally, (i) we provide a full characterization of machine learning models with maximal efficient reach; (ii) we derive separations between deep and shallow models for three representation tasks; and (iii) we prove that the efficient reach of shallow models constitutes an infinite hierarchy indexed by the interaction order.
