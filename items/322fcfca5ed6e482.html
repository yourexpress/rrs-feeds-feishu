Dissecting the GPU Load/Store Unit and Exposing Its Security Risks

arXiv cs.AR · 2026-11-17 · 论文PDF

剖析 GPU 载入/存储单元并暴露安全风险

GPUs are the main platform for large-scale machine learning and power the largest supercomputers. Their load/store units (LSUs) often determine kernel performance and have been exploited in prior attacks. Yet what is known about the LSU is only a few isolated mechanisms and parameters, and even a single one has been enough for an attack. In particular, it is still unknown what a load costs, where loads contend, and even whether a warp can starve due to contention. Until each is measured, neither its security risk nor a defense is known. We reverse-engineer how the LSU serves load instructions on recent NVIDIA GPUs with microbenchmarks and analyze the security implications. We give a cycle-accurate latency formula for a global L1 hit or shared memory access from the addresses it touches, whose address-dependent terms are bank conflicts and the L1 tag lookup; we show that loads contend at both, and that when the LSU pipeline is full a warp can starve a warp of a later-launched block.
