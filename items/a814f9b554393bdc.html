Quantum graph neural networks (QGNNs) apply parametrized quantum circuits to graph-structured data, with the aim of overcoming limits of classical graph neural networks in scalability, expressivity, and long-range information flow. Since the first Hamiltonian-based model in 2019, the field has produced many architectures, applications, and claims, but the evidence behind them has not been assessed systematically. This survey introduces a unified formalism that describes every QGNN as an encoder, a graph-conditioned circuit, observables, and an optional classical head, together with a circuit-level condition for permutation equivariance and a resource model for comparing costs. We organize the literature into five architecture families (convolutional, equivariant, message-passing and recurrent, Hamiltonian, and hybrid) and compare them by qubit cost, symmetry, scalability, and evidence. We then review symmetry and geometric inductive biases (permutation, rotation, and Lorentz), the results on expressivity, trainability, noise, and scalability, and applications in high-energy physics, chemistry, finance, healthcare, earth observation, and transportation.
