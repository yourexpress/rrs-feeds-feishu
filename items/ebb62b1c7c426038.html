Scalable Placement for Disaggregated LLM Serving on Heterogeneous Edge Fleets

Serving large language models (LLMs) at the edge is increasingly desirable for privacy-sensitive applications and scenarios that require data to remain on local devices. However, edge environments are constrained by limited computational resources and exhibit substantial device heterogeneity, making efficient LLM serving challenging. These limitations become particularly pronounced under bursty demand, where congestion and queue buildup can significantly increase response latency. Replicating the model into multiple pipeline instances can improve serving capacity and reduce congestion. Yet, deciding how devices should be grouped into replicas, assigning replicas to prefill or decode functions, and partitioning model layers across heterogeneous devices jointly creates a large combinatorial optimization problem. We present Spade, a scalable and runtime-efficient placement planner for disaggregated LLM serving with pipeline replication across heterogeneous edge clusters. Spade introduces a dominance-based pruning technique that discards inferior replica candidates and recursively eliminates larger candidates that contain them, dramatically shrinking the search space.
