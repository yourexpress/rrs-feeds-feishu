Translation Validation of SystemVerilog-to-SystemC RTL Conversion by Reading the Generated C++

arXiv · 2026-11-23 · 论文PDF

SystemC models of register-transfer level (RTL) designs are used for virtual platforms, firmware bring-up and simulation without a commercial license, and more of them now come from translators and language models. A wrong model usually compiles, and it usually agrees with the RTL. SystemVerilog evaluates an 8-bit sum in 8 bits while C++ evaluates it in 64, so a translation that forgets one truncation keeps a carry the RTL drops, and only inputs near the top of the range show it. I built svcc, an open-source SystemVerilog-to-SystemC converter, and my first check of its output read the generated C++ with SystemVerilog's rules. That check passed two wrong models. The check that replaced it gives the generated header the meaning C++17 and SystemC give it and turns undefined behavior into proof obligations. It prints what it read as SystemVerilog with every width change explicit, then proves that equivalent to a netlist of the source by induction over registers matched by name. On 50 author-written designs it proves 45 and reports 5 as outside its model. In a mutation experiment it accepted none of the 17 mutants that compiled SystemC showed to be wrong.
