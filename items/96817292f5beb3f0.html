LaBraM at Scale: Scaling Data and Models for EEG Pretraining

arXiv (cs.LG) · 2026-11-02 · 论文PDF

LaBraM, an early EEG foundation model, demonstrated that large-scale EEG pretraining can yield strong representations for diverse downstream tasks. Building on this foundation, we expand pretraining data and model capacity while retaining its core encoder and objective. We train ten encoders with 5.8 million to 1.97 billion parameters on three EEG collections spanning 25,067 to 79,910 hours, adapting signal preparation and training to heterogeneous recordings. Across 23 tasks, Atom-L* reaches 59.05% mean balanced accuracy at its best evaluated full fine-tuning (Full-FT) checkpoint, 3.61 percentage points above the strongest baseline EEG foundation model. Quark-G leads linear probing (LP) at 54.33%, a 1.24-point margin. Both also lead average task rank. At the last evaluated checkpoints, larger encoders improve Full-FT more consistently than LP, and stronger LP does not guarantee stronger Full-FT. Preserving patch structure improves LP over average pooling. Data-fraction experiments suggest capacity-dependent saturation, while checkpoint trajectories show that downstream performance can decline as pretraining loss falls.
