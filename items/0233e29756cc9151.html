Physical systems and distributed AI workflows can retain past information, receive delayed feedback and combine conflicting signals. These features pose a common modeling problem: determining what information about past interactions a state must retain to account for future responses. Active Neural Matter (ANM) addresses this problem through a finite-window response framework. Forward analysis uses each system's dynamics to predict state and output responses to perturbations, separating propagation from output sensitivity. Inverse analysis tests whether a candidate state retains enough information for these responses. With the system and future inputs or interaction rules fixed, distinguishable responses from histories assigned the same state identify distinctions the representation must preserve. Their distributional separation gives a lower bound on the worst-case error of approximating behavior from that state. Uncertainty bounds distinguish rejection, support within a fixed tolerance on tested comparisons, and unresolved cases.
