Mind the Delta: Early Pruning of RTL Variants for Agentic Hardware Optimization

Autonomous coding agents powered by large language models (LLMs) now write diverse microarchitectural variants of existing register-transfer level (RTL) designs at high throughput, shifting the primary bottleneck of agentic RTL optimization onto logic synthesis. Commercial toolchains take hours per variant, creating an acute compute asymmetry between proposing and evaluating variants. Existing fast evaluators falter in this regime. Subtle RTL edits trigger nonlinear synthesis shifts that learned predictors and coding agents cannot resolve, while synthesis proxies at reduced effort either alter the optimization trajectory or retain most of its runtime. Selecting a variant, however, does not require regressing absolute quality of results across designs, only identifying when a candidate cannot surpass the best completed feasible implementation. Based on the observation that synthesis trajectories share strong design-specific bias with their base design, we propose DiffScope, a training-free anytime selection framework that races full synthesis runs in parallel.
