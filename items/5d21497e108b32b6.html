Sparse-TD-JEPA: Improving Zero-Shot Reinforcement Learning With Sparse Latent Task Representations

Successor-feature-based zero-shot reinforcement learning methods pre-train a task encoder and successor-feature predictor on offline, reward-free data, then recover a policy at test time by solving a closed-form linear regression over a small labeled dataset. We identify an underexplored limitation of this approach: when the task encoder produces dense representations, the inferred reward signal becomes diffuse across the state space, reducing the contrast between goal-relevant states and the background and providing a weaker signal for the policy to follow. We address this limitation by introducing an l1 sparsity penalty that concentrates reward-predictive structure into a smaller effective subspace of the task encoder's latent space. The penalty is applied only during training and requires no architectural change or modification to the closed-form inference procedure. We instantiate this approach as Sparse-TD-JEPA, a single-line of code addition based on Temporal-Difference Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (TD-JEPA) that significantly improved its performance in zero-shot reinforcement learning.
