Neural architecture search (NAS) on a new dataset often affords only tens of short training runs, so what the search knows before its first evaluation decides its efficiency. Yet most NAS methods start from scratch, guided at best by zero-cost proxies that are unreliable across tasks. QuaNAS treats a library of earlier searches in the same search space as explicit knowledge of uncertain reliability. Main effects of design decisions learned from this library and, secondarily, zero-cost proxies become priors over candidate architectures without any label of the new dataset. A Gaussian process starts at the prior optimum and re-estimates after every evaluation how far each prior is trusted. Under a protocol frozen before any confirmation data were seen, QuaNAS was evaluated once on 12 new image, time-series, text and tabular datasets. It lowered anytime regret on the short-training objective 2.7- to 3.9-fold relative to five pre-specified NAS baselines that start from scratch (Holm-adjusted p = 0.0012 each).
