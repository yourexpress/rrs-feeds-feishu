Contrastive Proxy Forests: Completeness-Preserving Proxy Learning from Multimodal Records

arXiv · 2026-11-24 · 论文PDF

Proximal causal inference removes unmeasured confounding using two proxies of the confounder, an outcome-side W and a treatment-side Z. The proxies must satisfy exclusion restrictions and be complete, meaning that they reflect every aspect of the confounder. In practice, they are a few variables hand-picked from a structured table, even though clinical notes and physiological waveforms carry rich information about the confounder. Such records are too high-dimensional for a proximal estimator, and generic compression such as PCA may keep modality-specific variation (style) instead of the confounder, destroying completeness. Our key observation is that, under the proximal condition, the confounder is the only link between a treatment-side and an outcome-side modality beyond the covariates. Learning what they share therefore captures the confounder and filters out modality-specific style. Contrastive Proxy Forests (CPF) learns both proxies by contrasting the two modalities, given the covariates, on a separate unlabeled sample, and passes them to a proximal causal forest.
