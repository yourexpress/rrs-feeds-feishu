A spatial excursion set identifies where a field exceeds a threshold. With incomplete and noisy observations, inner and outer confidence regions quantify uncertainty in its spatial extent through lower and upper bounds at a specified confidence level. Constructing these regions requires repeated conditional simulations accounting for joint spatial uncertainty, making dense covariance factorization and simulation major computational bottlenecks. We present a GPU-accelerated framework that combines adaptive tile-based mixed-precision arithmetic with distributed task execution to accelerate universal kriging, prediction-uncertainty estimation, conditional Gaussian simulation, and confidence region calibration. In our parallel mixed-precision simulation workflow, triangular matrix-matrix multiplication (TRMM) inherits the adaptive precision layout of the Cholesky factor and uses FP16 simulation operands, while covariance scaling and compensating operand rescaling accommodate the numerical range of reduced precision arithmetic.
