Does Country Matter? Evaluating Geographic Sensitivity of Large Language Models in Zero-Shot Chronic Kidney Disease Screening

arXiv cs.LG · 2026-09-16 · 论文PDF

Large language models (LLMs) show strong zero-shot reasoning capabilities, yet their sensitivity to prompt context raises questions about reliability in deployment. This study investigates whether geographic context embedded in prompts influences LLM-based chronic kidney disease (CKD) screening, independent of any change in patient features. Using a community-based CKD dataset from Bangladesh (284 participants with 112 CKD and 172 non-CKD), we systematically varied the country attribute across 25 global contexts while holding all clinical inputs constant, and evaluated two open-weight LLMs (Qwen3 8B and Mistral 7B). Experiments were conducted on a common-correct subset of 156 participants for whom both models produced correct baseline predictions. Results show that non-CKD predictions were highly stable across geographic contexts, with both models maintaining consistent non-CKD predictions in nearly all settings. In contrast, CKD-positive predictions were considerably more sensitive. For Qwen3, CKD prediction dropped from a baseline of 65 to as low as 50 across country contexts, while Mistral showed smaller but non-negligible variation.
