Energy-efficient virtual machine (VM) consolidation matters as data centre electricity demand rises. Bio-inspired metaheuristics, including ant colony optimisation (ACO), particle swarm optimisation (PSO), and genetic algorithms, have reported substantial energy savings. However, these improvements are commonly evaluated against conventional allocation policies that differ in both initial VM placement and subsequent migration optimisation, making it difficult to determine the contribution of metaheuristic search. This study reproduces an existing consolidation implementation in CloudSim Plus and introduces a matched control that retains the same initial packing and empty-host deactivation mechanisms but excludes migration search. ACO, PSO, and a modified genetic algorithm (MGA) are evaluated across two fleet configurations and four workload regimes, with ten stochastic replications per condition. The matched control achieves energy savings of 0.8-81.4% relative to the spreading baseline, the amount available falling sharply as load rises, without migrations or incomplete workload execution.
