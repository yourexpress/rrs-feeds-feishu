What Conformal Selection Can Certify in CRISPR Off-Target Design:Benchmark Leakage, Free Baselines, and a Finite-Sample Cohort Requirement

arXiv cs.LG · 2026-11-11 · 论文PDF

Conformal selection promises distribution-free, finite-sample control of the error rate of a decision, and has been proposed as a safety layer for machine-learning CRISPR guide design. We ask whether such a certificate is attainable from the validated off-target data that exist. The constraint is arithmetic: a conformal p-value cannot fall below one divided by the calibration cohort size plus one, so a Benjamini-Hochberg selection certifies a single candidate only when that cohort is at least the candidate pool size divided by the tolerated error rate. Public cohorts supply ten validated guides, whereas a pool of two thousand at a ten percent error rate needs twenty thousand; the accepted set is consequently empty in over ninety-six percent of splits. Two benchmarking findings explain why this went unnoticed. The standard CIRCLE-seq file lists ten guides under eighty on-target strings, so grouping cross-validation on the sequence column trains and tests on one guide; corrected, pair-level discrimination and per-guide fidelity collapse.
