Fairness constraints in predict-then-optimize pipelines are necessarily enforced on predicted outcomes $\hat{y}$, since true outcomes $y$ are unobserved at decision time. We study whether a demographic parity constraint enforced this way continues to hold against $y$, and how the resulting violation scales with prediction error. Using a budget-constrained allocation problem solved as a linear program, we show empirically that the true-outcome fairness gap grows with prediction error $σ$ and saturates, well fit by $c(1 - e^{-σ/τ})$ with $c = 0.1545 \pm 0.0103$ and $τ= 3.9045 \pm 0.3745$; its mean is invariant to sample size while its variance decays at the standard $O(1/\sqrt{n})$ rate. We derive a two-part theoretical bound: a closed-form local sensitivity result showing the gap's first-order response to prediction error depends on only two individuals per group, validated against the solver to within three significant figures; and a universal, error-independent ceiling following from the boundedness of the decision variable, confirmed consistent with but looser than the empirical saturation level.
