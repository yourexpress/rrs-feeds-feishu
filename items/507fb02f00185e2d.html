Decomposing Earth Embeddings with Sparse Autoencoders

arXiv cs.LG · 2026-11-08 · 论文PDF

Earth observation foundation models produce global metre-resolution embeddings that underpin work across agriculture, ecology, hydrology, and beyond. Yet these embeddings are opaque: each dimension entangles unrelated physical concepts, which makes them difficult to interpret and their robustness hard to assess. Here, we propose sparse autoencoders (SAEs) as a principled way to decompose Earth embeddings into sparse, approximately monosemantic features over an overcomplete dictionary. Because LLM judges of interpretations do not transfer to satellite pixels, and spatial autocorrelation matters, we introduce a tailored evaluation protocol. It scores any SAE on reconstruction at matched sparsity, alignment with reference concepts, and spatial scale, each against baselines that include an SAE whose decoder is a frozen random dictionary. On this protocol, SAEs reconstruct AlphaEarth and Tessera embeddings better than every baseline at matched sparsity. Their features align with far fewer reference concepts than raw embedding dimensions (on AlphaEarth, 88% align with at most one of 20 concept groups, most with none, against a median of 12 per raw dimension).
