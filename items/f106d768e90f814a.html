Finite-Memory Control of POMDPs: Fundamental Limits and Efficient Design

arXiv (cs.LG) · 2026-11-02 · 论文PDF

Optimal control of partially observable Markov decision processes (POMDPs), in general, requires a complete history. The belief state, a distribution over hidden states, summarizes this history and provides the classical sufficient statistic to control POMDPs. However, the continuous nature of the belief space generally prevents a finite-memory controller from maintaining it exactly. This raises the question we address in this work: what must the available finite memory preserve to achieve near-optimal control performance? We study this question through an information-theoretic converse that gives a fundamental lower bound on the loss of any finite-memory controller. The analysis introduces the concept of a decision witness that characterizes belief states whose confusion would lead to unavoidable loss. Motivated by this bound, we propose RECAP, a constructive quantization method for designing representative memory states. We prove an upper bound on the optimality gap of any RECAP codebook and use the components of this bound together with the converse analysis to define the codebook objective.
