Recursive Sparse Attention: Million-Token Length Extrapolation at Logarithmic Cost

arXiv · 2026-11-06 · 论文PDF

At context lengths of about 1M tokens, dense attention is costly in prefill and decoding, and training typically relies on long documents, which become scarcer as the target length increases. Most sparse attention methods score all preceding blocks or tokens, so their selection cost per query is $O(L)$ at context length $L$; hierarchical selection reduces it to $O(\log L)$, but prior trainable hierarchical methods report accuracy only within their training length. We propose RecSA (Recursive Sparse Attention), which builds a tree over the KV cache in which each node summarizes its subtree with one key and one mean-pooled value. The keys come from one pooler per layer, shared by all levels and distilled from dense attention. Each level above the tokens selects at most $k$ candidates, so each query has $O(\log L)$ selection cost and reads $O(\log L)$ entries; the summaries of unselected candidates share one softmax with the exact tokens. Each entry's RoPE position is its index among the candidates of its level, so at any length every relative distance stays within the range seen during training.
