Loop-Count Schedules in Looped Transformers: An Empirical Study

arXiv · 2026-11-22 · 论文PDF

Looped transformers apply one block of layers several times with shared weights, trading parameters for repeated computation. We study how the schedule of the loop iteration count R during training determines whether each extra pass acts as a full layer or as a small correction, using a controlled ladder of 46M-110M-parameter models trained on 2B tokens with learned residual gates on every loop pass and three schedules: R fixed to 2, R sampled uniformly from {1,...,6}, and R sampled from {2,3,4}. We find that the lower bound of the sampling range is the decisive choice. If the sampled range includes R=1, so that some training steps run the block only once and compute the loss on that single pass, the gates collapse to 0.1-0.5 and each additional pass becomes a small correction to the first; if the range starts at R=2, so that every training step runs the block at least twice, the gates stay at 0.6-0.9 and each pass keeps the strength of a full layer, while the model can still be run at any R from 2 up to twice the largest trained value with validation loss within a few hundredths of a nat of its best.
