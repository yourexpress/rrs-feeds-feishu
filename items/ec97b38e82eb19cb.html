Blockchain consensus protocols implicitly follow a client-server architecture: validators are the only active participants, while clients (i.e., users) merely submit transactions and rely on validators to include them. This abstraction obscures a fundamental question: what should a client do to guarantee the timely inclusion of its own transaction? We study this question by introducing a leaderless consensus protocol in which clients are active. We define inclusion safety: the guarantee that a client's transaction enters some of the blocks produced for an epoch, and that every block omitting it belongs to a fork that is eventually abandoned. Because every transaction carries a reference to a block of the previous epoch, clients help select the surviving fork. This yields two paths to inclusion: reaching sufficiently many honest replicas within an epoch, or relaying a block containing the transaction across epochs until the corresponding fork becomes canonical.
