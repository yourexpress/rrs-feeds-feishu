Amine-impregnated sorbents with the highest nominal CO2 capacity are frequently the least deployable, as amine beyond the pore-volume limit blocks active sites. Yet multi-objective Bayesian optimization (MOBO) for direct air capture (DAC) treats formulation search as unconstrained. We encode the pore-filling boundary as an explicit outcome constraint, c(x) = tanh(1 - phi_hat) - tau, and use it as a diagnostic instrument on a hand-curated, DOI-traceable benchmark of 618 solid-amine formulations from 43 publications. Unconstrained MOBO attains higher nominal capacity (3.639 +/- 0.815 vs. 2.964 +/- 0.728 mmol/g, p = 1.0e-11), but across 100 seeds 63.2% of its Pareto-optimal candidates violate the pore-filling boundary versus 39.3% under the constraint: it is rewarded for proposing candidates outside the pore-confined design regime. The constraint steers the front to lower loadings (55.8 vs. 65.2 wt%) and larger pores. The boundary is corroborated once apparatus effects are harmonized hierarchically: 77.8% of kinetic variance is between-laboratory, and pore filling carries a significant kinetic penalty (p = 0.002).
