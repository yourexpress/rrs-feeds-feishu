Merit: Making every modality earn its contribution in multimodal attention decoding

More than 1.5 billion people have hearing loss. When several people talk at once, hearing aids amplify every voice alike. Auditory attention decoding (AAD) aims to solve this by reading a listener's brain signals to find the attended talker, letting the device amplify only them. A real device will not see the brain alone; smart glasses and earbuds also track eyes, head, and scene. These behavioral signals help, as people often look at who they listen to. However, they show where a listener looks, not whom they hear. A decoder relying heavily on them fails when a listener cannot move their head or listens without looking - exactly when a brain-based device is needed. A multimodal decoder must use these signals without letting them replace the electroencephalogram (EEG). Accuracy alone cannot show whether it does. We present MERIT, a decoder and evaluation method asking: how much accuracy does each input earn? MERIT shuffles each input stream across test examples, measures the accuracy drop, and splits the result across streams using Shapley values.
