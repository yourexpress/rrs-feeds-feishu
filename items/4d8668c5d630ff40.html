We introduce JAXbounce, a continuous control pong-like benchmark designed to evaluate active inference beyond discrete, low dimensional environments. JAXbounce combines continuous states and stochastic dynamics in discrete time with flexible and composable observation models that allow representing the environment as a state vector or a pixel grid, corruptible through the rich family of noise models we provide. It is fully differentiable and parallelisable in JAX. We run over 10,000 agent simulations on our environment comparing a baseline deterministic solution, to a state-based active inference agent with Unscented Kalman Filter perception and expected free energy planning, and a pixel based agent whose measurement model is a pretrained neural network encoder. All agents are evaluated across three scenarios of increasing difficulty with eight different noise conditions. Our experiments characterise how performance of continuous state agents scales with planning, planning horizons, observation modalities and knowledge of the noise process, exhibiting preference-oriented behaviour even in the presence of increasing uncertainty.
