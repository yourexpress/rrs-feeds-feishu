Pixel Auto-Encoder: Reconstructing Language Model Activations Through Pixel Art

arXiv · 2026-11-21 · 论文PDF

Interpreting the high-dimensional internal representations of large language models (LLMs) remains a challenging problem. Recent approaches have explored translating these representations into human-understandable forms, particularly natural language. In this work, we explore the use of images as an alternative target modality for interpreting LLM activations. As a first step toward this goal, we introduce Pixel Auto-Encoder, which converts intermediate LLM activations into $32 \times 32$, 32-color pixel art and reconstructs them for subsequent computation within the model. By encouraging the intermediate representations to resemble predefined concept images, we investigate whether LLM activations can be expressed visually while preserving the model's language capabilities. We find that the model can produce target-aligned images for some concepts, and that replacing pixel representations with concept images can influence next-token predictions. However, we also observe a trade-off between visual alignment and language-model performance, as well as sensitivity to pixel-level changes that are difficult to interpret visually.
