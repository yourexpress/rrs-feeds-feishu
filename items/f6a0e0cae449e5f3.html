Echo State Networks (ESNs), as lightweight implementations of reservoir computing, provide efficient solutions for temporal data processing in resource-constrained environments. While standard ESNs offer computational efficiency, their dense readout layers inflate the stored model and the per-sample inference cost. This paper introduces a Smooth-L1 regularized ESN (SL1ESN) that promotes sparsity in the readout layer while preserving differentiability for gradient-based optimization. The method employs a twice-differentiable Smooth-L1 approximation compatible with Newton's method, which we exploit both for centralized training and for a second-order (Newton-step) federated aggregation scheme.
