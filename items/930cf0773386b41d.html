Infrastructure for Verifiable AI: Where Proof Costs Go and How Hardware Can Help

arXiv cs.AR · 2026-11-12 · 论文PDF

Users of artificial intelligence services, including those powered by large language models and other transformer-based models, need assurance that inference and training follow the declared computation without exposing private models or data. Zero-knowledge proofs can provide this assurance, but proof generation remains expensive. Hardware acceleration research often reports gains for individual cryptographic operations, leaving their effect on the full cost of proving neural-network workloads, particularly large transformer models, unclear. We introduce a four-layer framework linking verifiable AI workloads, proof systems, cryptographic kernels and hardware platforms, use it to review the literature, and add original measurements of six provers and seven kernels to identify which costs matter in practice. Our measurements show that the dominant cost changes with the proof system and input length. In low-rank fine-tuning, the cost of opening commitments to fixed model weights remains nearly constant across input lengths from 64 to 2048, while the work associated with intermediate values grows.
