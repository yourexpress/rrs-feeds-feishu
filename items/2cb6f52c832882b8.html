Diffusion models evolve noisy states toward a learned data distribution, so their sampling behavior depends on the geometry of the representation in which denoising occurs. This dependence is especially important for multimodal data: independently trained encoders can map synchronized views to incompatible latent regions, while uniform alignment can let an uncertain view distort the shared geometry. To address this gap, we introduce a novel reliability-guided alignment rule that uses normalized VAE posterior variance to control directional cross-modal influence before diffusion training. We study this issue in minority-attack generation for imbalanced Internet-of-Things intrusion detection. Tabular flow attributes, a DeepInsight image, and a packet sequence are encoded by modality-specific variational autoencoders. Per-modality normalized VAE posterior variances provide bounded, sample-specific weighting signals in an asymmetric stop-gradient alignment objective. We use these quantities as heuristic encoder-derived reliability proxies rather than calibrated probabilistic uncertainties.
