Implicit Regularization via Spectral Neural Networks and Non-linear Matrix Sensing

arXiv · 2026-11-06 · 论文PDF

The phenomenon of implicit bias has attracted interest in recent years as a fundamental aspect of the remarkable generalizing ability of neural networks. In a nutshell, it entails that gradient descent dynamics in many neural nets, even without any explicit regularizer in the loss function, converges to the solution of a regularized learning problem. In this work, we show that this phenomenon extends to non-linear spectral parameterizations in matrix sensing. More precisely, we propose a matrix network architecture called Spectral Neural Networks (abbrv. SNN) where each neuron applies nonlinear activation to the singular values of the input matrix. Conceptually, this entails coordinatizing the space of matrices by their singular values and singular vectors, as opposed to by their entries, a potentially fruitful perspective for matrix learning. We prove the exponentially fast decay of squared loss during the training dynamics of an SNN parameterized matrix sensing model under gradient flow (which is a continuous approximation of the discrete gradient descent), and also that the training implicitly selects an approximate solution that minimizes nuclear norm.
