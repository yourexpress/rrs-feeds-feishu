Large language model (LLM)-based multi-agent systems are increasingly applied across diverse domains, yet different applications require different forms of agent organization. Existing studies typically adopt predefined organizations, such as Star, Chain, and Tree, or learn communication graphs to improve performance on specific tasks. However, these methods are largely designed and evaluated within a limited set of application domains, providing little understanding of whether an organization effective in one domain remains suitable for others with different collaboration requirements. We present \textbf{OpenDGO}, a controlled benchmark and analysis framework covering six real-world domains and three collaboration settings: Sequential Dependency, Parallel Decomposition, and Information Integration. Under matched tasks, agents, tools, and resource limits, OpenDGO compares fixed and learned organizations while separately examining communication graphs, role assignments, input assignments, resource budgets, and component failures.
