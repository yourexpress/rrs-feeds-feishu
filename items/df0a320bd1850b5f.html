Stochastic computing (SC) encodes values as the probability of ones in random bit-streams, which allows arithmetic circuits with very low area and power and a natural tolerance to faults. The accuracy and security of an SC system depend on the quality of those bit-streams. Conventional generators build them from pseudo-random or quasi-random sequences, which are predictable, periodic, and correlated across streams drawn from the same generator. This work proposes three methods for converting the unbiased outputs of a true random number generator (TRNG) into stochastic bit-streams (SBS) with arbitrary target probabilities: a block extraction and comparison circuit, a counter-logic circuit, and a cascaded majority-logic pipeline. All three accept bits from any true- random source and are built from standard CMOS logic. They reach the accuracy of a software random generator for both independent and correlated stream generation, and they support the common SC arithmetic operations with error that falls with stream length at the same rate.
