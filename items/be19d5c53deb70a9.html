Long-Range Video Presentation Attack Detection: A Three-Class Benchmark Across 1-100 m and Two Sensor Types

Face presentation attack detection (PAD) systems deployed in border control and airport surveillance are constrained by a critical assumption: subjects are captured at close range, typically under one meter. No existing benchmark systematically addresses multi-distance capture, diverse environmental conditions, and concurrent spoofing and occlusion-based attacks, leaving deployed models underprepared for real-world scenarios. We introduce a video-based PAD dataset of 374,000+ frames from 261 unique identities collected across five standoff distances (1 m-100 m) under indoor and outdoor conditions using a DSLR camera (C) and a mobile sensor (M), with three-class labels: live, spoof, and props (occlusions); models are trained and evaluated on video clips rather than single frames. We fine-tune and benchmark four CNN architectures: MobileNetV3, MobileNet, EfficientNet, and ResNet-50. The higher-resolution DSLR maintains near-perfect spoof classification rate across all distances, while the lower-resolution mobile sensor degrades sharply beyond 50 m, with props classification rate dropping to 55% at 75 m for MobileNetV3.
