Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) changes logit margins among non-target tokens at teacher-forced prefixes. Matching a local reference distribution keeps those probabilities and also penalizes margin expansions. We ask whether a soft auxiliary can preserve selected local non-target margin relations without freezing that distribution. We introduce \textbf{Selective Rank-Preserving SFT (SRP-SFT)}, a soft margin target: a hinge on reference-scaled lower bounds for pairs from the reference's top-$K$ non-target tokens, applied at fixed teacher-forced prefixes. The hinge stops once the requested bound is met. We derive the KL cost of violating a margin, characterize the feasible region and its conditional utility, and give a finite-step condition for reducing aggregate violations. In one-epoch experiments on Qwen3-4B-Base and UltraFeedback, this target raises MATH-500 and code-generation scores over CE and LP-SFT at all three training seeds. At seed 1234, MATH-500 pass@1 rises from 49.56\% to 54.56\% and the four-metric code mean rises from 65.91\% to 69.50\% relative to LP-SFT.
