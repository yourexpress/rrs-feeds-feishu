Subgraph enumeration in partitioned graphs across distributed-memory systems is beset with challenges in balancing data movement with the local computation. Especially, counting the number of triangles induced by the closed wedges (i.e., length-3 cycles) is a measure of connectedness in a graph, but often limited by the significant search space caused by open wedges or length-2 paths (usually greater than the number of edges in a graph), which must be inspected to assess whether a particular wedge is closed. To mitigate triangle counting on massive graphs, distributed-memory partitioned-graph approaches are viable (i.e., assessing closed wedges independently within a partition), but load imbalance due to arbitrary communication can overshadow any gains from enhanced concurrency, owing to the inherent lack of structure in regular graphs. In this paper, we discuss a distributed-memory exact graph triangle counting implementation, and assess its performance over billion-edge real-world graphs on contemporary scale-out and scale-up based NVIDIA GPU platforms (considering NVIDIA ``Ampere'' and ``Hopper'' architectures), depicting a scalability spectrum of multiple orders of magnitude.
