Physics-Informed but Not Physics-Consistent: Error Geometry and Subspace Projection for Neural AC Power Flow

arXiv cs.LG · 2026-10-06 · 论文PDF

神经交流潮流的误差几何与子空间投影

Recent neural power-flow solvers, including emerging foundation models, achieve accurate voltage predictions, yet such accuracy does not necessarily imply physically consistent solutions. Even small complex voltage errors can yield large AC power-balance residuals. We study this accuracy-consistency gap across PIGNN-GC, GridSFM, gridfm-graphkit, and LUMINA on realistic 2224-bus Great Britain network (GBnetwork) scenarios, with cross-grid evaluation of GridSFM over 31 systems. Using a singular value decomposition (SVD) basis fitted to training AC power-flow solutions, we find that neural prediction errors contain substantial components outside the dominant solution subspace. To address this mismatch, calibrated solution-subspace projection (CSP) suppresses off-subspace prediction components after train-only bias calibration, reducing Mean PB by 67.0%, 37.8%, 40.5%, and 68.9% for PIGNN-GC, GridSFM, gridfm-graphkit, and LUMINA, respectively, relative to calibrated predictions, while improving voltage-magnitude accuracy in all four models.
