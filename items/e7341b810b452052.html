Prune to Probe: Causal Decision-Stability Probes for Efficient Reasoning

arXiv cs.LG · 2026-11-08 · 论文PDF

Reasoning models often continue generating after producing an answer, yet stopping prematurely may interrupt useful verification or revision. In this work, we investigate whether targeted interventions in a model's internal computation can provide a reliable signal for when to stop reasoning. We introduce Prune-to-Probe, an inference-time controller that uses causal activation interventions to probe the stability of a model's current answer. Specifically, we identify late-MLP coordinates that contribute to post-answer continuation while excluding those important for answer completion. Once an answer is stated, Prune-to-Probe temporarily suppresses the selected coordinates and measures the resulting stopping response relative to matched random interventions. Crucially, these interventions serve only as probes: the model either returns its existing answer or resumes generation from an untouched cache. On MATH, Prune-to-Probe reduces reasoning-token forwards by 2.60%, preserving all naturally correct trajectories while repairing six errors.
