Local certification is the task of verifying graph properties using certificates assigned to vertices and checked using only local information. A central question is which properties admit constant-size certificates, independently of network size. A fundamental test case is the parity of the network order. Bousquet, Feuilloley, Valenzuela, and Zeitoun (DISC 2026) showed that the complexity of certifying even order depends dramatically on the verification model. On arbitrary graphs, three bits per vertex suffice with unique identifiers and verification radius two. In the anonymous radius-one model, they established an $Ω(\log\log^* n)$ lower bound and an $O(\log n)$ upper bound. They also showed that constant-size certificates suffice in this model on bounded-expansion classes. In this paper, we settle the complexity of anonymous parity certification on general graphs and further investigate the role of graph sparsity.
