Category differences in similarity between fixation density maps and psychological potential fields for sketches

arXiv · 2026-11-06 · 论文PDF

When an object comes into view, humans can instantaneously recognize it if known to them. However, assessing how this recognition takes place remains challenging. A few studies using simple sketches have reported varying degrees of image similarity between the fixation density map (FDM), which visualizes eye movements and durations, and the psychological potential field (PPF), which visualizes part of the visual cognition process, depending on the object category. This study reports how the similarity behaves across three categories---animal, non-animal, and meaningless shape---and under rotated presentation, which is known to increase cognitive load during object recognition. The similarity was quantified through area under the curve (AUC) measures, including a baseline-adjusted AUC that isolates the early rise in similarity from stimulus-dependent baseline levels. A variability score was introduced to assess individual differences, and linear mixed models were used to evaluate category and orientation effects.
