Hallucination detection is critical for ensuring the reliability of Large Language Models (LLMs) in real-world deployments. While recent works, such as Semantic Entropy (SE) and Kernel Language Entropy (KLE), identify hallucinations by measuring semantic disagreement across multiple generated responses using Natural Language Inference (NLI) models, these methods depend on separately loaded NLI classifiers to estimate pairwise semantic similarity, introducing significant computational overhead. We propose Embedding Graph Kernel Entropy (EGKE), a variant of Kernel Language Entropy (KLE) that replaces its NLI-derived semantic graph with a Radial Basis Function (RBF) kernel applied to dense sentence embeddings generated by a BGE embedding model, while retaining KLE's graph Laplacian, heat kernel, and von Neumann entropy formulation unchanged. By computing similarities directly in a continuous embedding space, the proposed method provides a symmetric and computationally efficient alternative to NLI-based semantic comparison.
