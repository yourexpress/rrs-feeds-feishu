Audited Gradients, Anchored Policies: Model-Based Reinforcement Learning for Efficient Flow Control

arXiv cs.LG · 2026-11-09 · 论文PDF

Feedback controllers for flows are expensive to design, since each candidate must be evaluated with a high-fidelity solver. Differentiable surrogates reduce that cost, but they also introduce a failure mode that prediction error cannot detect. Policy optimization uses the derivative of the objective with respect to the action, which we call the action gradient and which can be computed either through the surrogate or through the high-fidelity solver. Optimization through the surrogate uses only its action gradient, and a surrogate can follow the flow trajectory closely while misestimating this derivative. The surrogate action gradient is compared against a central finite-difference estimate from the high-fidelity solver on held-out states, under acceptance criteria fixed before the comparison, and we call that comparison an audit. A low-dimensional feedback law is then fitted to the surrogate action gradient when the surrogate passes the audit, and to the solver estimate when it does not.
