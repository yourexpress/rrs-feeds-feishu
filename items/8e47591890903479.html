DELTA: Diagnosing Exploration Lineages via Twin-path Alignment - What Can Contrasting Design Paths Reveal About Microarchitectural Behavior?

arXiv · 2026-11-24 · 论文PDF

Architects have two mature instruments for performance work. Top-Down analysis tells them where a design loses its pipeline slots, and design-space exploration (DSE) tells them which configuration scores best. Neither explains why a search stops improving: why a backend-bound design rejects a prefetcher that the bottleneck label recommends, or why a capacity increase that helped in one configuration hurts in the next. This paper argues that the evidence needed to answer such questions is already produced during exploration and then discarded. Every parent-child edge in an exploration tree is a controlled intervention: one knob changes, the rest of the design is held fixed, and a cycle-level simulator measures the outcome. When the same edit helps under one parent and hurts under another, aligning the two parents localizes the interaction that decides its sign. We present DELTA, a simulator-grounded root-cause analysis tool that builds a Design Memory of such interventions during exploration and lets an architect query it afterwards.
