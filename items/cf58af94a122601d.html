TurboMamba: Faster Inference, Faster Training, and Better Prediction with Lexical Memory

We introduce TurboMamba, a language-model architecture for faster inference, faster training, and better token prediction. A global Mamba-3 model runs once per completed token block. A small Transformer uses this global context and earlier tokens within the block to predict the next token. Three lightweight additions improve local predictions: direct access to the previous block's last token, a small correction to next-token scores, and a learned mixture with recent-token frequencies. We compare twelve small-scale implementations on public FineWeb-Edu, with comparable parameter counts and shared training-budget caps. The baselines include Transformer, Mamba-3, Jamba, and Zamba. The Jamba and Zamba versions use Mamba-3 in place of their original Mamba modules. TurboMamba achieves the lowest mean test prediction loss (negative log-likelihood, NLL) among all twelve models. Its mean NLL is 9.4% lower than that of Mamba-3, 7.3% lower than that of the Jamba version, and 18.3% lower than that of the Zamba version.
