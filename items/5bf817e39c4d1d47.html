Benchmarking EEG feature families for ADHD research in children and adolescents: performance and explainability across attention tasks

arXiv · 2026-11-20 · 论文PDF

Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder varies in presentation. Diagnosis requires clinical and developmental assessment. Reproducible electroencephalography measures may complement clinical and behavioural characterisation, but differing feature definitions and evaluation methods hinder comparison. We compared seven feature families and four combinations across attention tasks and assessed the stability of held-out explanations. Two serious games yielded four overlapping attention-task cohorts of 123-140 participants each. Repeated nested participant-held-out evaluation assessed recruitment-group discrimination. Exploratory paired participant-cluster bootstrap tests corrected for multiplicity in two separate families: 72 individual-task contrasts and 18 fusion contrasts. For eight configurations selected after inspecting predictive results, we explained held-out native probabilities before calibration. In the strongest task context, the highest configuration mean AUROC over three repetitions was 0.773, with a fixed-prediction 95% interval of 0.692-0.854 unadjusted for selecting the maximum.
