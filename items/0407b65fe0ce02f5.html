PARDIG: Global Precipitation Estimates Learned from Sparse Satellite Observations

arXiv cs.LG · 2026-11-10 · 论文PDF

Estimating precipitation at the global scale is vital for Earth system science and disaster response, yet observational coverage and measurement precision remain constrained. Existing solutions provide options that trade off desirable traits such as skill, latency, geographical coverage, temporal coverage, and spatial and temporal resolution. We present PARDIG, an artificial intelligence framework based on a spherical graph-mesh transformer architecture that produces near-real-time global precipitation estimates. PARDIG is trained on dense multi-modal inputs, including atmospheric state analyses and multi-spectral geostationary satellite imagery, to predict sparse, high-precision precipitation measurements from spaceborne radar. At inference, the trained model is applied densely across the globe at $0.1^\circ$ spatial and 15-minute temporal resolution, producing complete near-real-time precipitation fields from 2016 to the present, alongside a historical variant extending the record back to 1999.
