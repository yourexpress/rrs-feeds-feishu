Studies of what fine-tuning does to a language model often summarise the weight update $ΔW$ by its rank. We compare the rank measures used for this against the same measures on a random matrix of the same shape, and against two harder references: the pretrained weights, and matched fine-tunes without alignment content. Measures that count directions put our fine-tuning updates only a little below noise; stable rank, computed from the same singular values, puts them far below. Both are right: an update has a few strong directions on top of a long tail. The pattern holds for thirteen released pairs up to 8B parameters, and it is not a mark of alignment: with training held fixed, instruction data does not stand out from Wikipedia or code, DPO gives the same update whether its preference labels are real or half swapped at random (one model, modest preference strength), and scrambled text gives the most concentrated update of all.
