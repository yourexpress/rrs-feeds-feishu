Large language models (LLMs) offer a promising path for directly generating functionally correct and synthesizable HLS-C from natural-language specifications. However, existing approaches often struggle to turn multi-round tool feedback and reusable HLS knowledge into effective revisions, limiting end-to-end generation success. We propose MementoHLS, an agentic HLS generation framework built around executable and falsifiable dual-memory-guided repair. MementoHLS combines an Executable Repair Contract Library (ERCL), which converts cross-task HLS knowledge into evidence-gated repair actions, with a Reviewer-Grounded Falsification Ledger (RFL), which converts task-local repair history into trajectory evidence to suppress ineffective routes and preserve validated progress. To ensure reliable outcome attribution, a Same-Candidate Reviewer binds Parse, Compile, Testbench (TB), and Synthesis (Synth) outcomes to the corresponding candidate, while a Single-Commit Arbiter commits at most one candidate per round.
