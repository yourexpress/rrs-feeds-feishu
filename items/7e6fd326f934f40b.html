Unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) networks require a network provisioning mechanism that guarantees delivery and latency quality of service (QoS) under dynamic conditions in next-generation airborne mesh networks by continuously learning and repairing multi-hop paths under rapid link turnover without flooding the control plane. This paper presents INC-SRv6, an in-network computing routing mechanism that repurposes the IPv6 Segment Routing Header (SRH) and its Segments Left (SL) pointer into a self-learning, fast-failover distributed control plane. Rather than treating the SRH as a static source-committed vector, INC-SRv6 uses the active SL pointer to partition the segment list in flight: slots ahead of SL provide capacity for hop-by-hop next-hop recording, while the suffix behind SL serves as an in-band multi-destination reachability vector. Through this dual-zone header discipline, transit routers extract distance-weighted reachability updates from arriving suffixes, gate admission by single-pass confidence with bounded binary probing, advance or reverse SL deterministically, and restore pre-transmission SRH state for local failover upon link loss.
