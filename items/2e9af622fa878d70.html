Subspace Crystallization in Grokking: Rank Compression Tracks the Phase Transition

Grokking describes understanding something deeply and intuitively. In neural networks, it is the phenomenon where a network generalizes long after memorizing its training data. This delay has been linked to a fall in the effective rank of the network's weight matrices, but whether this rank compression drives grokking or follows it is still unresolved. We study a mean-field MLP and a one-layer transformer trained on modular arithmetic and observe that: (i) Compression follows the onset, not memorization. Every transformer memorizes its training data within 250 steps, while the grokking onset (the first step at which test accuracy reaches 95%) varies from 6,500 to 21,000 steps. The steepest fall in effective rank comes about 900 steps after the onset, however late the onset occurs, and networks that memorize without grokking compress far less. (ii) Subspace crystallization. We call the span of the fewest top singular directions of a weight matrix that preserve 99% of test accuracy its sufficient subspace.
