Which Successes Make Good References? Action-Sequence Alignment for Multi-Turn On-Policy Distillation

arXiv cs.LG · 2026-11-09 · 论文PDF

On-policy distillation (OPD) reduces train-inference distribution mismatch by supervising a student on its own trajectories. For multi-turn tool-use agents, conditioning the teacher on a validated same-task successful trajectory as a reference can provide useful hindsight, but different successes may reach incompatible interaction states and offer misleading supervision. We cast successful-reference selection as a free-target optimal transport problem, which reduces to assigning each failed trajectory its most compatible successful reference. Because latent state paths are hidden, we use action-sequence alignment as an observable proxy for compatibility and instantiate it with longest common subsequence (LCS), which identifies a shared ordered backbone of actions while allowing recoverable detours. This perspective makes trajectory compatibility the central criterion for reference selection in multi-turn OPD. Experiments on ALFWorld and the airline domain of $τ^3$-bench show that LCS is the only evaluated reference selector to improve over reference-free OPD on both ALFWorld overall scores and all three airline metrics.
