Detecting Empty Abstractions and Pruning Dead Paths in the Linux eBPF Verifier with Abstract Step Functions

arXiv · 2026-11-17 · 论文PDF

Linux eBPF verifier 用抽象步函数检测空抽象并剪枝死路径

The Linux kernel's eBPF verifier uses abstract interpretation to ensure the safety of user-provided programs before execution. It tracks each register with multiple interval domains and a bitwise domain (tristate numbers). Refining the abstract state at conditional branches can produce mutually contradictory constraints across domains, indicating an unreachable path. However, the verifier lacks an empty-set ($\bot$) representation and cannot detect when these domains have an empty intersection, causing it to analyze dead paths. Passing such contradictory states into domain reduction or downstream operators violates their implicit non-emptiness precondition, triggering domain invariant violations. This has been a multi-year issue in the Linux kernel causing production outages, false rejections, and state explosion. This paper presents a sound and complete constant-time emptiness check across the eBPF verifier's abstract domains to prune dead branches. We introduce abstract step functions, a general framework that tests whether intervals and non-convex domains intersect in O(1) time without scanning individual values.
