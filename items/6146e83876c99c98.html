SPEC CPU2026 Under the Microscope: Programs, Phases, and Samples

arXiv cs.AR · 2026-11-12 · 论文PDF

SPEC CPU2026, the latest generation of the SPEC CPU benchmarks, is set to become the standard benchmark suite in computer architecture research, where new designs are often evaluated through sampled simulation with tools such as SimPoint. Existing characterizations of the suite focus on whole-run averages, which reveal neither how behavior changes within a run nor how well sampling captures it. We characterize CPU2026 on real hardware at three levels: programs, phases, and samples. At the program level, we report whole-run metrics and top-down bottleneck classes for all four suites and examine how they change with workload size, compiler version, and platform, across machines with three different instruction set architectures: x86, Arm, and RISC-V. Notably, changing only the compiler version can halve or nearly double the IPC of individual benchmarks. At the phase level, we track hardware counters every 10 ms over all 90 SPECrate inputs, identify recurring patterns of time-varying behavior, and divide the inputs into three groups: steady, multi-phase, and bursty.
