Layerwise Learning-Rate Scaling in Vision Networks: Effective-Rate Identification, Power-Mean Aggregation, and Budget Dependence

arXiv cs.LG · 2026-11-16 · 论文PDF

视觉网络逐层学习率缩放的等效学习率刻画

Layerwise learning-rate scaling assigns different learning rates to different parts of a network. Although established in fine-tuning pretrained models, its effects in vision networks trained from scratch are commonly assessed by training each candidate recipe. We show that, in this setting, the rate-related component of a layerwise strategy is well summarized by an effective learning rate computable directly from its multiplier profile. Across matched-control comparisons, changes in test accuracy closely track changes in training loss (r = -0.996), which supports an optimization-progress explanation of the short-budget rate effect. Increasing the untuned base learning rate tenfold shifts the rate-matched contrast upward in all six tested architectures (p = 0.016) and increases the number of architectures in which a published discriminative schedule, excluded from model fitting, outperforms the uniform control from two to five of six (p = 0.031). Within the evaluated strategy space, a parameter-weighted power mean with exponent 0.35 (95% confidence interval [0.20, 0.50]) accounts for 42.6% of within-architecture variance, compared with 36.4% for the arithmetic mean.
