Does the Workspace Do the Work? A Causal Protocol for Verifying Global-Workspace Computation in a Language Model

Global Workspace Theory (GWT), a leading theory of consciousness, holds that information becomes conscious when it is broadcast to specialized processes that use it. Building a workspace into a network does not reveal what the trained network computes with it: a module that reports an answer may have combined a broadcast message with its own knowledge, or merely forwarded an answer computed elsewhere, and task accuracy and ablation cannot tell these apart. We introduce a causal protocol that identifies who computes when modules communicate. Its central test transplants a message from another input, chosen so that ignoring, relaying and recombining it predict three different answers. On networks built to relay or to recombine, the test assigns the right computation in every case. Among trained workspace models it finds all three outcomes: one leaves its broadcast nearly unused, and of two models that differ only in their training data, one relays and the other recombines.
