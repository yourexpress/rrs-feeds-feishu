BRAT: Bounded Residual-Aligned Training for Stable Multi-Pass LLM Post-Training

arXiv cs.LG · 2026-11-07 · 论文PDF

Reinforcement learning for large language model post-training often reuses each batch of sampled rollouts for several gradient passes to amortize the cost of generation. Under such multi-pass reuse, existing objectives fail in different ways: unclipped updates collapse as the policy drifts away from the sampling policy, adding a negative baseline avoids collapse but reinforces every sampled response, and PPO-style ratio clipping cuts off gradients abruptly. We first ask why reuse is fragile. Measuring how well an update estimated from a small rollout group aligns with the KL-regularized target on held-out rollouts of the same prompts, we find that this alignment drops by nearly an order of magnitude from in-sample to held-out rollouts across three base models, suggesting that repeated passes largely overfit the sampled group. Motivated by this finding, we propose Bounded Residual-Aligned Training (BRAT), motivated by a proximal update toward a bounded reweighting of the sampling policy.
