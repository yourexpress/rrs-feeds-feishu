How AI Load Swings Reduce Datacenter Power Conversion Efficiency

arXiv(cs.AR) · 2026-10-23 · 论文PDF

AI training and inference workloads make GPU power swing repeatedly between compute and communication phases.

AI training and inference workloads make GPU power swing repeatedly between compute and communication phases. As a result, the cascaded converters in a datacenter rack keep moving between operating points with different efficiencies. This paper uses an energy based, cycle averaged method to quantify that effect along a 240 V AC to 400 V DC to 51 V DC rack power path, using measured efficiency curves of commercially available GaN based PFC and LLC converters. For a 1 Hz, 50% duty-cycle load swinging between 5 kW and 510 W, conversion efficiency falls to 95.97%, compared with 96.82% at the equivalent constant average load, a loss of 0.88%. When 51/48V to 1V point of load conversion to the chip rail is included, it is shown that the loss grows to 1.66% (89.45% vs. 90.96%). These results show that efficiency rated at average load overstates real world performance, and that the penalty grows with each load dependent conversion stage between the grid and the GPU.
