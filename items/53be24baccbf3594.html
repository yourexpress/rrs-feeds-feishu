Reading or Remembering? Measuring Temporal Contamination in Language Models

arXiv cs.LG · 2026-11-16 · 论文PDF

测量语言模型时间污染：读还是记

Language models are now used to read central-bank text for economic forecasting, but most were trained on text written after the documents they read. A model reading a 2021 FOMC statement may already know that inflation rose in 2022, so part of its apparent skill can be memory of the outcome, a temporal form of test-set contamination. We measure this memory on FOMC statements and minutes from 2000 to 2026 by comparing modern open models with chronologically consistent models, which are trained only on text from before a fixed date. Asked about past GDP outcomes given only a date, models that cannot know the answer still reach an AUC of up to 0.64 from priors on dates and eras, so chance is the wrong baseline. Against these memory-free models on the same questions, most modern models recall which years had strong growth. Membership-inference scores sort documents by era and document type rather than by whether a model saw them, as exact counts in an open pretraining corpus show.
