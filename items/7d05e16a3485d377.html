CoVeR: Coverage-Based Token Pruning for Multi-View 3D Reasoning in VLMs

Representing a 3D scene as multi-view images allows 2D VLMs to reason in 3D by reusing priors from pre-training, sidestepping the scarcity of annotated 3D data. However, it produces thousands of redundant visual tokens whose cost grows with every view. Existing visual token pruners fall into two families, each limited in the 3D multi-view setting. Learned importance methods rank tokens by attention or encoder features; because redundancy here is fundamentally spatial, they keep near-duplicate tokens from a few prominent regions and leave most of the scene unrepresented. Voxelization methods improve spatial coverage but cannot enforce an exact token budget and saturate as multi-view observations overlap in 3D, capping retention well below the target. We show that spatial coverage is associated with 3D reasoning performance and introduce CoVeR, a deterministic, training-free selector that uses only token coordinates, with no learned signals.
