Surprise Alone Learns Lies: Evidence-Gated Continual Learning for LLMs from Streaming Claims

Continual learning methods for large language models increasingly decide what to learn by surprise, keeping the inputs the model finds least predictable. This rule is unsafe when the stream contains lies. A planted falsehood about something the model knows has low probability under the model's belief, so it is highly surprising by construction, and a surprise-driven learner absorbs nearly every such lie, at a rate similar to random selection with the same budget. We study the problem with LoRA-adapted students (Qwen2.5-1.5B, Qwen2.5-3B and Llama-3.2-3B) that learn from a stream of true and false claims about invented people and real CounterFact facts. We report four findings. (1) A verifier key, a judge that must approve each claim, removes lies to the extent that its verifier is accurate; real LLM judges are cautious and reject unsourced truths and even source-backed updates. (2) Calibrated regression tests on unrelated knowledge slow learning down without filtering out lies. (3) The original model's own prior flags claims that contradict what it knows, without a judge.
