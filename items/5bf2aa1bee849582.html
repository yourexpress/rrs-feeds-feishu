Characterizing Recoverable Expert-Utilization Signatures in Fine-Tuned Mixture-of-Experts LLMs

arXiv (cs.LG) · 2026-10-29 · 论文PDF

As Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) large language models are increasingly deployed in distributed environments, from federated learning to edge-cloud systems, their architecture harbors an underexplored landscape of security vulnerabilities. Moving beyond textual analysis, we uncover structural memorization: a novel vulnerability where a fine-tuned model memorizes not only its training data but also the internal pathway used to process it, creating a characteristic routing fingerprint unique to the training process. We provide comprehensive empirical evidence that structural memorization emerges consistently even from a few samples, and that its intensity is directly influenced by the choice of trainable modules. We show that these memorized expert pathways can be stealthily recovered through the model's unconditional generation, using only routing and output logits, with no knowledge of the fine-tuning data or procedure. In distributed deployments, this vulnerability is amplified as routing information is exposed at multiple trust boundaries, including federated aggregation points, edge-cloud interfaces, and multi-tenant monitoring systems.
