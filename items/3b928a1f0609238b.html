e-means (Bessa et al., 2026) is a recent mini-batch k-means algorithm, now part of the FAISS library, that reaches the error of Lloyd's algorithm about an order of magnitude faster on large embedding datasets. Every five mini-batches, it removes every cluster whose running point count falls below a tenth of the average and reuses its centre to split a large cluster. In the authors' ablation, removing this mechanism, called dead-cluster revival, raises the error more than removing any other component added to the moving-average update. The rule, however, considers only cluster size: it cannot distinguish a cluster that is small because it is redundant from one that covers a rare, isolated group of points, and on data with such groups it repeatedly removes centres whose removal is costly. We add a second condition, based on the distance between centres: a small cluster is removed only if its centre is close to another centre, relative to a running estimate of the mean squared error. When a centre equals the mean of its points, this squared distance is an upper bound on the average increase in squared error, per point of the cluster, caused by removing the centre.
