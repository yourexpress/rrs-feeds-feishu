Large language models trained on data generated by other models can sometimes inherit traits unrelated to that data, a phenomenon called subliminal learning. We study both hard label distillation, where students train on numbers generated by a biased teacher, and on-policy soft label distillation, where students generate the data and the teacher's logits provide the learning signal. Using Pythia, PolyPythia, and DataDecide OLMo models, we find that transfer persists in both settings across differences in weight initialization, pretraining data order, pretraining corpus, model size, and architecture. Across pretraining checkpoints, the ability to transmit a preference and the ability to acquire one develop separately: some checkpoints are more effective teachers but weaker students, and vice versa. Consistent with this distinction, cross-model transfer can be strongly asymmetric in magnitude when teacher and student roles are reversed.
