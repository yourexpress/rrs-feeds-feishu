Detecting Harmful Short Videos in Toxic Diet Culture: A Governance-Ready Evidence Framework with Sparse Autoencoders

arXiv · 2026-11-23 · 论文PDF

Algorithmic content moderation is increasingly important for governing short-form video content, yet harmful-content detection research focuses primarily on prediction or limited explanations. This leaves a governance challenge underexplored: producing decision-linked evidence that supports the review and justification of moderation decisions. Using toxic diet culture as an empirical case, we conceptualize governance-ready evidence as computational traceability, evidence localization, policy grounding, and evidence inspectability. We develop the Sparse Autoencoder-based Policy-Aligned Contrastive Evidence (SPACE) framework in a pretrained SAE feature space derived from a large multimodal model. A global relevance gate and decomposable linear evidence head identify decision-linked evidence; segment supervision and within-video ranking localize this evidence; policy alignment maps it to platform guidelines; and Pareto-efficient selection yields a compact, task-specific evidence vocabulary.
