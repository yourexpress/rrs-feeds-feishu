DeFSM: Robust and Efficient Decentralization of Federated Submodular Maximization

arXiv cs.DC · 2026-11-09 · 论文PDF

Submodular maximization seeks a small subset best representing a large dataset. In its federated setting, the server is a single point of failure and some clients may be Byzantine. Removing the server raises a trilemma of decentraliza- tion, efficiency, and security. We propose DeFSM, a Byzantine-robust and communication-efficient architecture that replaces the server with a lightweight smart contract and a pool of independent, consensus-free agents. Its aggregation rule, SafeGM, builds on two notions from combinatorial geometry: Tukey depth and safe points (the points lying in the convex hull of the good points wherever the bad points are). SafeGM makes the robust decision on 2D Gaussian random projections, which the contract can compute efficiently, and lifts it back as a convex combination of actual updates. Since lifting loses information, it uses the projected geometric median only when its depth certifies it, and otherwise a Tukey median. The share of bad updates is then bounded only by a count: how many there are, not what they contain. We prove, and confirm empirically, that this bound controls the solution quality.
