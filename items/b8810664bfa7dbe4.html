Concurrent Use of FP32 and FP64 Computing Units on GPUs and Mixed-Precision Placement in WENO Reconstruction

arXiv cs.DC · 2026-11-06 · 论文PDF

Scientific computing often requires FP64 arithmetic, although FP64 performance is limited on NVIDIA GPUs. Double-float (DF) emulation, which represents a value using two FP32 values with about 48 bits of significand, outperforms native FP64 where FP64 units are limited. On the other hand, mixed-precision strategies offload to FP32 the operations that do not determine accuracy. In both approaches, the native FP64 units remain idle during FP32 instruction execution. We investigated whether this idle resource can be recovered on A100, GH200, GB200, and RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell Max-Q. A microbenchmark shows that co-resident FP32 and native-FP64 kernels exceed the throughput of the faster kernel alone on A100 but not on GH200 and GB200; on RTX 6000 Blackwell, they do so against FFMA but, against DF emulation, only when the issue density of the DF kernel is reduced. We then tested whether this gain carries over to computing the WENO-Z reconstruction with the nonlinear weights in FP32 and the candidate polynomials in native FP64 (W32P64).
