Looped language models such as Ouro reuse one block of layers several times per token. A rollout engine can batch the deeper loops of several response positions and run them only when a deep readout is due. This deferred schedule is fast, but the learner of an on-policy RL algorithm recomputes the sampled tokens' log-probabilities with a dense pass, and the two computations differ in floating point: in FP32 at 4096 tokens about 18% of the sampled tokens miss a $10^{-5}$ same-weight gate, and with bf16 weights nearly every token does. We use a hybrid learner log-probability, $\ell=\ell_{\mathrm{dense}}+\mathrm{sg}(\ell_{\mathrm{sched}}-\ell_{\mathrm{dense}})$, where $\mathrm{sg}$ denotes stop-gradient. Its value is a no-grad replay through the rollout's own schedule, and its gradient is the dense learner's.
