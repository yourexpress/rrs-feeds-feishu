Selecting LLM-Generated Hypotheses Raises Truth Beyond What an LLM Panel Expects: A Pre-registered Study on NAS-Bench-201 and HPOBench

arXiv (cs.LG) · 2026-10-31 · 论文PDF

LLM research-ideation systems aim for hypotheses that are both true and non-obvious, yet neither property can normally be checked. We build a testbed in which both can. Generators propose conditional interaction claims about two fully tabulated benchmarks, NAS-Bench-201 and HPOBench; every claim is verified exactly against the tables, and its non-obviousness is scored against a calibrated blind prior elicited from other LLMs. Excess truth measures how much more often a run's claims hold than this panel expects. Across a series of pre-registered stages with Claude and GPT generators, our main positive result is a simple two-stage step found by a harness optimizer: draft a large pool of claims, then select a subset in a separate call. For Sonnet on NAS-Bench-201, this step raised excess truth in every pre-registered test, including against a same-pool ablation that keeps only likely-true claims, without lowering precision. The gain comes both from credit for true claims and from avoiding near-miss false ones.
