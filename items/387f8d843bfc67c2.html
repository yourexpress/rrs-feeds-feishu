Knowledge Distillation from Neural Networks to Differentiable Logic Networks

arXiv cs.LG · 2026-11-06 · 论文PDF

Differentiable logic networks (DLNs) are interpretable, edge-friendly classifiers that learn both their logic operators and their connections through gradient descent, achieving accuracy competitive with multi-layer perceptrons at orders-of-magnitude lower inference cost. Their accuracy, however, is bounded by the problems of vanishing gradients, scalability, and lack of an ecosystem, leaving a gap relative to conventional neural networks on several datasets. We introduce knowledge distillation for DLNs to narrow this gap. We distill from a trained multi-layer perceptron teacher into a DLN student, integrating the distillation objective into a two-phase training loop without altering the architecture or the inference procedure. The teacher is discarded after training. Hence, the distilled model retains the interpretability, sparsity, and inference cost of a conventionally trained DLN.
