On-policy distillation (OPD) trains agents on their own trajectories using supervision from a stronger teacher. We uncover a counterintuitive phenomenon in multi-turn agentic tasks: a smaller, initially weaker student can substantially outperform its teacher during training, only to regress as distillation continues. We term this phenomenon distillation overshoot. Through decision- and task-level analyses, we trace overshoot to two competing effects of OPD: repairing weak student decisions and eroding successful student choices that the teacher disfavors. Early in training, repair outpaces erosion, allowing the student to combine the teacher's corrections with its own strengths and surpass the teacher. As training continues, erosion overtakes repair, causing these gains to vanish. Motivated by this finding, we propose Success-Preserving On-Policy Distillation (SPOD), which selectively masks conflicting teacher supervision on successful student choices and introduces a soft probability floor to counteract their erosion, while retaining teacher guidance elsewhere.
