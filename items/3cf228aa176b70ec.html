Understanding Data Influence in Transportation Networks: A Physics-Constrained Influence-Function Framework

arXiv · 2026-11-06 · 论文PDF

Understanding how individual training observations influence model predictions is important for developing reliable and interpretable data-driven transportation systems. Existing influence-function methods, however, are primarily formulated for unconstrained learning and do not explicitly account for physical constraints that couple different components of a transportation network. Under such constraints, perturbing a training observation can affect model parameters and predictions only through physically feasible directions, potentially changing how its influence propagates across the network. This paper develops a hard physics-informed influence-function framework for constrained learning, in which training-data influence is characterized within the physically feasible space defined by network constraints. The analytical results reveal how physical constraints alter the magnitude, direction, and spatial distribution of training-sample influence relative to conventional influence measures.
