NANO: Unifying Missingness and Input Uncertainty in Tabular Learning

arXiv · 2026-11-22 · 论文PDF

Missing values are common in tabular data, yet current approaches to handling them often overlook two dimensions. First, an observed value may be uncertain, while a Not-Observed (N.O.) value may still have a reasonable estimate. Inputs should therefore span a continuous spectrum from no evidence to a fully trusted observation. Second, Not-Applicable (N.A.) means that no meaningful value exists for a record, yet this semantic state often lacks explicit representation. To address both dimensions, we introduce NANO, a unified plug-in for input encoding that fits most token-based architectures. Guided by Bayesian evidence weighting, NANO adjusts the influence of a supplied value according to confidence, with all zero-confidence estimates mapped to the same N.O. representation. NANO accepts, but does not require, per-example confidence information during training or inference. For N.A., a separate representation enables models to encode inapplicability and its schema-defined reasons into context.
