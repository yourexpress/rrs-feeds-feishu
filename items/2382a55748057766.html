Multi-teacher on-policy distillation (MOPD) extends on-policy distillation (OPD) to consolidate capabilities from multiple teachers. Although prior work reports near-perfect in-distribution (IND) distillation performance, we find that this can mask incomplete capability consolidation beyond the supervised distribution. A preliminary study on synthetic reasoning shows that OPD does not fully transfer teachers' generalization capabilities. We then extend this investigation to real-world tasks, evaluating extrapolative and compositional generalization across mathematical reasoning, coding, agent, and long-context capabilities using Qwen3 models. Across extrapolative settings, OPD and MOPD reach 96.04-102.73% of their teachers' IND scores. Yet their out-of-distribution (OOD) scores remain 2.45-16.25% below the base model's. Across three compositional settings, MOPD improves IND performance over the base model, but its performance on composed tasks as OOD remains 18.19-24.34% below that of the best teachers.
