In this work, we propose a unified framework for training algorithms across different stages of modern large language model development, based on the perspective of distribution matching. We show that a broad class of language model training algorithms can be interpreted as minimizing a certain divergence between the current model and a target distribution, and that characterizing the properties of this target distribution provides substantial insight into the behavior of the corresponding training algorithms. By analyzing specific forms of the target distribution, we establish that On-Policy Distillation (OPD) enjoys an advantage over classical Knowledge Distillation (KD) when the current model distribution is close to the target distribution, while exhibiting a disadvantage when the two distributions are far apart. Furthermore, when the target distribution itself depends on the model being optimized, we show that the accumulation of estimation variance can induce a deterministic degradation of the target distribution.
