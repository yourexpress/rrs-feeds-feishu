A Simple Phase-King Protocol with Predictions

arXiv cs.DC · 2026-11-08 · 论文PDF

Inspired by recent work (Ben-David, Dzulfikar, Ellen, and Gilbert, $\textit{Byzantine Agreement with Predictions}$, PODC 2025) we study synchronous binary Byzantine Agreement in the learning-augmented setting, where each process receives predictions about which processes are honest or Byzantine. We present a deterministic Phase King protocol for the anauthenticated setting, combining early stopping with a specifically designed prediction-aware king selection procedure. For at most $t<n/3$ Byzantine processes, the protocol handles arbitrarily many prediction errors, improving upon the $O(n^{3/2})$ bound of Ben-David et al., and terminates within $O(P)$ communication rounds, where $P=1+\min\{\mathbf{B}/n,f\}$, $\mathbf{B}$ is the total number of incorrect prediction bits held by honest processes, and $f$ is the actual number of Byzantine processes. Our protocol uses $O(n^2P)$ messages and $O(n^2P\log n)$ bits, avoiding the initial cubic bit cost of exchanging complete prediction vectors.
