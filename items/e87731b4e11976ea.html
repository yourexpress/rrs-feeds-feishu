Sample-Efficient In-Context Adaptation via Sleep

arXiv · 2026-11-24 · 论文PDF

The ability to learn and generalize from limited experience, or sample efficiency, is a cornerstone of intelligence. How can we build language models that turn a few user interactions, a few snippets of unfamiliar code, or a handful of experimental results provided in context into knowledge that generalizes? Prior work has found that sleep, a process where the model performs $N$ recurrent forward passes to refine its representations of a chunk of context, can improve long-context reasoning (specifically, within SSM-attention hybrid models). We study whether this method also improves in-context adaptation on general, non-synthetic tasks for transformer language models. To this end, we pre-train language models up to 1.2B-parameters on up to 100B tokens with sleep mechanisms. Notably, longer sleep (i.e., using a larger number of recurrent forward passes $N$) enables the model to learn faster in-context on multiple few-shot algorithmic, question-answering tasks, while also achieving lower next-token prediction loss on out-of-distribution evaluations.
