A Bias in Disguise: Tiny Gradient Edits Stop a Small Language Model's Thinking Loops and Complement a Frontier Model's Weight Edit

arXiv · 2026-11-24 · 论文PDF

Qwen3.5-0.8B rarely finishes reasoning in thinking mode: at a 4,096-token budget only 1 or 2 of 500 MATH-500 answers close their think block (two seeds), and accuracy is 15.4%. A recent demonstration had GPT-6 Astra edit 131,072 of the model's weights without gradients and reported MATH-500 rising from 7.9% to 48.1% at a 32,768-token budget. We provide the missing gradient baseline, then combine the two edits. Fine-tuning only the 140 to 139,750 weights with the largest gradient, on the model's own correct answers to MATH training problems, raises thinking-mode MATH-500 at 4k tokens to 39.0-45.5% after 2-9 minutes of training on one GPU; 50 training examples suffice, and 1,398 random positions give only 16.3%. The gradient concentrates the edit on the most nearly constant channel of each of the first three layers. Because the model has no bias terms, an edit confined to those channels acts as a constant vector added to the residual stream: adding three fixed vectors to the unedited model matches the accuracy of such an edit.
