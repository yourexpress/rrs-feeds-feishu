Optical Wireless Communication (OWC) systems provide high-bandwidth, spatially secure, and RF-interference-free digital transmission. However, direct free-space optical links suffer from extreme sensitivity to line-of-sight disruptions, optical attenuation, high ambient light saturation, and transmitter-receiver misalignment. Traditional channel diagnostic frameworks rely on external computing nodes or cloud-based processing, which introduces severe diagnostic latency and reduces edge node autonomy. This paper presents an autonomous, self-diagnosing optical wireless receiver node that integrates physical-layer signal telemetry extraction with an on-device TinyML classification engine. Built around an ESP32-S3 microcontroller, the receiver recovers 1000 bit/s On-Off Keying (OOK) digital payloads using a 5-sample majority voting mechanism while concurrently extracting real-time channel features: Bit Error Rate (BER), received signal voltage (Vsignal), Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNRdB), and ambient light baseline (Lambient).
