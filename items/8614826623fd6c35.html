PACER: GPU Excursion Management for ML Workloads

arXiv cs.DC · 2026-11-12 · 论文PDF

Variability is an inherent characteristic of modern GPU systems, arising from a combination of process variation, dynamic environmental conditions, and power management decisions. Prior work has characterized inter-GPU variability and found consistent performance differences across GPUs. However, we observe that modern workloads, particularly large language models (LLMs), exhibit significant intra-GPU variability. Their short-running kernels interact poorly with the coarse granularity of dynamic power management mechanisms, leading to unpredictable performance. A key source of this intra-GPU transient variability is power excursions, also referred to as electrical design power peaks (EDP$_{p}$), where power temporarily exceeds the GPU's rated power limits. While power management mechanisms are designed to ensure reliable operation despite the presence of EDP$_{p}$ sustained power peaks cause throttling that detrimentally impacts overall application performance.
