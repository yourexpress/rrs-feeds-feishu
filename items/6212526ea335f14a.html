Moving Generality from Model to Modeler: LLM Agents for PHM on a Deployment-Style Benchmark

Coding agents built on large language models (LLMs) now help develop many models published as general solutions. Should generality instead reside in the agent that builds a model for each new task? We examine this question in prognostics and health management (PHM), where every model must work on unseen equipment. Our deployment-style benchmark, NextUnit-Bench, covers 24 public datasets split into 58 folds that hold out units or operating conditions; each agent has two hours to deliver an executable predictor, then run on the hidden units. First, frontier agents outperform AutoML, which reaches less than half the best agent's score, and faithful implementations of published methods on four datasets, of which only 6 of 30 beat the strongest agent's own design; published accuracies, where reproducible, rest on the papers' own conventions, and a single convention, capping the remaining life of test engines, can halve the reported error.
