Control-Flow Profiling for Workload Representativeness

arXiv cs.PF · 2026-11-12 · 论文PDF

Workload characterization in performance engineering often replaces expensive target executions with lower-cost proxies, including reduced datasets, miniapps, and training inputs. Such substitutions require empirical verification that the proxy faithfully exercises the target's dynamic execution behavior. This paper presents an address-keyed control-flow profiling methodology and tool suite designed to evaluate workload representativeness directly from compiled, unmodified binaries. We pair time-ordered basic-block vector (BBV) recurrence analysis with exp-branchprof, an experimental Valgrind tool that captures five orthogonal structural profile families spanning branch direction bias, four-bit global history contexts, dynamic indirect targets, function call transitions, and intraprocedural CFG edges. Our method compares weighted event distributions from two executions of the same binary. It reports support coverage, reference event-mass coverage, distribution distance, entropy-derived quantities, and phase structure, rather than claiming that a single similarity score certifies a proxy.
