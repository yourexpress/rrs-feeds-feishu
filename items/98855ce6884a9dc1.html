Accelerating Distributed Digital Ising Machines

分布式数字 Ising 机的加速：面向组合优化与概率采样的规模扩展

Ising machines, dedicated accelerators built around the dynamics of Ising-like spin models, have emerged as a leading platform for combinatorial optimization and probabilistic sampling. The problems that motivate them require millions of spins, far beyond the on-chip capacity of any single device, so a practical Ising machine must distribute its computation across many devices. Prior work reports throughput at a single operating point, leaving the binding resource unidentified and platform crossovers measurable only by experiment; no calibrated cross-platform cost model of distributed Ising machines has been reported, nor have systematic techniques to accelerate their Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) workloads. This work presents a calibrated cost model that decomposes digital Ising sweep time into compute, memory, synchronization, and inter-device communication terms, validated with 4.8% mean error across three GPU generations, FPGA and ASIC up to 1 million spins.
