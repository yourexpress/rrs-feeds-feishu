On the Design of a Slow Control Interlock System for the European GateMate FPGA

arXiv · 2026-11-24 · 论文PDF

Slow Control Systems (SCSs) are essential for the monitoring, configuration, and protection of distributed safety-critical systems in scientific, industrial, and medical applications. Although software-based SCS architectures offer flexibility and scalability, they suffer from software aging and non-deterministic latencies, which limit long-term reliability and timely reaction to critical errors, often requiring additional hardware interlock systems. This paper presents an FPGA-centric lightweight Slow Control Interlock System (SCIS) designed to achieve deterministic ultra-low-latency reaction times in long-running dependable systems. System parameters are acquired and fused at runtime in a Supervisory Monitor Unit (SMU), enabling real-time fault condition classification and prioritization through configurable priority rules and triggering kill-switch actions with microsecond-scale latency. The proposed SMU has been described in VHDL as vendor-agnostic architecture, and assessed on the 28-nm GateMate M1A1 FPGA, using the open-source OSS-CAD-Suite toolchain.
