When Can You Trust Neural Critics? Auditing Mutual Information Estimates of Higher-Order Epistasis in Protein Language Models

arXiv cs.LG · 2026-11-06 · 论文PDF

Mutual information (MI) estimation is used to probe what neural representations encode, including in protein language models, but variational MI estimators have variance that scales at least exponentially in the true MI. We audit a prior neural-MI study of order-resolved epistasis in ESM-2 on the four-site GB1 landscape. Run-to-run differences show no systematic bias and a magnitude comparable to ordinary cross-seed retraining variability, and the evaluation-pairing scheme shifts scores substantially. At the original dimensionality, two of three alternative estimator families stabilize the estimate across seeds and one does not. In a synthetic calibration at the representation dimensionalities of two other protein language models (a Gaussian channel with known MI; no model representations), all three families recover materially less signal than required, despite correct signal ordering and stable seeds.
