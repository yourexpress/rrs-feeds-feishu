Auditing and Adapting kNNGuard for Long-Context Prompts

arXiv · 2026-11-20 · 论文PDF

kNNGuard 长上下文提示的审计与适配

Training-free activation guardrails such as kNNGuard score prompts by comparing last-token LLM hidden states (the Layer Ensemble branch, kNNGuard LE) and sentence embeddings (all-MiniLM-L6-v2, Embedding-kNN) against a small reference bank (50 examples per class), combining the two branches into a Fused Ensemble (kNNGuard FE). While effective on short inputs (up to 256 tokens), standard kNNGuard degrades substantially on long inputs (over 1000 tokens): averaged across Qwen3.5-4B and Gemma-4-E4B-it, kNNGuard LE AUROC drops from 0.742 (0.777 and 0.706) to 0.554 (0.587 and 0.521) on Content Harm Moderation (Aegis-2.0), and from 0.994 (0.995 and 0.992) to 0.556 (0.598 and 0.513) on RAG Hallucination Detection (HaluBench). We trace this degradation to three causes: (1) hidden-state drift as sequence length increases, (2) hard 256-token truncation in MiniLM, and (3) signal dilution when a long document is compressed into a single final-token vector. We then evaluate three training-free adaptations: a Length-Routed Dual Bank, a long-context embedding model (Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B), and Strided Window Pooling across intermediate token positions.
