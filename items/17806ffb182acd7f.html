Large language models (LLMs) trained on vast amounts of data have demonstrated impressive performance over a range of tasks, but future performance gains will require increasing amounts of data and compute. Federated Learning (FL) facilitates training on decentralized private data, but client resource limitations can make training impractical, particularly as model sizes continue to expand. These resource constraints make the assumption of a shared server-client architecture impractical. Model heterogeneous FL offers a promising alternative by adapting model size to client capabilities; however, previous work is limited to the pre-training setting, and conclusions on sub-network selection do not necessarily hold for fine-tuning. Furthermore, prior approaches create client sub-networks without considering their unique data distributions, thereby overlooking the opportunity to tailor sub-network architecture to local data.
