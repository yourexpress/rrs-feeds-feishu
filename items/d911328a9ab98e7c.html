Beyond Binary Classification: Multiclass Machine Learning for Diabetes and Prediabetes Risk Prediction Using NHANES

arXiv cs.LG · 2026-11-12 · 论文PDF

Detecting diabetes risk early is important for preventing disease progression. Most existing machine learning approaches focus on binary classification, which limits their ability to capture intermediate risk states such as prediabetes. We develop a supervised learning framework to classify adults as normal, prediabetic, or diabetic using National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey data. The framework uses routinely collected demographic, clinical, and lifestyle variables from 15,356 adults, with glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c) used only to define labels and excluded from input features to prevent leakage. We compare conventional machine learning and transformer-based models and examine two binary formulations: normal versus prediabetes, and normal versus combined prediabetes and diabetes. Models are evaluated on a held-out test set using precision, sensitivity, specificity, and F1 score. In the multiclass setting, performance is broadly consistent across models, with accuracy up to 0.72 and macro-averaged F1 of 0.70.
