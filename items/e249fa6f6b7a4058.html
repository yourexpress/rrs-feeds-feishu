In many communication networks, the transmission of a packet may automatically trigger the transmission of a subsequent packet from the same source after a (possibly random) delay, without requiring acknowledgment or feedback. Such behavior arises in multi-stage status updating, proactive protocols, and other applications where users generate causally dependent packet streams. In this paper, in order to analyze these systems, we introduce the $\mathrm{M^T/G/1}$ queue. In this model, primary customers arrive according to a Poisson process, and each primary customer triggers a secondary customer to join the queue after an independent delay. This arrival mechanism falls outside the scope of classical queueing models with renewal arrival processes. When the triggering delays follow an exponential distribution, we exploit the memoryless property to set up a tractable Markov description. By truncating the number of pending secondary customers, we derive a finite system of linear algebraic equations in the Laplace--Stieltjes transform domain and solve them using matrix-analytic methods.
