Surrogate modeling of transport requires operators that capture the combined influence of spatially distributed sources and Dirichlet and Neumann boundary conditions, together with training datasets that can be generated at low computational cost. This work presents MIGONet, a multiple-input graph neural operator, and a discrete method of manufactured solutions (MMS) that trains it without solving a partial differential equation, using steady heat conduction as the model problem. In the discrete MMS procedure, temperature fields are prescribed a priori, and the source terms and Dirichlet or Neumann boundary data are evaluated with the same discrete operators as the reference finite-difference method (FDM), so every training sample satisfies the discrete governing equations by construction. Because the boundary data are derived from each field, a Dirichlet or Neumann condition can be assigned to every boundary segment of every sample, and the boundary specification becomes an operator input.
