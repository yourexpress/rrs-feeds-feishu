Joint Classification and Generation on Tabular Data: an Empirical Study

arXiv · 2026-11-24 · 论文PDF

Many applications of machine learning on tabular data need both a classifier and a generator of realistic records. The default is to train two separate models, whereas a single joint model promises both from one set of weights. Several families of tabular deep learning models can be turned into such a joint model, which raises the question of which joint model is preferable at which computational budget. In this empirical study, we introduce and compare joint models based on four state-of-the-art tabular data modeling families (energy-based models, probabilistic circuits, tabular foundation models and tabular diffusion models) against measured FLOPs and wall-clock time, on 29 classification datasets. Methods are evaluated based on two defined aggregate scores that take into account different aspects of prediction and generation. We then also compare these joint models with every pair of a separate classifier and generator at different computational budgets, to see when a joint model may be a defendible choice against the regular practice of keeping a separate classifier and generator.
