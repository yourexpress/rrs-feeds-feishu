Mamba Flow Matching Neural Processes: Linear-Time Inference for Irregularly Observed Spatial Fields

Interpolating a spatial field, such as daily maximum temperature, from sparse, irregularly located observations recurs across the environmental and health sciences. Standard Gaussian-process kriging refits hyperparameters and solves an $O(N^3)$ system for every new field. When many related fields are observed, such as daily maps from one sensor network, neural processes (NPs) instead learn the interpolation map from past fields and predict a new field in one forward pass. Joint-predictive variants, which sample whole fields rather than independent per-location values, typically use self-attention over context and query points, costing $O(K^2)$ in the combined point count $K$ and limiting practical use to a few thousand points. State-space models offer a linear-time alternative, but a direct state-space adaptation compresses the context into one hidden state before reading queries, reducing what the decoder sees. We propose MambaFlowNP, a linear-compute joint-predictive NP trained with conditional flow matching.
