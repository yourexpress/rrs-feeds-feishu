Revisiting Fast Tendermint: An Agreement Violation and Two Repairs

Fast Tendermint reduces Tendermint's good-case latency to two communication steps for n>=5f+1. We show that its published round-change rule violates Agreement: one Byzantine process suffices for every f>=1, and for f>=2 two Byzantine processes suffice without double signing. The failure occurs when a process advances past a round without recording the value that may have been decided there. The Quint specification that accompanies the protocol does not allow this kind of round skipping and is not affected; the violation lies in the published pseudocode. We present two repairs. Walk requires a process to leave each round on a quorum of that round's precommits and to precommit before leaving. It keeps the recorded valid state and removes the wait after a precommit quorum. Justify carries the evidence needed to accept a proposal in the proposal itself, removing both the recorded valid state and the proposer's synchronization wait. Both satisfy Agreement and Validity under asynchrony and Termination under the partially synchronous gossip model of Tendermint and Fast Tendermint. Both retain proposal timeouts.
