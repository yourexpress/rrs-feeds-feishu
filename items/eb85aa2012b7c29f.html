The Illusion of Grounding: Do Vision-Language Models Weigh Retrieved Text and Images Like an Ideal Observer?

arXiv cs.LG · 2026-11-17 · 论文PDF

检索增强 VLM 的证据权衡基准（20 万条）

Retrieval-augmented vision-language models (VLMs) answer from two sources of evidence that can each fail: the retrieved text can be wrong or noisy, and the image can be degraded or contradicted. We introduce GIB-200K, 200,000 image-question items built from COCO and Visual Genome, each expanded into controlled stress conditions: hard-negative retrieval noise (1-20 documents), visual ambiguity (Gaussian and motion blur, low-resource crops) and cross-modal conflict with counterfactual flip pairs. Instead of only measuring degradation, we ask how models combine the two modalities. From ideal Bayesian cue combination we derive a testable prediction that yields formal definitions of Knowledge Inertia (text over-weighted relative to the model's own image evidence) and Sensory Capture (the reverse), and we estimate the weights with a measurement-error-corrected regression whose recovery of known weights we verify. A counterfactual flip decomposition separates modality bias from concept priors, and a Grounding Illusion Score measures how much clean accuracy is not grounded in the image.
