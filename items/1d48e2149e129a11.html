Machine learning weather models now rival operational global forecasts but still start from analyses produced by separate, expensive data assimilation (DA) systems, which for global models are typically updated every six hours. Observation-to-observation models avoid this dependence, but most omit the recursive filtering cycle that lets DA systems carry a background state through sparse, irregular observations. We introduce WeatherDreamer, a world model that learns this cycle end-to-end from 18 global observing systems, without reanalysis. It infers a latent state from observations, carries it forward hour by hour with learned dynamics, corrects it each hour with new observations, and can predict what any of its observing systems would measure at any location. At held-out stations, its ensembles beat NOAA's Global Ensemble Forecast System (GEFS) for temperature, dew point and wind out to 12 h and are better calibrated than GEFS and the ECMWF ensemble. Precipitation, cloud-top height and solar irradiance, never inputs at inference, are inferred with skill matching or exceeding operational products at short leads.
