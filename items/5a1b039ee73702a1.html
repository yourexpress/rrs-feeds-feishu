Does Random Search Discover Task Experts? Revisiting Neural Thickets

arXiv (cs.LG) · 2026-10-31 · 论文PDF

Pretrained models are typically viewed as generalists, requiring bespoke post-training to achieve task-specific expertise. Gan & Isola (2026) recently proposed a striking alternative: that pretrained models lie in a dense neural thicket of task specialists, which can be surfaced with simple Gaussian weight perturbations. We examine this neural thicket of task-specific experts and identify several key findings that question this perspective. First, we find that the apparent lift of the best-of-N selected perturbation decays quickly with selection-set size m, strongly suggesting overfitting to the selection questions. In fact, for 10 of the 12 model-task pairs we study, a simple null model of pure selection noise predicts more than 75 percent of the top expert's reward lift. Second, the top experts' advantage often vanishes on held-out questions or remains within the run-to-run variability of the unperturbed pretrained model. Third, we find that majority voting over the top-K selected perturbations, termed RandOpt(N, K) by Gan & Isola (2026), is often matched or outperformed by majority voting over K samples from the base model.
