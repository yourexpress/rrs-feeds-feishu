Player Disjoint Multi-Task Tennis Stroke Analysis from Monocular Pose Sequences

arXiv · 2026-11-06 · 论文PDF

Analyzing tennis technique from monocular RGB video could make coaching more accessible, but prior methods usually rely on hand-trimmed strokes, test on random splits that leak player identity, or fail on players with different body proportions. We present an end-to-end, player-disjoint framework with an autonomous kinetic stroke event detector, a 78-dimensional causal velocity representation ([X, V]), a 569,095-parameter multi-task Transformer Encoder (E7), exploratory 9-dimensional kinematic deviation profiling, and a heuristic feedback prototype. All models are evaluated with Clean LOPO v2: Leave-One-Player-Out cross-validation over 7 players and 3 seeds, giving 21 outer evaluations and 147 training runs per configuration, with nested inner cross-validation. Against 206 frozen consensus impact events, the detector reaches an F1-score of 85.65% (+3.24% over baseline thresholding) with a mean absolute timing error of 80.6 ms. Under heavy class imbalance, Stroke Type reaches 74.27% accuracy, 65.37% balanced accuracy, and Macro-F1 = 0.6453.
