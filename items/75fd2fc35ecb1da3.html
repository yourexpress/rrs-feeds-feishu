Predicting Process-Level Behavior: A Benchmarking Study

arXiv · 2026-11-18 · 论文PDF

预测流程级行为：基准研究（预测式流程监控）

Predictive process monitoring (PPM) typically forecasts the future of individual process instances, hence operating on trace level. Process managers, however, typically operate on process level, e.g., when assigning staff or calculating KPIs. The question is how well such process-level managerial decisions are supported by existing approaches, given that PPM research has so far not focused on process-level predictions. We answer this question through a comprehensive benchmarking study of two process-level prediction tasks: concurrent trace prediction and average throughput time prediction. Across synthetic and real-life event logs, we compare statistical and machine-learning-based methods, foundation models, business process simulation, and, importantly, established trace-level PPM approaches, aggregating their predictions to the process-level. We assess which modeling paradigms are most suitable for predicting process-level behavior and whether existing trace-level PPM can be effectively repurposed for this task.
