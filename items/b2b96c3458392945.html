Read disturbance in dynamic random-access memory (DRAM) depends on both local device interactions and the electrical conditions imposed by shared array circuitry. Here, we combine DIMM-level characterization results, device measurements, and TCAD simulations to interpret RowHammer, RowPress, and ColumnDisturb within a unified device-level physical framework. We relate charge evolution at the floating storage node to three key mechanisms: trap-assisted electron migration (EM), capacitive coupling (CC), and sense-amplifier-mediated disturb (SAMD). EM is local and trap-sensitive, while CC weakens with wordline separation. SAMD extends disturbance through shared column circuitry by modifying unselected-cell leakage under rail-level bitline biases, a contribution supported by the approximately ninefold higher OFF-state leakage measured relative to retention-like conditions for a high-voltage storage state. These distinct spatial ranges account for localized RowHammer and RowPress failures and remote ColumnDisturb populations with abrupt changes at subarray boundaries.
