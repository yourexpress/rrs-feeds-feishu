National-scale mapping of tree species composition in Switzerland from high-resolution aerial imagery and multimodal data

arXiv · 2026-11-06 · 论文PDF

Tree-species composition shapes forest productivity and how forests respond to climate disturbances such as drought, windthrow and insect outbreaks. Yet most national forest maps assign each pixel a single dominant species and cannot show the share of accompanying species. We mapped the basal-area shares of ten main tree species and a residual Other class across Switzerland at 30 m resolution. A deep-learning model combining 0.1 m aerial RGB imagery, airborne laser scanning (ALS), annual AlphaEarth satellite embeddings, and climate variables was trained on basal-area observations from 3,992 Swiss National Forest Inventory (NFI) sites. In spatial cross-validation, the model reached a macro-$R^2$ of 0.485 across the 11 classes. On an independent forest-reserve dataset, the map reached a macro-$R^2$ of 0.552 for individual 30 m pixels and 0.733 for plots, and identified the dominant class in 73.2% of pixels and 81.1% of plots. The map shows maple and ash as widespread minor components that dominant-class maps omit.
