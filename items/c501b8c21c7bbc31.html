Computational coarse-graining aims to replace a sequence of updates with a cheaper module while preserving task performance. We present a continuation-relative evaluation framework that assesses replacements through the computation that follows them, using downstream task quality and total execution cost as joint criteria. The framework distinguishes boundary representations, summaries of skipped computation, executed operations, and downstream loss. It makes interfaces and permitted deployment information explicit, combines fine-path references with directly trained controls using the same shortened architecture, and accounts for selection overhead. An exact loss decomposition separates replacement effects at a fixed downstream action from effects of changing that action. A finite construction shows how retained information can reduce the stipulated cost of zero-error prediction under fixed information and controller restrictions. Experiments in two frozen pretrained language models show that representation fidelity and task loss can favor different fitting objectives.
