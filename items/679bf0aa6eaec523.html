Approaching Optimal Scaling of Queue Size for an Input-Queued Switch

We study the scaling of the expected total queue length in an $n\times n$ input-queued switch. It has been conjectured that the optimal scaling is $O(n/(1-ρ))$, up to logarithmic factors, where $ρ$ is the system load. This conjecture has been established in the traditional heavy-traffic regime and in certain sub-regimes of the large-system regime. But it remains unresolved over a wide range of load scalings in the large-system regime. In this paper, we develop a family of scheduling policies parameterized by a positive integer $k\ge 2$. We show that under uniform traffic, our policy achieves an expected total queue length of $O\bigl({n\log\max\{n,1/(1-ρ)\}}\big/{(1-ρ)^{2^k/(2^k-1)}}\bigr)$, as long as $ρ\to 1$ in the large-system regime. As $k$ increases, the exponent $2^k/(2^k-1)$ approaches $1$, bringing our bound arbitrarily close to the conjectured scaling. The expected amortized scheduling cost of the policy is $O(n^3 k)$ per time slot. Our policy is of a batching type, with a novel recursive design that allows an arbitrary number of batching levels.
