Understanding the Role of Reasoning Data in Pretraining

arXiv cs.LG · 2026-11-14 · 论文PDF

理解预训练中推理数据的作用

Reasoning data expose models to multi-step reasoning, but reasoning traces may cover only a subset of the available facts. How should pretraining balance atomic facts with reasoning traces? We study this question by constructing a setting in which every fact provides an atomic training signal, while only a subset appears in reasoning supervision, enabling us to measure generalization to transitions observed atomically but never used in training traces. For a linear transformer, we derive the weight assigned to atomic training signals that minimizes held-transition squared error and show that its leading-order optimum increases with reasoning-chain length and decreases with the fraction of facts held out from reasoning traces. These trends persist in a full transformer layer with an MLP, softmax attention, and cross-entropy loss. We further validate the role of reasoning complexity and data coverage in two natural-language settings: controlled multi-hop reasoning and mathematical pretraining. Together, our results provide a principled view of how reasoning-chain structure and data coverage should shape the allocation of training data during pretraining.
