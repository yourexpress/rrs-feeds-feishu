What Field Enumeration Costs to Compile in C++26: Native Reflection, Structured-Binding Packs, Reflection Libraries and Hand-Written Serializers on GCC 16

arXiv · 2026-11-23 · 论文PDF

C++26 adds standard ways to enumerate a struct's fields, among them static reflection (P2996, with P3394 annotations) and structured-binding packs (P1061), alongside hand-written code, X-macros and libraries (Boost.PFR, reflect-cpp, Glaze) that now ship pre-C++26 and C++26 paths. We time each in a struct serializer with byte-identical output on GCC 16.2.0, at the front end and at -O2, in randomized interleaved rounds with code-level checks of every single-factor contrast. At the front end, inline native enumeration in the form used in several of P2996's own examples, an expansion statement over the std::span of define_static_array, costs about as much per struct of 16 scalar fields as Boost.PFR and 7.96x a P1061 pack, itself about as cheap as hand-written code. The same loop over a constant array costs 0.47x as much: about half of the span form's cost comes with the span. The ratio to P1061 shrinks on a heterogeneous type graph and at -O2.
