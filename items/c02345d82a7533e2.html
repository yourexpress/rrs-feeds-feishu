Early detection of mild cognitive impairment remains a critical bottleneck in dementia care, demanding scalable tools capable of capturing subtle behavioral decay. To capture the subtle temporal changes of cognitive decline, we introduce a behavioral dataset collected across seven unscripted multimodal interactions over an 18-month horizon. This dataset formalizes continuous, multi-session interactions into a dense 4D tensor (Batch * Session * Time * Feature) that explicitly preserves concurrent local dynamics alongside long-term changes. To effectively process these complex behavioral patterns, we propose CogDrift-4D: a novel, hierarchical 4D Transformer framework. To capture the complex trajectory of cognitive decline, CogDrift-4D implements a dual-level behavioral fatigue and drift modeling architecture. At the intra-session level, the model computes localized least-squares regression slopes to quantify the acute ``fading'' of frame-level facial action units and acoustic energy. At the inter-session level, a recursive cross-attention feedback encoder acts as a cumulative fatigue accumulator, mapping the patient's continuous behavioral drift across successive sessions.
