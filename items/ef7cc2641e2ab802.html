Corrective Diffusion Language Models

arXiv · 2026-11-06 · 论文PDF

While Diffusion Language Models (DLMs) are theoretically well-suited for iterative refinement due to their non-causal structure, off-the-shelf masked diffusion language models (MDLMs) often fail to reliably revise incorrect tokens in practice. The key challenge is that the model cannot tell correct from erroneous visible tokens: standard MDLM training supervises only masked positions, undermining confidence-guided refinement. We study corrective behavior in DLMs, the ability to assign lower confidence to incorrect tokens and iteratively refine them while preserving correct content. Under matched compute and data, we show that conventional masked-diffusion fine-tuning induces much less of this capability than a correction-oriented post-training principle that explicitly supervises visible corrupted tokens, which enables discriminative confidence and targeted refinement. We further find that also supervising clean visible tokens, as in BERT- or GIDD-style training, stops remask-based refinement from improving with additional steps.
