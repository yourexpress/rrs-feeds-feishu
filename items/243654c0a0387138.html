From WSIs to Bulk, Spatial and Single-Cell Transcriptomics via Multi-Scale Graph Neural Networks

arXiv (cs.LG) · 2026-10-30 · 论文PDF

Understanding the spatial distribution of gene expression is key to studying complex diseases like cancer. While Spatial Transcriptomics (ST) and Single-Cell Transcriptomics (scT) provide high-resolution insights, they remain expensive and scarce. In contrast, standard histopathology Whole-Slide Images (WSIs) paired with bulk RNA sequencing are widely available. This work introduces a Multi-Scale Graph Neural Network (GNN) capable of simultaneously predicting bulk, spatial, and single-cell transcriptomics directly from WSIs. By extracting morphological features using a Pathology Foundation Model (PFM) (UNI2-h) and constructing spatial graphs at both patch and cell levels, the model learns the hierarchical relationships within the tissue. The network is trained on a combined dataset of The Cancer Genome Atlas Program (TCGA), Visium, and Xenium samples across lung, kidney, and breast tissues. A hybrid training strategy is employed, leveraging direct supervision alongside weakly supervised consistency losses to ensure predictions align across biological scales.
