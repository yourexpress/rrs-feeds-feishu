Pearl: TEE-Enabled Fair Ordering with Minimal Replication

arXiv · 2026-11-23 · 论文PDF

Recent research has investigated fair transaction ordering in blockchain consensus. An orthogonal line of work has demonstrated that equipping replicas with trusted execution environments (TEEs) enables more efficient consensus protocols with only $2f{+}1$ replicas. In this work, we bridge these two areas and demonstrate that two key fair-ordering properties, ordering linearizability and $γ$-batch-order fairness with minimal batching, cannot be implemented in partially synchronous networks with $2f{+}1$ replicas, even when the replicas are equipped with TEEs. We show that Byzantine replicas can circumvent TEE protections by manipulating the timing of transaction submissions to the enclave, thereby biasing the resulting ordering indicators. However, we prove that assuming a synchronous network allows both properties to be implemented with a reduced replica count. We propose two mechanisms: one uses absolute TEE-attested timestamps to guarantee ordering linearizability in a system of $n \ge 2f{+}1$ replicas, and the other uses TEE-maintained relative sequence numbers to guarantee $γ$-batch-order fairness when $n > \frac{2γf}{2γ-1}$.
