Masked diffusion models train a denoiser on masked cross-entropy and generate by unmasking many positions in parallel. Existing MDM sampling bounds assume an exact denoiser or leave its error uncontrolled while training bounds cover only the masking distribution on which the model was trained. We present an end-to-end guarantee for a learned denoiser under conditional mixing (CM): stationary sources (e.g. floored order-$r$ Markov and two-sided-Doeblin hidden-Markov sources) for which revealing distant visible tokens moves a masked token's posterior on average exponentially little in the distance with correlation length $τ_c$. We show the empirical risk minimizer trained under any masking distribution attains per-token excess $\varepsilon'$ on that distribution from $\varepsilon'^{-\mathcal{O}(τ_c\log|\mathcal{V}|)}$ training sequences, independent of sequence length $N$ -- one sample budget serves every decoding schedule when each schedule's denoiser is trained under its own weighting. We further show that the exponent's order $Θ(τ_c\log|\mathcal{V}|)$ is minimax-optimal up to constants; more efficient rates require added structure.
