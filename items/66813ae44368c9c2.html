Learning Pseudo-Riemannian Manifolds for Heterophilic Graphs via Graph Signature

arXiv · 2026-11-05 · 论文PDF

Several widely used families of Graph Neural Networks carry a positive-definite geometric prior. Graph convolution read as heat diffusion needs a positive semi-definite operator, hyperbolic models place features on positive-definite Riemannian manifolds, and dot-product and GAT-style attention scores use positive-definite inner products. On heterophilic graphs, where edges often signify dissimilarity or structural repulsion, that prior is a mismatch: no positive-definite form can assign a negative squared distance to a direction the labels separate. To measure the mismatch, we introduce the Graph Signature Index ($s_{\mathcal{G}}$), the inertia of a class-compatibility matrix built from label topology alone, and we prove that no value of the edge or node homophily index in $(0,1)$ rules out a negative eigenvalue. We then propose the Pseudo-Riemannian Attention Network (PRAT), whose attention logit is a signed squared pseudo-distance under a learnable diagonal metric initialized from $s_{\mathcal{G}}$.
