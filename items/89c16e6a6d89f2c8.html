Stationary tail computation for high-dimensional reflected Brownian motions (RBMs) remains computationally challenging. Building on NN4MGF's shared-coordinate basic-adjoint-relationship (BAR) framework, we develop Holomorphic Cauchy Networks (HCNs). Native complex-valued rational Cauchy subnetworks enforce holomorphicity on pole-free domains, removing sampled Cauchy-Riemann penalties and their input-derivative graphs. Exact origin anchoring and boundary-coordinate exclusion enforce structural identities, while anchored interactions represent dependent transforms. Under holomorphic-log assumptions, we establish simultaneous value and first-derivative approximation for the constrained architectures and a mixed-derivative lower bound for coordinate-only representations under nonzero cross-covariance. Closed-form real-axis derivatives and sequential backpropagation further control memory. On the same NVIDIA H100 NVL, the tested 30D HCN configuration uses approximately tenfold less peak allocated memory and has 9.7-fold lower mean update time than NN4MGF; extrapolation to the respective scheduled update counts gives a projected full-schedule update-time ratio of 58.2.
