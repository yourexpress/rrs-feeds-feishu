Large Language Models (LLMs) rely on Multi-Head Attention (MHA) for sequence modeling, but scaling to long contexts is constrained by the quadratic cost of attention, motivating sub-quadratic recurrent alternatives. This paper formalizes a Dynamic Wave recurrent layer, based on input-dependent exponential damping and cosine phase modulation, and evaluates three 10.94M-parameter configurations (Pure Transformer, Pure Wave, and Hybrid Wave-Attention) on TinyStories under perplexity, latency, throughput and peak VRAM profiling up to 4096 tokens. The Hybrid configuration reaches the best perplexity, 5.95 on the full held-out split against 6.93 for the Transformer and 7.26 for Pure Wave. The efficiency picture, however, is governed by implementation rather than by architecture. Against an unfused attention baseline, Pure Wave uses 78.7% less peak VRAM at a 4096-token context (1404.3 MB vs. 6601.6 MB); against a fused scaled-dot-product attention kernel the same comparison yields no advantage at all (1404.3 MB vs. 1403.6 MB), and in training mode Pure Wave is 8.3% more expensive than the Transformer.
