- The Moroccan Ministry of Digital Transition and Administration released two open-source AI tools developed with France's Mistral AI to process local language nuances, including Moroccan Darija.

- The release features an Arabic dialect classifier and an adapted automatic speech recognition model based on Mistral's Voxtral architecture that transcribes code-switching between Arabic, French, and English.

- Developed under the "Maroc Digital 2030" strategy, the tools provide reusable components for public administrations, startups, and developers to build local AI applications.

Moroccan authorities partnership with French startup Mistral AI began delivering concrete deliverables with the release of two open-source artificial intelligence components designed to process national linguistic nuances. The Ministry of Digital Transition and Administrative Reform made the tools available to developers and enterprises on Tuesday, September 29.

The initial release includes two specialized language components. The first tool acts as a classifier capable of identifying various Arabic dialects, specifically Moroccan Darija. The second component provides automatic speech recognition based on Mistral AI's Voxtral architecture, adapting the model to transcribe complex audio exchanges that alternate between Arabic, French, and English.

This development focuses on adapting artificial intelligence models to specific local linguistic environments rather than creating a foundational model. Moroccan citizens use Darija extensively in daily communication, but the dialect differs significantly from Modern Standard Arabic and frequently appears in multilingual conversations.
