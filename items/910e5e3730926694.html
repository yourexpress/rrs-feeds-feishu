Stephen: Verifying Sequencer-Aided Coordination Systems with Cross-View Prophecy

Many distributed systems achieve high performance by relying on dedicated sequencers to order requests, leaving replicas to commit them in that order. Despite more than a decade of such sequencer-aided coordination (SAC) systems, from Corfu and NOPaxos to LazyLog and SwitchBFT, their correctness has been argued only with pen-and-paper proofs or bounded model checking at the protocol level. In this paper, we formally verify implementations of SAC protocols. The sequencer seems to make this easy: it decides the order of all requests, so the proof can place each request's linearization point at the sequencer using a well-known technique, prophecy variables. Yet a linearization point placed at the sequencer is not durable, because the sequencer orders a request before any replica commits it. Crashes and other rare events may trigger a view change that discards the request, nullifying the earlier decision and the proof that rests on it. Our framework, Stephen, extends prophecy variables to handle view changes, and we make three contributions.
