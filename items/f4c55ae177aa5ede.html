Expert-level detection of low-voltage intervals in EEG as a robust biomarker of neonatal encephalopathy

arXiv · 2026-10-08 · 论文PDF

Background: Discontinuity in neonatal EEG is a critical marker of brain dysfunction but can be difficult to quantify visually. Inter-burst intervals (IBIs) are a key characteristic of discontinuity, yet their definition does not easily translate to AI detection. Objectives: To introduce low-voltage intervals (LVIs), defined exclusively by amplitude and duration, as a context-independent alternative to IBIs, develop an AI detection method for LVIs, and assess their clinical utility as a marker of brain injury. Methods: We developed a ConvNeXt model to detect LVIs and compared its performance with existing methods. We then tested the clinical relevance of detected LVIs across four applications: 1) grading background EEG abnormalities on an open benchmark dataset, 2) classifying clinical hypoxic-ischaemic encephalopathy (HIE) grades and comparing with a normal EEG dataset, 3) seizure prediction, and 4) association with seizure burden. Results: For training and validation, 654 hours of per-channel EEG from 123 neonates were reviewed and 51,162 LVIs were annotated.
