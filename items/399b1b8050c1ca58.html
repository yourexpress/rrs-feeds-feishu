Geometry of Confabulation and Refusal in Small Language Models

arXiv (cs.LG) · 2026-10-31 · 论文PDF

Faced with an unanswerable question, small language models can either refuse or confabulate: confidently produce a wrong answer. Can the hidden state before the first generated token provide a clue? Can we flip confabulations into refusals? I introduce ConfabQA, a 784-item benchmark whose obscure-pre-cutoff category breaks the typical correctness/knowledge-cutoff confound and map the geometry of the hidden state with linear probes, a sparse autoencoder (SAE), and direct optimization of intervention directions. On Qwen3-1.7B and Gemma 2 2B the probe separates upcoming refusals from confabulations well beyond prompt features (+7.4/+16.3 pp); on Llama 3.2 3B the ConfabQA margin is within fold-to-fold spread, but a direct refusal probe on PopQA and TriviaQA recovers +18-25 pp. On Qwen I closely inspect three candidate directions for the refusal vector: probe-recovered, Qwen-Scope SAE feature, and directly optimized. Although the three directions are pairwise 50-81 degrees apart, a push at or below the state's own norm along any of them flips most confabulations into refusal openings dominated by the "As..." family.
