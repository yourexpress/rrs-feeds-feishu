AmbiHTMLBench: A Benchmark for HTML-based Visually Rich Document parsing

arXiv · 2026-11-05 · 论文PDF

Traditional document parsing systems use Markdown as their output representation, which is effective for linear text but does not fully preserve fine-grained spatial structure, table geometry, visual hierarchy, or rendering semantics. We show that HTML is advantageous for heterogeneous and layout-sensitive documents whose representations must remain editable, inspectable, and reusable, and introduce AmbiHTMLBench to systematically study this setting. In this benchmark, given a real-world document, a model must recover executable HTML that preserves its content, organization, and visual appearance. The benchmark contains 1,115 diverse pages with structured annotations and evaluates predictions in two different ways: The objective protocol aligns ground-truth and predicted rendered blocks and measures table fidelity, page-level text error, and a two-dimensional layout consistency; a complementary reference-anchored protocol measures perceived fidelity by aligning LLM judges with human preferences.
