Iterated Narrow Networks: Turing Completeness at Width 4 on Convex Input Spaces and the Complexity of Verification

arXiv (cs.LG) · 2026-11-02 · 论文PDF

We study the iterates of a fixed ReLU network $N$ of width 4, that is, the discrete-time system $x_{k+1} = N(x_k)$ on $[0,1]^4$. Equivalently, $x_k$ is the output of a weight-tied network made of $k$ copies of $N$, or of a recurrent network with no external input, started at $x_0$. We prove that every deterministic Turing machine $M$ admits such a network $N_M$. The network can be constructed in polynomial time from $M$. Each iteration has depth $\mathrm{poly}(|M|)$. The iterates of $N_M$ simulate $M$ exactly on every encoded tape. The number of iterations is proportional to the running time of $M$. Moreover, the fourth coordinate irreversibly flags every point of the convex cube $[0,1]^4$ whose simulation reads an invalid digit. This flag is the certificate that makes hardness reductions sound on convex input regions, the regime of practical verifiers. Convex regions were previously inaccessible because of a continuity obstruction inherent to ReLU networks. The correspondence has three consequences.
