Learning by Examples: Power Grid Steady State Analysis with a Tabular Foundation Model

Foundation models have shown strong generalization across scientific domains, but their use in power systems remains largely task-specific. We investigate whether a pretrained tabular foundation model (TFM), TabPFN, can serve as a general-purpose neural solver for power grid steady-state analysis. We evaluate the TFM on three tasks: power flow (PF) prediction, N-k contingency screening (NCS), and voltage stability margin (VSM) prediction. We performed experiments on 118- and 2000-bus systems, comparing the TFM against task-specific neural baselines. Without retraining or fine-tuning, the TFM matches or outperforms the best task-specific model in several settings across the three tasks. For 118-bus PF prediction, a variant of the TFM achieves errors of $1\times 10^{-3}$ pu for voltage magnitudes and $0.30^\circ$ for voltage angles, outperforming all baselines. On unseen N-1 contingency and close-to-infeasible PF cases, the TFM is competitive with the DCPF approximation. For millions of unseen N-2 contingencies, the TFM achieves AUCs of 0.92 and 0.95 for branch and voltage violation screening, respectively.
