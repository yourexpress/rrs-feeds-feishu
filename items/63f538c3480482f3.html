Large Language Model (LLM) agents are vulnerable to adversarial inputs that induce attacker-specified malicious behaviors, commonly referred to as jailbreaking. Existing optimization-based jailbreak attacks primarily employ first-order (FO) methods that leverage gradient information or zeroth-order (ZO) methods that rely on function-value feedback. However, their complementarity and optimization ordering remain underexplored. We systematically investigate four strategies: pure ZO, pure FO, ZO->FO, and FO->ZO, and develop a ZO-FO framework that explores adversarial prefixes through ZO optimization and refines their corresponding suffixes through FO optimization. To coordinate the two processes, we introduce Pair-aware optimization, which retains multiple prefix candidates, conditionally refines their suffixes and selects among the resulting prefix-suffix pairs. We further investigate agent jailbreaking from a more general stochastic-generation perspective, where the same adversarial input can induce diverse reasoning trajectories and actions that a single greedy output cannot capture.
