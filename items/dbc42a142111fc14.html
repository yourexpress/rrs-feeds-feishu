Inference-Time Scaling in Agentic Hardware Verification Workflows: A Multi-Strategy Characterization Across Protocol Complexity

arXiv · 2026-11-18 · 论文PDF

Agentic 硬件验证工作流中的推理时扩展：多策略刻画

Inference-time scaling, allocating more compute during generation rather than pre-training, has demonstrated dramatic quality improvements for LLM reasoning tasks. Its behaviour in structured hardware verification code generation, where outputs must satisfy compiler correctness, protocol semantics, and functional coverage constraints simultaneously, is less understood. We present a systematic, simulator-grounded study of inference-time compute scaling for agentic UVM testbench synthesis targeting AMBA protocol bridges. Four inference strategies---zero-shot (\$0.04), raw-feedback iterative (\$0.81), structured inner-loop (\$0.95), and full dual-loop agentic synthesis (\$1.06)---are evaluated across four AMBA targets (Protocol Complexity Index 11.8--22.6) using the Protocol Verification Completeness (PVC) metric, functional coverage, and a six-class UVM error taxonomy (E1--E6). Our central finding is that structured diagnostic feedback acts as an inference-time compute multiplier: the structured inner loop yields roughly $15\times$ more PVC per inference dollar than raw EDA-tool feedback (marginal efficiency, S1$\to$S2 vs. S0$\to$S1).
