Neural operators offer fast surrogates for solving PDEs, but training them typically requires expensive input-solution pairs. Two solver-free alternatives include physics-informed learning and training on manufactured data. Physics-informed learning approximates solutions by minimizing PDE residuals. While the residual norm must be strong enough to control solution error, stronger norms increase mini-batch variance and hinder optimization. Manufactured data constructs input-solution pairs for learning by applying the PDE operator to artificial solutions. However, the resulting training distribution can be mutually singular with the target distribution, yielding poor accuracy on the target distribution. We introduce the Wasserstein Data Synthesis Pipeline (WaSP), which trains on manufactured input-solution pairs while gradually mitigating the distribution shift. WaSP synthesizes new pairs by applying the learned operator to a path of intermediate distributions converging to the target input distribution. Training proceeds in stages along this path.
