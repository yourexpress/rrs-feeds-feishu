Parameter-Efficient Adaptation of Self-supervised Vision Backbones Survives Distribution Shift in Radar Micro-Doppler HAR

Micro-Doppler radar enables contactless, privacy-preserving human activity recognition (HAR), but deployment robustness remains underexplored. We compared full fine-tuning, frozen probing, and parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) using subject-disjoint in-distribution cross-validation and few-shot cross-age, cross-orientation, and cross-dataset transfer. Full fine-tuning suffered negative cross-dataset transfer, while frozen features under-adapted on cross-orientation. Only PEFT avoided both failures. We paired DINOv3 with all-layer principal-singular-direction adaptation (PiSSA), to our knowledge the first application of a self-supervised image foundation backbone to radar micro-Doppler HAR. DINOv3-PiSSA ranked first among PEFT methods in every evaluated setting. Under our protocol, its Small variant matched the in-distribution accuracy of the prior Base-size PEFT state of the art using 4.5x fewer total parameters, with only 3% trainable. It surpassed a size-matched variant of that baseline in every evaluated setting, with transfer gains increasing with shift severity: 2.1, 6.6, and 8.9 percentage points, respectively.
