Efficient neural architecture search (NAS) requires accurate performance estimation under limited evaluation budgets. Existing zero-cost proxies (ZCPs) provide fast training-free signals, but each proxy usually captures only a partial property of an architecture, leading to unstable rankings across datasets and search spaces. In contrast, GNN-based predictors can improve accuracy, but often depend on labeled architectures, graph encoders, and costly representation learning, which makes them less suitable for extreme few-shot settings. We propose FENAS, a topology-agnostic few-shot prediction framework that turns complementary initialization priors into robust ranking signals. Its core component, CAST, is a feature pipeline that combines copula alignment and synergy transformation. CAST maps heterogeneous ZCPs into a compact feature manifold, enabling lightweight meta-learners to produce reliable rankings from scarce supervision. On NAS-Bench-201, with only 0.5% labeled samples, FENAS achieves a Kendall's Tau ($τ$) of 0.70, outperforming representative topology-based few-shot predictors in this low-label regime.
