The Tsetlin Machine (TM) is a logic-based machine learning model well suited to low-latency inference on resource-constrained edge devices. Because each clause is an AND over Boolean literals, both the literals and their associated Tsetlin Automaton (TA) actions can be represented at the bit level, enabling bitwise evaluation and an early exit that is triggered as soon as any included literal evaluates to zero, forcing the clause output to zero. In this work, we study how the two TM training hyperparameters, T and s, govern both classification accuracy and inference latency. Building on the inversely proportional T-s relationship along which accuracy is nearly constant, we find that inference latency is reduced primarily by increasing s; rather than exhaustively searching the (T, s) grid at high training cost, we propose a directed search strategy that treats accuracy as a constraint and advances toward larger s to reduce inference latency within a fixed evaluation budget.
