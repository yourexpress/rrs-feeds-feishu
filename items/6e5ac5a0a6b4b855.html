AllotNet: an Interpretable Mixture-of-Experts Choice Model for Moral Judgement in Humanitarian Aid Allocation, Validated against a Planted Synthetic Population

arXiv · 2026-11-17 · 论文PDF

AllotNet：可解释 MoE 选择模型用于人道主义援助分配

Official development assistance fell by 23.1 per cent in 2025, and the decision of who is helped first when there is not enough for everyone is moving into software. A leading published machine-learning targeting system excluded 53 per cent of eligible households as deployed. If the rule of distribution ends up in code, it should carry the judgement of many cultures, and the machine should execute while a person judges. AllotNet is an interpretable mixture-of-experts conditional logit for the allocation judgements elicited by Allot, a pre-registered six-language instrument: eight linear taste experts over seven ethical criteria, weighted by a gate over participant context, with a damped residual, an encoder for household intake, an outcome head and a budget-constrained planner that builds a review queue and never rejects anyone. A model of human judgement has no answer key on real data, so we wrote one first: a planted synthetic population of 5.6 million sessions and 104 million responses (98 million usable choices), generated by the production code path from a ground truth hidden from the loss.
