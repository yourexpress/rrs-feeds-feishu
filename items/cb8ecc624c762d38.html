Small differences between internal representations can shrink or grow as they pass through a Transformer layer, affecting how information is preserved and how sensitive the model is to perturbations. Yet it remains unclear when changes in attention weights can turn a representation difference that would otherwise shrink into one that grows. We develop a \textbf{Mixture-Gated Theory of Anisotropic Representation Dynamics} that separates self-attention into value transport, query gating, and key gating. Our analysis reveals how the geometry of attended representations constrains gating and how interactions between these mechanisms can reverse contraction induced by value transport. We formalize this phenomenon as gating-induced regime switching, where a representation difference contracts under fixed attention routing but expands under full attention. We derive a necessary and sufficient spectral criterion that certifies the existence or absence of switching and identifies switching directions without exhaustive search.
