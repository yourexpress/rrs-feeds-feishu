Eliciting the Recall Capacity of Linear Attention

arXiv · 2026-11-24 · 论文PDF

Linear attention models exhibit a disproportionate deficit relative to softmax attention in associative recall (AR) in language modeling. We investigate this gap through natural language evaluations, synthetic recall tasks, and targeted training interventions. Using a synthetic setting, we quantify the role of state-size in how many items can be recalled from memory and find a similar empirical scaling across the tested linear-attention architectures. Testing this relationship reveals that even a single layer at scale can learn to retrieve thousands of associations across long distances. Yet, standard language model training does not reliably teach a linear model to copy from context, whereas a comparable softmax model learns to do so. Considering this, we strengthen recall supervision during training through in-context sentence repetition and context-sensitive loss reweighting. Both methods improve recall-intensive benchmark performance and narrow the long-distance AR gap to the softmax baseline without changing the architecture, token budget, or number of optimization steps, at a slight cost to general language modeling.
