Capital Efficiency, Isolation, Risk, and Ruin in Generative AI Inference Serving Infrastructure

arXiv cs.DC · 2026-11-06 · 论文PDF

Generative AI serving is an extremely capital-intensive endeavor that demands rapid, step-change improvements in capital efficiency to prevent industry wide increases in the cost of capital from limiting growth plans. Keeping pace with the economic necessities will increasingly require infrastructure providers to confront operationally-inconvenient realities of production serving, including the extreme heterogeneity of generative AI request costs. We have explored workload isolation as a foundational, fleet-level lever for increasing capital efficiency. Isolation is the practice of intelligently grouping "like" requests before execution. Rather than exposing every replica to the full heterogeneous workload distribution, isolation deliberately reshapes the workload presented to the hardware by assigning requests with different cost characteristics to different portions of the fleet. Workload isolation on its own does not make expensive requests cheaper; it prevents a costly portion of the workload from unnecessarily determining the operating point and capital efficiency of the entire fleet.
