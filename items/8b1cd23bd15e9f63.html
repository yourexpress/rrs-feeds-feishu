ZipDrift: Generating Multimodal Short-Horizon MPC Costs with One-Step Drifting Models

arXiv · 2026-11-06 · 论文PDF

Short-horizon model predictive control (MPC) is fast but myopic. ZipMPC compensates by learning a context-dependent correction of the short-horizon cost that imitates a long-horizon expert. We observe that the optimal correction is often multimodal: when a slower opponent can be passed on either side, good corrections of opposite sign coexist, and the expert's choice between them hinges on tiny cost differences. Deterministic regression then averages modes or inherits inconsistent expert tie-breaking. We propose ZipDrift, which trains a one-step drifting generator over MPC cost corrections, tilted toward low closed-loop cost and learned only from cost evaluations of candidate corrections, without differentiating through the optimiser. A few generated candidates are ranked by a learned critic, and the model-based MPC then enforces dynamics and constraints. In a kinematic overtaking benchmark derived from ZipMPC, more than half of the contexts are bimodal and the manual short-horizon MPC crashes in 28% of them.
