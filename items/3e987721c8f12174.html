Routing the bus between a processor and its DRAM remains largely manual in printed circuit board (PCB) design, and published algorithms optimize proxies such as crossing or layer counts. We present a router that plans every lane of a two-layer BGA-to-BGA bus before any copper is laid, and carries the plan to design-rule-clean copper. Its core is an exact constraint-programming model that chooses, for all lanes at once, where each necessary crossing occurs, in what order -- by a rule over every triple of lanes -- and where each lane changes layer, with via room; for the ends it is given, it proves its plan optimal within the model. The model reads naturally as physics: worldlines, frustrated Ising spins and contact forces. A linear program places the lanes in millimeters; the constraints it must violate return to the model as cuts, and audit findings as negotiated congestion prices, until the plan passes an audit against the production router's rules or lays clean. The escapes are chosen by an estimate of the same objective, and differential pairs carry their polarity, with an automatic via crossover where the ends disagree.
