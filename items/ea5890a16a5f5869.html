Graph anomaly detection (GAD) aims to identify irregular entities in graph-structured data, yet heterogeneous anomaly patterns and distributional differences across graphs challenge generalizable detection. Existing training-free approaches avoid task-specific optimization but primarily rely on predefined numerical scoring rules, leaving the semantic interpretation of heterogeneous anomaly evidence largely unexplored. In this paper, we propose Jev-GAD, a training-free generalist graph anomaly detection framework that bridges heterogeneous numerical evidence and pretrained decision mechanisms. To address heterogeneous anomaly patterns, we construct complementary structural, attribute, and neighborhood evidence and transform heterogeneous statistics into graph-relative anomaly percentiles. To move beyond predefined numerical scoring rules, we leverage a single frozen pretrained language model for evidence-conditioned anomaly assessment.
