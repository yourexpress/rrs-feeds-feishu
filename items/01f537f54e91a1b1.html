Reconfigurable Intelligent Surfaces (RISs) enable programmable control of the wireless propagation environment through adjustable reflecting elements. A fundamental challenge in RIS-assisted communications is the optimization of discrete phase shifts to maximize the received signal power. While recently developed algorithms can obtain optimal solutions with linear complexity, their computational cost may still be excessive in applications requiring large-scale simulations, real-time adaptation, or repeated optimization over numerous channel realizations. This paper proposes a low-complexity discrete phase-shift optimization algorithm based on multiple reference directions within the feasible optimality sector. By evaluating candidate phase configurations generated from uniformly distributed reference directions, the proposed method systematically improves upon existing approximation approaches that rely on a fixed set of reference directions. The number of reference directions serves as a design parameter that enables a controllable trade-off between computational complexity and optimality.
