Aggregation Dispersion: An Information-Geometric Diagnostic of Oversmoothing in Graph Neural Networks

arXiv · 2026-11-22 · 论文PDF

Message-passing neural networks (MPNNs) learn node representations by iteratively aggregating information from neighbors, but stacking too many layers causes all representations to converge, an effect known as oversmoothing. We propose a geometric perspective on this phenomenon. At each depth $k$, the propagation operator assigns to every node $v$ a walk distribution $p_v^{(k)}$, a probability distribution over source nodes. These walk distributions form a point cloud on the probability simplex, whose contraction to a single point characterizes complete mixing of the propagation operator, and thus oversmoothing. To measure this contraction, we use the Fisher-Rao metric, the unique Riemannian metric on the simplex invariant up to scale under sufficient statistics, whose scaled chordal distance under the square-root embedding $p \mapsto \sqrt{p}$ is the Hellinger distance. The resulting diagnostic, the aggregation dispersion $D_k$, is the variance of the embedded point cloud on the unit sphere.
