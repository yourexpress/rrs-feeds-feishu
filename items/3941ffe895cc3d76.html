Heteroscedastic Gaussian Process Q-learning for Sample Efficient Experimentation

arXiv cs.LG · 2026-11-16 · 论文PDF

异方差高斯过程Q学习用于样本高效实验

Self-driving laboratories must learn to control a process from tens to hundreds of expensive experiments, too few for deep reinforcement learning (RL). We learn the Q-function directly with a Gaussian process (GP), which avoids learning the dynamics, and show that the GP posterior supplies the learning rate of Q-learning. If each Bellman target is treated as a noisy measurement of the Q-function, the learning rate that minimises the mean squared error is the Kalman gain, and applying it to every observed transition is equivalent to GP regression. Heteroscedastic Gaussian Process Q-learning (HGPQ) sets the noise variance of each target with a heteroscedastic GP, which learns how unreliable the targets are across the states and inputs. Targets from noisy operating conditions, or from regions where the value function has large gradients, then move the estimate less than targets from low-noise regions, so HGPQ learns quickly where the data are reliable without being misled where they are not.
