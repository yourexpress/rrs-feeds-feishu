See the Answer, Learn to Embed: Learning Universal Multimodal Embeddings from Hindsight

arXiv · 2026-11-24 · 论文PDF

Universal Multimodal Embedding (UME) models map heterogeneous multimodal inputs into a shared embedding space, where pairwise similarity supports diverse downstream tasks. Recent reasoning-driven UME approaches use intermediate reasoning to generate additional context for embedding, but often incur extra computation at inference. In this paper, we explore the matched candidate (i.e., the paired target) as an alternative source of context, asking whether a model can "see the answer" during training and "learn to embed" without it at inference. However, direct conditioning on this privileged context can introduce candidate-specific information unavailable from the query alone or cause the candidate-conditioned query representation to collapse toward the candidate embedding. To address this, we introduce Hindsight-Enhanced Multimodal Embeddings (HEME), which learns query-grounded hindsight representations from such candidate context. HEME first trains a hindsight teacher with a hindsight attention mask, mismatched-candidate conditioning, and query-token alignment to limit excessive reliance on the candidate.
