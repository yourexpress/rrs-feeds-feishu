Accurate short-term wind power forecasting is critical for grid stability and electricity trading. While transformer-based deep learning is state of the art, industry practice relies mostly on local models trained for individual assets. This study investigates whether a global temporal fusion transformer (TFT), trained jointly on a heterogeneous portfolio of wind farms across Germany, outperforms site-specific local models in 48-hour-ahead forecasting. Using numerical weather prediction (NWP) data from the regional ICON-D2 and the global ECMWF HRES models, we compare the global TFT against local models and a linear regression baseline. The global model leverages static covariates, such as farm age, altitude above sea level, hub height, rotor diameter and power curve parameters, to learn shared representations across sites. We evaluate on 100 synthetic wind farm profiles and a real portfolio of 11 German wind farms, across two seasonal test periods with intermediate retraining. The global TFT outperforms the local models for 87% of the synthetic and 77% of the real wind farms and clearly surpasses the linear baseline.
