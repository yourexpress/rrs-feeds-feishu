Queuing for Secrets: Model-Based Remote Timing Attacks

arXiv · 2026-11-23 · 论文PDF

Remote timing attacks exploit variations in response times to infer sensitive information about remote systems. Their effectiveness, however, is often limited by the difficulty of accurately modeling the stochastic nature of server-side response times, taking into account the intrinsic variability of the service time of a request and the impact of varying workloads on the observed response times. In this article, we propose a new stochastic framework for remote timing attacks based on queuing models and Bayesian statistics. We assume that the attacker knows the service time distributions of two classes of requests and that the information leakage occurs when a malicious observer can distinguish with high probability the class based on the observed response times in the presence of noise induced by exogenous workload. Our experimental evaluation on popular websites demonstrates the practical viability of the approach: our attack outperforms formal techniques based on deterministic service times and existing empirical techniques, while requiring less tuning and fewer hyper-parameters compared to previous formal approaches.
