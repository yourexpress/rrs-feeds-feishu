Self-Supervised Diffusion Models for Handwritten Signature Verification via Ruin Reconstruction

arXiv · 2026-11-06 · 论文PDF

Handwritten signature verification (HSV) is a critical biometric technique in legal and forensic authentication, whose efficacy hinges on the discriminability of signature representations. Current HSV methods predominantly adopt supervised learning to enhance representation quality, leaving self-supervised learning largely unexplored. We propose \emph{SigDiffuser}, a diffusion-based self-supervised framework for signature representation and verification. At its core, \emph{SigDiffuser} introduces a novel pretext task termed \emph{Reconstruction from Ruins (RFR)}, which deliberately ``ruins'' signatures through severe crumpling, obliterating their structural integrity and writing styles. A visual encoder is employed to capture crucial handwriting patterns from intact signature regions, guiding the denoiser to reconstruct the ``ruined'' portions and learning meaningful representations. Additionally, we introduce a \emph{pyramidal feature aggregation module (PFAM)} to bridge the visual encoder and denoiser, enhancing representation quality via hierarchical aggregation and modulation.
