One Vector or Many? Clustered Optimal-Transport Steering under Quantization

Activation steering changes what a language model generates by editing its hidden states at inference time. Its most common form adds one vector, the difference of class means (DiM), to every hidden state. CHaRS (concept heterogeneity-aware representation steering) uses optimal transport instead: an entropic plan pairs the clusters of the two classes, and each hidden state moves along a mixture of the cluster displacements, weighted toward its nearest clusters. We study this family of clustered optimal-transport maps for toxicity mitigation in Gemma-2-2B and Granite-3.1-2B, and ask what the plan adds to DiM and whether that survives INT8 and NF4 quantization. We prove an identity: whatever the plan, the displacements averaged with the cluster sizes as weights equal DiM, so a clustered map can differ from DiM only for tokens whose routing weights depart from the cluster sizes. In the CHaRS protocol they stay close, and CHaRS is statistically equivalent to Mean-AcT, which adds DiM at every layer, in all eight pre-specified equivalence tests.
