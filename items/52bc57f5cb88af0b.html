TSBPO: Tree-Structured Branching for Policy Optimization in Interactive Language Agents

arXiv · 2026-11-06 · 论文PDF

Terminal feedback in multi-turn agent training leaves both rollout allocation and local credit underdetermined. Dynamics-Predictive Sampling (DPS) [9] provides a task-level predictive sampling reference, but interactive agents also need to choose which visited states merit further continuations. We introduce TSBPO with two method components. A lightweight, fine-grained Root/Branch budget allocator distributes factual rollouts across tasks and additional suffixes across replayable states using likelihood-fitted estimates of terminal-outcome contrast. On the resulting physical rollout trees, a tree-aware step-level credit assignment algorithm compares immediate sibling segments and assigns their local outcome contrasts to the turns they contain. Exact task/observation groups provide a complementary step term. The allocator selects what to sample; observed outcomes determine the subsequent credit. With a Qwen2.5-1.5B actor, fixed checkpoints attain 97.79% seen and 96.64% unseen stochastic success on ALFWorld, and an 81.07% per-episode success rate over six stochastic episodes for each of the 500 WebShop test goals.
