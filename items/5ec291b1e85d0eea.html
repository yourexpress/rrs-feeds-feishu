Adapting Multi-Stage Distributed Real-Time Systems Design to Vehicular Edge Computing

arXiv cs.DC · 2026-11-09 · 论文PDF

Vehicular Edge Computing (VEC) aims to support computation-intensive applications in intelligent transportation systems while meeting stringent latency requirements. When processed remotely, applications require access to different resource types in a specific sequence, forming a multi-stage processing pipeline. This structure closely resembles multi-stage distributed applications executing across heterogeneous resources, making established multi-stage distributed real-time scheduling techniques well suited for modeling VEC systems. The resulting model accommodates diverse resource configurations and access patterns. Building on this model, we propose a joint mapping and scheduling approach that accounts for vehicle mobility and real-time constraints. The proposed heuristic extends optimal priority assignment for multi-stage systems to the VEC setting, to construct feasible schedules while minimizing local-device energy consumption and ensuring that offloaded jobs meet their deadlines. Extensive simulations show that the proposed approach consistently outperforms both a baseline based on the same delay-composition framework and a deadline decomposition-based approach.
