PosteriorBench: Evaluating Behavior Posterior Sampling in Language Models

Rare language model behaviors can be difficult to study because obtaining them through ordinary sampling can require a very large number of samples. This motivates the behavior posterior sampling problem: given a language model and a behavior of interest, is there an algorithm that efficiently samples from the model's distribution over transcripts that exhibit this behavior? We introduce PosteriorBench, a benchmark for comparing algorithms by their compute cost and by how close their samples are to the true posterior in forward and reverse KL divergence. PosteriorBench contains 21 tasks on Qwen3.5-0.8B with behavior probabilities from $10^{-5}$ to $10^{-1}$, each with exact posterior samples from a corpus of one billion labeled responses. We find that no method we evaluate is both efficient and faithful: simple methods like prompting are cheap but plateau at a high KL divergence from the posterior, fine-tuning on posterior samples is often cost-prohibitive, and several training methods from prior work do not perform better.
