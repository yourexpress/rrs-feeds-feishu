CSI-JEPA: Towards Foundation Representations for Ubiquitous Wi-Fi Sensing with Minimal Supervision

arXiv · 2026-11-06 · 论文PDF

Channel state information (CSI) provides a widely available sensing modality for human and environment perception, but existing CSI sensing models usually rely on task-specific supervised training and require substantial labeled data for each task, device, user, or environment. This limits their scalability in practical deployments where unlabeled CSI is abundant but labeled data is costly to collect. In this paper, we present CSI-JEPA, a self-supervised predictive representation learning framework for label-efficient and transferable Wi-Fi sensing. CSI-JEPA learns reusable temporal-spectral representations from unlabeled CSI samples by predicting latent features of masked channel regions from visible context. To better match the physical structure of CSI, it jointly tokenizes CSI along the time and subcarrier dimensions and introduces a channel variation-aware masking strategy that prioritizes regions with stronger local channel dynamics. After pretraining, the encoder is frozen and adapted to downstream sensing tasks using lightweight task-specific adapters.
