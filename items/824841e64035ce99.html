The Race Is Decided Early! A Kernel Law Predicts Lock-In in Majority-Vote Test-Time RL

arXiv cs.LG · 2026-11-08 · 论文PDF

Test-time reinforcement learning with majority-vote rewards trains a language model on unlabeled questions by rewarding the samples that agree with its most frequent answer. During training, each question locks into one answer, on hard questions often a wrong one, but which answer it locks into is known only after training. In this paper, we show that this outcome is decided early, in a short race driven by sampling noise between the answers that are close at the start, and we derive the probability of each outcome. With a separate logit for each answer, the runner-up wins with a normal tail probability of the initial gap, scaled by an explicit noise constant. In a network, however, the answer logits depend on shared parameters, and through the kernel of these parameters the updates of the trailing answers also change the gap of the two leaders. This spillover shifts the boundary between the starts won by each leader, and a closed form computes the shifted probability from a kernel probe and the start logits.
