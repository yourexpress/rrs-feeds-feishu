Zuse: A Decision Model for European Languages

arXiv · 2026-11-06 · 论文PDF / 每日精选

面向 27 种欧洲语言的决策模型 Zuse（4B/9B/27B）

Decision models read a document and return a probability for each of a fixed set of options instead of generating text. Several appeared in 2026, but none has published results outside English. We describe Zuse, three decision models (4B, 9B and 27B) that train a low-rank adapter and a pointer head on frozen open-weight bases, targeting 27 European languages, and use them to study how this design behaves across languages and tasks. On a broad European suite of 11 sources excluded from training, covering 9 task groups and 27 languages, a development set that also guided model selection, the 27B model reaches 87.5% accuracy with a pooled expected calibration error of 0.016, and 0.035 when weighted per source. Post-hoc analyses of these single-seed results give three findings. First, miscalibration is structured by task rather than language: on parallel reading and topic items in 26 languages the confidence gap varies about as much as sampling noise, while across sources it spans -0.07 to +0.09, with knowledge tasks overconfident and acceptability judgements underconfident at all three sizes, so one fitted temperature cannot correct both.
