Lower Bounds for the Distributed Computation of Degeneracy and k-Core

arXiv cs.DC · 2026-11-16 · 论文PDF

分布式计算degeneracy与k-core的匹配下界

The degeneracy of a graph $G$ is the maximum integer $k$ such that $G$ has a non-empty $k$-core, where the $k$-core is the largest induced subgraph with minimum degree $k$. Computing the $k$-core of a graph $G$ is P-complete. In the decentralized setting, the sequential peeling algorithm can be distributed so that the core value of every node, i.e., the largest integer $k$ for which the node belongs to the $k$-core, is computed in $O(n)$ rounds in $n$-node networks, where adjacent nodes exchange only $O(\log n)$ bits at each round, i.e., in the standard CONGEST model. However, while $Ω(D)$ rounds is a trivial lower bound for these problems in networks of diameter $D$, this does not rule out the existence of an algorithm running in $O(D)+o(n)$ rounds in CONGEST, as it is the case for many very important graph problems, including MST. We show that it is unlikely that such an algorithm exists, or, if it does exist, then it requires $\tildeΩ(\sqrt{n})$ rounds.
