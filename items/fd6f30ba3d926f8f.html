Memory Admission and Result-Retention Sizing in an RV32IMC FPGA Soft Processor

arXiv (cs.AR) · 2026-11-02 · 论文PDF

In FPGA soft processors with shared backing block RAM, memory admission can stall a multiplication after its product has been prepared. We study how admission timing changes result retention in PaceRV, our board-tested five-stage RV32IMC core, using fixed binaries and matched dynamic instructions. Offering data requests one edge earlier reduces geometric-mean Embench cycles by 18.2% and retention opportunities from 375,063 to 4,373. Admission and retention cycle gains are conditional and non-additive. Under slower reads, a 99% capture target selects an 18-edge validity window with registered requests and 28 edges with early requests, although no multiplication matched within both measurement intervals waits longer. A predeclared sizing rule predicts opportunity recovery exactly in all finite tests, including kernels excluded from model development. Independent timing analysis closely estimates execution-cycle savings. The results support choosing admission first, then deciding whether to extend validity and sizing it against an explicit capture or missed-opportunity objective.
