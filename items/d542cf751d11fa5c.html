AMARA: Compiler-Driven Accelerator Architecture Design for Graphical Probabilistic Sampling

arXiv cs.AR · 2026-11-11 · 论文PDF

Sampling methods are fundamental primitives for training and inference on probabilistic models. However, practical deployment of sampling algorithms such as Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) remains difficult on general-purpose processors. Existing hardware accelerators improve sampling efficiency, but they are typically specialized for a narrow class of models, graph topologies, or update schedules, making it difficult to support the diverse workloads used in real applications. This paper presents AMARA, an end-to-end compiler-driven accelerator framework for probabilistic sampling on arbitrary probabilistic graphical models. AMARA introduces MCMC-IR, a domain-specific intermediate representation that separates local factorized computation from global update dependencies. Based on this representation, the compiler performs architecture-aware partitioning, scheduling, and memory-access optimization to expose parallelism and reduce irregular graph communication. The optimized program is lowered to a compact ISA and executed on a programmable ASIC accelerator with specialized compute, sampling, and memory support.
