Firms using large language models under fixed budgets must decide which model to call and whether to retry a failed task. Because a call's charge is revealed only after completion, one long response can exhaust the remaining budget and leave later tasks unserved. Our objective is to maximize the number of tasks solved before service stops. We develop a contextual bandit policy, ADP-HT, which learns each model's task-specific success probability and constructs conservative cost estimates from coarsened response-length labels on a quantile grid with $2^K$ intervals. The policy weighs predicted success against these costs, adjusting the cost penalty to realized spending and the budget available per remaining task. Under calibration, local regularity, and grid-coverage conditions, we bound the excess allowance in the policy's conservative cost estimates. At a fixed sample size, increasing $K$ reduces the discretization term but increases the estimation allowance. At the tightest tested budgets, ADP-HT solves more than twice as many MATH500 and GPQA tasks as the best implemented baseline.
