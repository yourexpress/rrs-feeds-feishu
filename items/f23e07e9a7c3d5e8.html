Within the broader landscape of artificial intelligence architectures, this paper introduces a compact, edge-centric neural framework that resolves the computational bottlenecks of modern edge-activated paradigms like Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks (KANs) while retaining their expressiveness over traditional Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs). While KANs demonstrate remarkable representational capacity by placing learnable univariate functions on connection edges rather than hidden nodes, their reliance on non-local B-splines introduces severe optimization hurdles, high memory overhead, and poor hardware scaling. To bridge this gap, we propose the Parametric Tanh Connection Function, a smooth, bounded architectural primitive defined as $a_{\tanh}(x;δ,α,β,γ) = δ+ α\tanh(βx + γ)$. This formulation unifies offset, amplitude, local slope, and horizontal displacement into a lightweight, 4-parameter differentiable mapping optimized edge-by-edge via backpropagation.
