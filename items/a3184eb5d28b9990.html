Real-world deployment of personalized federated learning (PFL) requires adapting to heterogeneous clients while preserving recognition performance beyond local distributions. However, high local accuracy can conceal failures on locally rare or absent classes. Effective personalization should therefore strengthen global recognition while raising the personalization floor. We propose an evaluation protocol that complements local accuracy with global, rare-class, and unseen-class evaluations to reveal generalization obscured by local scores, and assesses personalization floors among the worst-performing clients to expose weaknesses hidden by aggregate performance.
