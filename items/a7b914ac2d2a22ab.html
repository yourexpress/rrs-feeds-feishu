Small Learned Projections for Semantic-Reversal-Aware Retrieval with Spectral Acceleration: A Four-Domain Empirical Study

arXiv · 2026-11-23 · 论文PDF

Dense-embedding retrievers can fail adversarially: when a query is answered by a document A and reversed by a near-copy B that differs from A by a small meaning-changing edit (a "trap"), raw cosine over E5-Large-v2 places B in the top-10 for 82-100% of queries at a pool of 5,000. We study two things. First, a small learned projection W: R^1024 -> R^d (d in {11, 32}), trained by a deterministic recipe on a declared set of cases, that suppresses the trap: for the declared cases, HN@10 = 0.0000 (no trap in the top-10) in all twelve (domain, pool-size) settings across four independent domains (legal, medical, patent, financial) and pools up to 100,000, while the correct paraphrase is retained for 93-99% of cases, under exact nearest-neighbour search. Second, a boundary-conditioned accelerator that serves the projection at that scale: an acceleration of spectral graph retrieval (SpectRAG) that replaces a dense eigendecomposition with a boundary-value solve, holding HN@10 = 0.0000 at n = 100,000 in every domain and retrieving the ~300-query suite in 0.6-1.1 s (~2-4 ms per query on average).
