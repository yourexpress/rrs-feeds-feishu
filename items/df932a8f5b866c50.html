Autoregressive transformers trained on tokenized tabular event streams are a simple and strong baseline for predicting financial events such as fraud. Yet institutions do not act on probabilities; they act on decisions whose consequences are asymmetric and heteroscedastic: the cost of a missed high-value fraud and that of a wrongly declined small purchase are not the same. The standard bridge is post-hoc Bayes-optimal thresholding of calibrated probabilities under a cost matrix. We ask whether fine-tuning the model to the decision objective can do better. We fine-tune the same pretrained model so that its restricted softmax over label tokens (already a distribution over business actions) maximizes expected utility under an institution-specified, amount-scaled cost matrix. Because the reward is a known function of (action, outcome, amount) and the outcome is observed in the data, the expected-utility objective and its gradient are available in closed form. This is example-dependent cost-sensitive learning whose per-example loss is linear in the predicted probability, with no sampling, critics, or advantage estimation.
