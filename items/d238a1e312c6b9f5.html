Meta shares ten health research manuscripts built with its Muse AI platform

The studies drew on electronic health records from 4.95 million patients, with AI handling the grunt work and human experts checking it

On October 14, 2026, Meta unveiled ten research manuscripts produced with help from its Muse AI platform. All ten draw on electronic health records, and all ten were carried out with human experts supervising the work.

Meta’s system tries to automate research preparation. The work centers on Muse Spark, a component of the broader Muse platform, which handles four core tasks: identifying patient cohorts, running statistical analysis, retrieving relevant literature, and drafting manuscripts.

The workflow relies on multi-agent AI models. That means several AI components split up the job, each handling a different piece, while human experts oversee the process.

The underlying dataset covers detailed health information on 4.95 million patients, drawn from an EHR release in June 2026. Within that pool, 156,051 patients carry breast cancer codes.

One of the ten studies chased a genuinely obscure target: Cladophialophora bantiana, a rare fungal pathogen. The project was prompted by a report in the Morbidity and Mortality Weekly Report, or MMWR, which flagged a rise in cases.
