Real-world tables are often heterogeneous, combining numerical features with free-form string columns. Table foundation models (TFMs) excel on numerical tables but require strings to be encoded numerically, while large language models (LLMs) hold semantic knowledge but are not designed for tabular predictions. Most existing bridges use LLMs as plug-in encoders for cell strings, yet how to extract representations effectively for tabular learning remains underexplored. We show that the interface between LLMs and tables matter as much as the LLM itself. Applied cell-wise, larger LLMs do not give better features. With our interface, the same LLMs improve steadily with size. TILE (Table-Interfaced LLM Embeddings) combines four ingredients: row serialization to provide cross-column context, intermediate-layer extraction to avoid layers specialized for next-token prediction, columnar pooling to remove mean pooling's length bias, and by-pass features to route numbers and categories past the LLM.
