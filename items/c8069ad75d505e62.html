A Theoretical Reduction From Membership Inference to Reconstruction Attacks Against Learners

arXiv cs.LG · 2026-11-08 · 论文PDF

We study reconstruction attacks, which recover training data from a learned model. In our adversarial model, the adversary designs a hard instance, drawn from a parameterized family of data distributions, and is given the parameter used to generate it. Our main contribution is a general reduction showing that, in this model, membership inference (determining whether a given datapoint was used for training) implies reconstruction: for a training set of size $n$, a membership test with recall at least $q(n)$ and false-positive rate $O(\delta^2/n^2)$ yields a reconstruction algorithm that, with probability $1 - O(\delta)$, outputs only training points, and recovers $\Omega(n \cdot q(n))$ of them in expectation. This reduction applies to any learning algorithm. For stochastic convex optimization (SCO), we make the reduction computationally efficient over exponentially large domains via structured distributions whose support is partitioned into polynomially many categories of exponentially many elements each, so that reconstruction iterates over categories rather than the full support.
