Which Objects Stay? Learning Hierarchical Persistence Beliefs for Lifelong Open-Vocabulary Object Maps

arXiv · 2026-11-06 · 论文PDF

Lifelong object maps must estimate whether an object remains at its previously observed location after it leaves view. Existing persistence filters rely on class-based survival priors, making them difficult to apply reliably in open-vocabulary settings. We introduce HiPer, a hierarchical persistence filter that derives persistence beliefs directly from data rather than semantic categories. For a new object, HiPer retrieves visually and geometrically similar training instances and estimates an initial survival rate from their observed histories, without requiring class labels. This estimate initializes a conjugate Beta belief over the object's survival probability, which is updated in closed form using detections and missed observations under Rosen's persistence-filter framework. When an object is re-identified at a new location, its persistence belief is transferred with the object, enabling the map to learn object-specific mobility over time. Experiments on 120 held-out simulated sequences show that HiPer consistently achieves the best performance among competing methods.
