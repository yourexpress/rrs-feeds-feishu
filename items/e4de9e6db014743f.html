Power Setpoint Tracking for Large-Scale EV Charging: Transformer-Constrained Charging Policy Learning with Self-Tuning Constraint Prices

In power setpoint tracking (PST), an operator follows a purchased charging profile without overloading shared transformers or undercharging vehicles. PST is cast as a constrained Markov decision process that minimizes tracking error subject to overload and mean user-satisfaction constraints. Transformer-Constrained Charging Policy Learning (TC-CPL) extends soft actor-critic with proportional-integral Lagrange-multiplier updates from measured violations, a residual on the incumbent dispatcher that is warm-started from its demonstrations, and a transformer-aware encoder shared by all ports. It observes no departure times or states of charge. Simulations (five seeds, 100 paired days) compare it with the incumbent, Round-Robin. On a 600-port, 122-substation district, raw overload falls from 3.59 to 0.0017 kWh/day (overloaded seed-days from 77% to 0.4%), and absolute tracking error falls by 27%. Delivered energy rises by 16%, and satisfaction rises from 0.959 to 0.989. On a 42-kW transformer with household load, overload falls from 1.86 to 0.0014 kWh/day and satisfaction rises from 0.974 to 0.985, but absolute tracking error is 22% higher.
