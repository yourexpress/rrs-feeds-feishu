The rapid growth of large loads, especially data centers driven by artificial intelligence, is creating unprecedented demands on the North American electric grid. Data centers now account for nearly 80% of large load interconnection requests in the Western Interconnection, and U.S. electricity consumption from data centers is projected to reach 325-580 TWh by 2028. Despite this urgency, technical interconnection requirements for large loads have not yet matured and still contain several gaps. This paper presents a comparative analysis of large load interconnection requirements across eleven U.S. entities, including AEP, Ameren, CAISO, Dominion, Duke Energy, ERCOT, LIPA, MISO, PJM, Southern Company, and SPP, under seven technical categories: large load definition, load data and modeling, fault ride-through, load curtailment and shedding, dispatchability, power quality, and reactive power control. The analysis reveals significant variation in both the scope and specificity of requirements.
