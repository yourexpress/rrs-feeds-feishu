Policy controllers must determine which workloads match rules spanning several label dimensions. Hash sets, compressed bitmaps, and GPU set-processing primitives address the underlying set operations. However, intermediate results, repeated scope evaluations, dependent policy updates, and host transfers can dominate the cost of a heterogeneous batch. We study this problem as batched intersection-of-unions (IoU). Our design exploits independent scope--word tasks, reusable endpoint expressions, and policy dependencies. The challenges are preserving wildcard, empty-group, and exclusion semantics, recognizing equal scopes, and retaining unchanged recipients affected by source edits. BitPCE addresses them with register-resident GPU evaluation, canonical scope compilation, and dependency-complete candidate selection under a stated rendering contract. IP-only changes reuse cached memberships. We establish correctness and word-work bounds. On a four-vCPU A10G host, resident GPU execution is 3.46--36.60$\times$ faster than the best tested native CPU path, including CRoaring, and 1.14--2.22$\times$ faster than an adapted operand-parallel GPU reduction.
