Joint SLA Admission and Risk Allocation for Cloud Resource Provisioning

arXiv · 2026-11-20 · 论文PDF

云资源供给中 SLA 接纳与风险分配的联合决策

Cloud controllers must reserve enough CPU and memory to meet demand without leaving too much capacity idle. Under a shared service-level agreement (SLA), a shortage of CPU or memory (considered as resource) counts toward the same limit. Dividing the allowed shortage risk between them changes how much extra capacity each need. We study this choice when forecast-error histories are short or correlated over time. Our controller compares different approaches to split the allowed shortage risk between resources. For each split, the controller uses historical forecast errors to determine how much extra capacity to reserve. It checks whether the history is enough to support that split and its consistency across different parts of the history. Among the splits that pass, it chooses the one with the lowest combined cost of shortages and idle capacity. Otherwise, the controller continues serving the workload with a calibrated fallback. In our simulation, we set a joint SLA target of sufficient CPU and memory in at least 95% of time slots and used 192 hours of historical forecast errors to structure the controller.
