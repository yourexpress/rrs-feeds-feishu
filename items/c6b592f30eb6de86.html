Improving Hamiltonian Learning through Local Energy Decomposition

arXiv (cs.LG) · 2026-11-02 · 论文PDF

Hamiltonian neural networks provide a principled framework for learning conservative dynamics. A scalar Hamiltonian is parameterized by a neural network and the vector field is derived using Hamilton's equations. However, preserving Hamiltonian structure does not guarantee that the energy function itself is represented accurately. When modeled by a conventional multilayer perceptron, the Hamiltonian inherits the spectral bias of coordinate-based neural representations, favoring smooth over fine-scale structure. These fine-scale errors can be amplified by differentiation when computing the dynamics. To address this limitation, we introduce Local Multiscale Hamiltonian Neural Network (LM-HNN), which restructures the Hamiltonian according to its physical interaction structure. LM-HNN decomposes the energy into kinetic and potential components and represents them as sums of shared local functions over multiple interaction neighborhoods. This replaces a single high-dimensional approximation problem with lower-dimensional, reusable interaction laws, making fine-scale energy structure easier to resolve while preserving Hamiltonian dynamics.
