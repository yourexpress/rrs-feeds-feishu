When using a large language model (LLM) to simulate a user population, how should we combine individual behavioral histories to predict the fraction of users who take a particular action? We study this question in e-commerce, predicting observed product-level conversion rates from browsing sessions without task-specific model training. We vary two design choices, each involving a bias-variance tradeoff. Summarizing a product's sessions before inference reduces cost but can discard predictive detail, adding bias, whereas predicting outcomes for individual sessions and averaging afterward preserves that detail but requires many calls to reduce variance. Evaluating products together in one prompt can help the model infer what is typical in the setting, reducing bias, but also makes each prediction depend on which other products appear alongside it, adding noise. We evaluate both choices across five LLMs and two datasets. Our main finding is that predictions from individual sessions outperform predictions from summaries when products are evaluated separately, but summaries lead when products are evaluated together.
