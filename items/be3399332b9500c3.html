Early stopping is commonly used to prevent fitting label noise from degrading generalization. Does this mean that, when a positive fraction of the training labels is intrinsically unpredictable, the best test error must occur before interpolation? Experiments on a CIFAR10 classification task with randomly labeled examples motivate the opposite possibility: although fixed-feature and shallow models exhibit late-time deterioration of the test error, the need for early stopping essentially disappears in larger, deeper convolutional networks even after the random labels have all been classified correctly. We determine when late-time deterioration disappears entirely in a solvable setting. For kernel gradient flow on a synthetic noisy classification task, we derive sharp high-dimensional learning curves and obtain a phase diagram controlled by the balance between the linear kernel component that learns the shared relation between inputs and labels and the higher-order components that enable fitting the label noise.
