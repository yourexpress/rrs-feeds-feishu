The penetration-resistant design of concrete structures requires the spatiotemporal evolution of the stress field within the target, whereas in design only the strength grade, the geometry and the projectile parameters are known, and the aggregate arrangement is not. Empirical formulae and scalar-output surrogates serve design screening, and mesoscale finite element models resolve the aggregate-scale response, but one solution cannot cover random aggregate samples at scale, and no framework links mesoscale heterogeneity to field-level prediction. A physics-coupled graph neural operator (PC-GNO) is proposed, which learns in function space the evolution of the mid-plane six-component stress tensor field. The training data are generated by full-scale, aggregate-resolved finite element models and comprise 359 cases at the impact velocities of 100, 135 and 165 m/s, corresponding to compressive strengths of 14, 23 and 48 MPa; the data source is verified by laboratory measurements. The coupling strength of message passing is constructed from the impedance contrast across the material interface, and the update is constrained by spectral normalization.
