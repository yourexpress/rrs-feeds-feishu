From Last-Layer to Cross-Layer: One-Shot Joint Classifier Reconstruction for Federated Learning

Federated learning (FL) enables collaborative model training while preserving data privacy. However, non-independent and identically distributed (non-IID) data inevitably introduces classifier bias, leading to significant performance degradation. Existing studies primarily focus on calibrating or reconstructing the classifier after FL training to alleviate this bias. We find that the last-layer representations of the global model are often suboptimal for establishing a global decision boundary. Instead, classifiers constructed based on intermediate-layer representations can achieve superior performance. This finding provides a critical insight into overcoming the performance bottleneck inherent in existing paradigms. Nevertheless, identifying the optimal layer without auxiliary datasets or prior knowledge remains a formidable challenge. In this paper, we propose FLARE (Federated Cross-Layer Joint ClAssifier REconstruction) which rethinks classifier reconstruction from a cross-layer perspective and generalizes ridge regression to jointly exploit cross-layer representations, thereby surpassing the performance of any individual layer.
