TRIAGE: Reliability- and Load-Conditioned Escalation for Two-Tier LLM Serving with a Semantic Evidence Judge

arXiv (cs.LG) · 2026-10-31 · 论文PDF

Two-tier LLM serving escalates selected requests from a small model to a large one, but prompt-based routers cannot see whether retrieved evidence supports an answer, and they treat the GPU cost of an escalation as constant. We study TRIAGE, which conditions escalation on a post-generation reliability state and on a load-dependent GPU price, and we report both what held and what did not on one A40 serving Qwen2.5-7B and 32B (AWQ) under VLLM. Escalation cost rose 21.9x from 1 to 10 requests/s but showed no monotone dependence on KV-cache headroom, and a pre-registered claim that the reliability state detects corrupted evidence (AUROC>=0.70) was falsified at 0.571, although any prompt-only router scores exactly 0.500 by construction. Post hoc, a semantic evidence judge raised detection to 0.846 (0.926 on never-served items) and predicted wrong small-model answers at AUROC 0.90-0.96 on RGB and three RouterBench model pairs; the resulting cascade stayed within about 3 pp of GPT-4 at 79.5% lower API cost for GPT-3.5->GPT-4, yet fell below random routing for open-model pairs with 2-3x price ratios.
