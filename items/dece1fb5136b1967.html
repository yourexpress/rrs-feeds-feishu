Financial transaction networks are naturally represented as multigraphs, where repeated transactions between the same pair of accounts are modeled as distinct edges. Recently proposed multigraph neural network architectures leverage this structure explicitly to achieve a strong performance on anti-money laundering (AML) tasks. However, their robustness to adversaries that manipulate transaction structures to evade detection remains largely unexplored. We present the first scalable framework for structural attacks and adversarial training of multigraph neural networks using node-edge incidence matrices, adapting projected gradient descent and projected randomized block coordinate descent methods to optimize structural perturbations over such matrices. We further introduce a cycle-based regularizer that penalizes changes in the global counts of directed 2- and 3-cycles, providing a structural measure of attack plausibility.
