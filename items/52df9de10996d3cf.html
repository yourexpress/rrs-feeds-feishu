Reliable deployment of LLM agents requires grounding diverse user expressions in their environments. Although RL-finetuned smaller large language model (sLLM) agents rival larger prompted LLMs on embodied tasks, their robustness to instruction paraphrases remains unclear. We introduce ALFWorld-Para to evaluate this robustness under controlled paraphrases, revealing 21-52% declines in success rates (SRs), predominantly driven by object synonyms. Despite retaining knowledge of synonym referents, agents often fail to take the corresponding objects even after observing them, exposing a gap between referential knowledge and object binding. Partial observability further complicates binding, as referents may remain unseen or have already been encountered under different labels. To address this challenge, we propose BINDIntent, a training-free framework that probabilistically binds referring expressions to observed labels using semantic proximity and estimated persistence against unseen candidates.
