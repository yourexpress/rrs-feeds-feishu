When Do Summaries Become Instructions? Unsupported Policy in LLM Agents' Self-Written Handoff Summaries

arXiv · 2026-11-06 · 论文PDF

Long-running LLM agents compact their context, replacing most history with a summary. We ask whether handoff summaries state permissions or requirements the record does not support, whether such sentences change what the next instance (the successor) does, and where they come from. Synthetic tool-use tasks carry a ground-truth permission ledger; same-snapshot replay reruns the successor with those whole sentences replaced by neutral filler. Two AI readers, reading only rule-flagged held-out summaries, confirmed unsupported restrictions in 1.2% and 2.4% of all readable summaries from two open-weight models, and unsupported authorizations in 0.1% and 1.3%. For restrictions, a pre-specified replay test was not significant (p = 0.58); a follow-up, planned after it, found a fragile effect (p = 0.027). Neutralizing false approvals cut unauthorized actions from 24 to 7 of 120 held-out runs (post hoc; later set: 17 to 11 of 48). Most development restrictions followed the agent's earlier reasoning that an action needs approval (post hoc).
