Benchmarking in reinforcement learning is usually relative: algorithms are ranked by returns on environments with unknown optima. Distance to optimality remains unobservable, while conclusions may depend on reward design, stochasticity, hyperparameters, environment selection, and simulator details. We develop a ground-truth-first framework based on converse optimality, constructing each task with a certified optimal value function and policy withheld from learners and used only for evaluation. The framework unifies two modes: sensitivity benchmarking samples certified environments from continuous-time generators to assess robustness to task variation, while oracle-referenced comparison uses native discrete-time generators to evaluate policies via optimality gap, regret, and policy error. Continuous-time models support physically interpretable sensitivity studies but introduce dependence on the simulator step ($Δt$) and numerical scheme. Native discrete-time models align the environment, oracle, and learner on the same transition law, eliminating external integration choices as a confounder.
