A Per-Step Load-Balancing Engine for Expert-Parallel MoE Serving

arXiv cs.AR · 2026-11-08 · 论文PDF

In expert-parallel Mixture-of-Experts serving, a layer finishes only when the most loaded GPU of its group does. Token routing shifts at every decode step and concentrates on a few experts, so most of the group's reserved GPU time waits on stragglers. Recovering that time means re-deciding, at every layer of every decode step, how each replicated expert's tokens divide between its copies. The exact linear-program solvers that make this decision optimally take longer than many of the layers they serve, and their working set outgrows GPU shared memory at deployment scale. We introduce Relief, a bidirectional pairwise relaxation that makes the linear program unnecessary, coming within a fraction of a point of the per-step optimum in a few passes of integer halving over the token counts. We build and measure three deployments, CUDA kernels on the serving GPU, the same verified engine on an FPGA over PCIe, and a fixed-function block beside the accelerator. On decode routing from three open MoE families, Relief holds the balance near the per-step optimum at every group size and layer time measured.
