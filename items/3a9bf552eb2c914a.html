SPIRE: Task-agnostic embodied continual learning through autonomous situation inference and causal motor-primitive reuse

arXiv cs.LG · 2026-11-10 · 论文PDF

Embodied continual learning requires controllers to infer changing situations, retain prior behavior and reuse motor skills without task labels or known boundaries. The challenge is deciding when to recover earlier behavior and which skills can support new learning. We introduce Situation-guided Primitive Integration for Recurrent Embodiment (SPIRE), a recurrent-spiking system that infers latent contexts from action-outcome evidence and learns context-specific motor actors online. Mature actors become protected sources that combine with a target-context residual to drive recurrent-spiking motor populations. Across coordinate, three-joint and hidden-force control, SPIRE maintained control, autonomously inferred situations, retained prior behavior and accelerated acquisition of an unseen source composition, reducing mean interactions to criterion by approximately 40% relative to matched fresh controllers. Rewinding compatible source actors impaired early acquisition in every matched intervention, demonstrating causal reuse.
