Large-language-model register-transfer-level (RTL) generation has concentrated on how to train the model: fine-tuning, continued pre-training, and reinforcement learning from a verifier. We argue this is the second-order question. On a bounded, formally specifiable task of generating single-error-correcting, double-error-detecting (SECDED) ECC memory wrappers, we hold the task, data, and correctness oracle fixed and vary two axes independently: the training regime (seven settings) and the output representation (four levels, from fully expanded flat Verilog to an eight-field configuration expanded by a deterministic generator). Because a reference generator exists, correctness is decided by formal equivalence over a design space of approximately ten million points, rather than sampled from a hand-written testbench. Two results follow. First, representation dominates: every representation close to raw RTL reaches a pass rate of at most 0.17, while the configuration reaches 0.83 with no training at all.
