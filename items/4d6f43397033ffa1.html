Machine-Learning-Assisted Hybrid Cooling of a Raspberry Pi 5

arXiv · 2026-11-24 · 论文PDF

Thermal management is a critical challenge in compact computing platforms operating under sustained high computational loads. Elevated processor temperatures can lead to thermal throttling, degraded performance, and reduced reliability. This study develops and experimentally evaluates a smart hybrid heat sink that integrates passive thermal energy storage with active cooling for sustained device operation. The proposed design comprises an aluminum cavity containing paraffin wax as a phase change material (PCM) embedded within a copper foam and integrated with an adaptively controlled fan. Fan operation is guided by a Random Forest regression model trained on experimentally measured CPU temperature data to predict future CPU temperatures. The predicted temperature is then used to support the adaptive fan control strategy. The heat sink is mounted directly on the CPU of a Raspberry Pi 5 and experimentally evaluated under an extreme CPU workload of approximately 99 percent CPU utilization. The performance of the proposed hybrid system is compared against two benchmark scenarios: passive cooling using PCM embedded in copper foam and a commercial Raspberry Pi 5 active cooler.
