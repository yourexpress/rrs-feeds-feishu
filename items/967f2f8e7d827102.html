Fine-tuning large language models at scale is increasingly limited by the memory cost of backpropagation. Zeroth-Order (ZO) optimizers reduce this burden by relying only on forward function evaluations, but they still exhibit a substantial performance gap to first-order (FO) optimizers such as Adam. We argue that this gap does not arise solely from the absence of explicit gradients: FO fine-tuning also benefits from informative low-rank optimization geometry, large-scale signal aggregation, and well-conditioned update directions. Motivated by this, we introduce StructZO, a structured Zeroth-Order optimization framework for memory-efficient LLM fine-tuning. StructZO performs large-query ZO estimation within an activation-induced informative subspace and applies spectral control to the resulting reduced-space update before mapping it back to the original parameters. This lets ZO aggregate useful directional signals in a compact, geometry-aware space while avoiding the activation and optimizer-state memory costs of backpropagation.
