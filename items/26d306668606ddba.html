Task Vectors Can Be Linearly Decomposed into Interpretable and Controllable Components

arXiv (cs.LG) · 2026-10-30 · 论文PDF

Task Vectors (TVs) provide a compact way to steer large language models, but they are typically treated as atomized directions merely extracted from hidden states, leaving their internal functional structure poorly understood. Motivated by prior work that decomposes in-context learning into Task Recognition (TR) and Task Learning (TL), we ask whether a single TV can likewise be decomposed into functionally meaningful components. We formulate a TR/TL-based decomposition from candidate-label unembeddings, separating each TV into TR, TL, and residual components, and try to optimize the TR and TL coefficients using Bayesian optimization for more effective intervention. We evaluate this formulation across multiple model families, classification tasks, and both instruction- and demonstration-based TVs. Across these settings, TR/TL-based optimization identifies intervention directions that outperform the original TVs. Although optimization over random directions can also improve performance, TR/TL-based search yields more reliable gains, particularly for instruction-based TVs.
