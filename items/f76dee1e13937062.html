Information-Theoretic Scaling Laws for Generative Diffusion Training

arXiv(cs.LG) · 2026-10-23 · 论文PDF

Diffusion models trained on increasingly large datasets sampled from the same data distribution will converge to the same learned distribution. However, in practice, a model trained on finite data depends on the particular training dataset; this dependence is reflected in properties of the model's outputs, such as memorization and sample quality. To understand this dependence through an information-theoretic lens, we study \textit{model information}, a computationally tractable measure of how much samples from the trained model depend on the particular training dataset. We explore its scaling behavior as model size and training dataset size vary. Specifically, we uncover two qualitatively distinct scaling regimes: (1) a nonlinear model information regime marking the transition from pure memorization to imperfect generalization, and (2) a generalization regime characterized by linear scaling of the model information and progressively improved generalization and sample quality. We then posit an information-theoretically principled scaling law to quantify these two regimes, and show that this law yields induced scaling laws for memorization and sample quality.
