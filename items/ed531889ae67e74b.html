Collective Dynamics of Self-Reinforcement: How Cross-Task Coupling Drives Policy Collapse in Unsupervised RLVR

Label-free reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) rewards a language model for agreeing with its own majority vote. It improves reasoning at first but eventually collapses: the self-assessed reward goes to one while accuracy falls, often until the model gives the same answer to every problem. The collapse appears only when training uses many problems. We explain this dataset-size dependence as a collective instability. In a solvable many-problem model in which problems share parameters, a population whose problems prefer different answers amplifies any shared perturbation for as long as the population-averaged slope $\langle F'\rangle$ of the single-problem self-reinforcement drift $F$ is positive. This holds while problems are still uncertain about their answers. Per-problem sharpening, slowed by $1/N$ for $N$ training problems, races against the shared mode, which grows at a rate $\proptoλ^2$ independent of $N$, where $λ$ is the strength of the shared coupling. This yields a critical number of problems $N_c\simeq\ln(1/δ)/(λ^2G)$, where $δ$ seeds the shared mode and $G$ decreases with the initial confidence.
