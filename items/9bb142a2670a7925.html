Systolic arrays (SAs) have emerged as prominent hardware accelerators for matrix operations in deep learning, while floating point number formats enable precision control across computational domains. This research investigates approximate computing techniques for floating point (FP) multipliers in Weight Stationary Systolic Arrays, focusing on IEEE 754 (FP32), TensorFloat-32 (TF32), and Brain Floating point (BF16) formats. By integrating partial product matrix (PPM) column truncation with positive and negative compressors in the FP multiplier architecture, we optimize the trade-off between computational efficiency and accuracy. NSGA-II optimization algorithm was employed to explore the vast design space for evolving FP multiplier designs, towards achieving substantial hardware improvements while maintaining acceptable output quality. Substantial hardware benefits were observed in the FP multiplier designs across various applications, while preserving output quality. The FP approximated Processing Elements designed in the SA was found to offer comparable CNN accuracy for models trained on MNIST, F-MNIST, and CIFAR-10 dataset.
