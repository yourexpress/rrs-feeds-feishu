Fault-Guided Cross-Layer Prediction for Unsupervised Anomaly Detection

arXiv · 2026-11-06 · 论文PDF

Feature reconstruction is widely used for unsupervised industrial anomaly detection in the single-class setting, where only normal images from one product category are available for training. We propose a single-class detector that asks what information one block can pass to another. We treat the depth of a frozen vision transformer as a sequence of discrete transitions. A directed predictor learns a block-to-block hidden-state transition pointwise over tokens. Adjacent-layer cosine similarity exposes four sharp fault lines in DINOv2 ViT-L, separating stable depth segments. The deepest blocks retain semantic context but lose local detail; a prediction from deep or shallow sources to sensitive targets therefore exposes the information that cannot be transported. The fault structure fixes the source and target roles, and the same rule transfers to ViT-B and RADIO-B. We use a bounded foreground prior calibrated solely on training images: it attenuates background evidence without also erasing defects outside the estimated foreground.
