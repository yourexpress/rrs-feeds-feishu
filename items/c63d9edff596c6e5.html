Multi-step predictions of future transition realizations can improve reinforcement learning, but their value remains difficult to quantify. Directly including these predictions in the state can lead to exponential state-space growth, making direct dynamic programming computationally expensive. We study discounted finite MDPs under a block-feedback protocol: at the start of each K-period block, the decision maker observes $K$-step probabilistic transition predictions for a subset of actions and adapts decisions to observed states within the block. We show that the optimal value satisfies a Bayesian Bellman equation on the original state space. This representation reveals that predictive information creates value by recursively changing the local order of maximization and expectation in the Bellman expansion. We formalize this effect through a Bellman--Jensen analysis and quantify the effects of prediction horizon, error, action coverage, and within-block feedback.
