Autonomous Continual Learning and Knowledge Transfer via Cortex-Inspired Neural Assemblies

arXiv cs.LG · 2026-11-09 · 论文PDF

Unlike the brain, artificial neural networks (ANNs) suffer from "catastrophic forgetting": complete overwriting of solutions when re-training on new functions ("concept shift") or on a disjoint domain of the current function ("domain shift"). ANNs require each batch of data to be roughly representative of the complete distribution, a typically expensive and impractical requirement. In this paper, we introduce Functional Task Networks (FTNs), a method of continual learning and knowledge transfer modeled after several properties of the mammalian neocortex. FTN is formulated as a distributed, stochastic mixture of experts in which a sparse, binary routing mask is computed at inference time using a small support sample of input-output pairs. Per-batch reconfiguration of the router during training, together with mask-overlap-filtered replay, shapes a basis of overlapping, partially trained subnetworks. Unrelated tasks route through mostly separate assemblies, related tasks overlap on shared components.
