Risk-sensitive reinforcement learning (RL) addresses decision-making under uncertainty and adverse outcomes. Learning risk-sensitive policies from fixed datasets is important when additional interaction with the environment is costly. Existing work on risk-sensitive RL focuses on online settings or specific risk measures, leaving a general theoretical understanding of its offline setting underdeveloped. In this work, we investigate efficient offline risk-sensitive RL using the optimized certainty equivalent (OCE), a general class of risk measures. We consider two formulations: dynamic-OCE RL, which applies OCE recursively at each step, and static-OCE RL, which applies a single OCE to the cumulative rewards. Under the tabular setting, we develop pessimistic value-iteration algorithms for both formulations and establish tight sublinear suboptimality bounds, explicitly capturing how risk sensitivity, encoded by utility functions, affects learning difficulty. Moreover, we establish minimax suboptimality lower bounds to characterize the problem's fundamental limits.
