Indirect and noisy observations are difficult to invert when the forward operator smooths out the unknown signal. In this case, the unstable modes are precisely the modes that the data reveals least, thus the success of any reconstruction method depends on how prior information interacts with the geometry of the operator across regularity scales. Diffusion priors have become ubiquitous for such problems; however, the current theory still does not determine when a learned score remains informative under ill-posed forward operators. In this paper, we study posterior diffusion for linear inverse problems on Hilbert scales and derive a stability theory under an explicit score-amplification condition. Under a smoothing condition on the forward map, a source condition on the truth, and weak-norm control of score error, we derive non-asymptotic recovery bounds in \(\Hs^s\) that separate observation noise, score approximation, and reverse-time discretization. These bounds give a recoverable regularity threshold determined jointly by operator smoothing, truth regularity, score regularity, and the posterior correction.
