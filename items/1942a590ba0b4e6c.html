Seeing Why: Exact Evidence Maps for Quantum and Optical Image Classifiers

arXiv · 2026-11-06 · 论文PDF

When an image classifier decides, we can usually only estimate why. For quantum and all-optical classifiers that act linearly on amplitude-encoded pictures up to the measurement, with no trained classical network before or after them, each class's Born probability above its no-information share, $P_g - b_g$, splits exactly into contributions of the encoded amplitudes, shown as Born evidence maps (BEM). The split itself follows from known results on quadratic forms: with the transmitted light held fixed, it is what Integrated Gradients from a zero baseline and the Shapley value give. What we add is a way to measure it on the hardware that classifies, from class probabilities on a quantum chip or, in simulation, from camera frames on a lens bench, with no gradient circuit and no light sent backwards; a $g\times g$ map takes $1 + 2g^2$ runs. Deleting the 5% of pixels with the highest evidence drops accuracy from 73% to 9% (one seed), as occlusion and gradient $\times$ input do, while plain gradients remove much less.
