Decoupling Faults from Uncertainty: Message-Efficient Byzantine Agreement with Local Predictions

arXiv · 2026-11-23 · 论文PDF

Byzantine Agreement protocols are traditionally designed under worst-case uncertainty about which processes are faulty, coupling the adversary's corruption budget $t$ with the system's epistemic uncertainty. Recent work showed that auxiliary failure predictions can improve round complexity, but cannot improve communication complexity when prediction errors are unconstrained. In this paper, we initiate a theoretical study of the complementary question: how much can Byzantine Agreement benefit when each process is guaranteed a local bound on its prediction error? We study this question through the local $k$-error prediction model, in which each process receives a local classification vector differing from the ground truth in at most $k$ positions. The predictions need not be common, and honest processes may hold substantially different views about whom to trust. Under this promise model -- where correctness relies on the local error bound holding -- we present a synchronous Byzantine Agreement algorithm requiring $n \ge t + 4k + 1$, where $n$ is the total number of processes, $t$ is the maximum number of failures, and $k$ is the local error bound per honest process.
