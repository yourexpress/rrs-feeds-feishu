A Minimal ReLU Classifier on Non-Linearly Separable Data: Convergence and Implicit Bias at and beyond the Edge of Stability

Gradient descent (GD) with large learning rates can successfully optimize neural networks even though classical smoothness-based learning rate conditions are violated. Yet theory for this regime largely covers linear models or linearly separable data. We analyze a minimal setting outside both regimes. Specifically, a two-neuron ReLU network trained with logistic loss on 1D non-linearly separable data. The objective is nonconvex and nonsmooth, and GD exhibits edge-of-stability (EoS) behavior. We identify an active cone in parameter space that contains every perfect classifier, where the problem becomes piecewise logistic regression with a varying effective dataset. Within this cone, we prove a finite-time loss bound for constant learning rates and show that GD converges in direction to an explicit max-margin vector, while the orthogonal component converges to the unique minimizer of a non-strongly convex potential. We further establish catapult results where exponentially small loss is reached after a single loss spike.
