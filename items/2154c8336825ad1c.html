BenchMint: From Real-World Tasks to Executable Benchmarks

arXiv · 2026-11-22 · 论文PDF

Large language models (LLMs) need evaluations of everyday reasoning and tool use, such as planning itineraries under user constraints and grounding numerical answers in evidence. We introduce BenchMint, an automated benchmark-construction workflow, and Daily-Life, its collection of 100 selected tasks across 11 real-world domains. BenchMint jointly constructs tasks and scoring criteria from the same source documents. Language-model judges assess possible answers against these criteria using tool traces and private evidence. We report 26 model/harness configurations, including GPT, Claude and Gemini families; 17 have complete domain profiles. These are descriptive configuration results, not a controlled model ranking. In one selected travel task, we corrected the reference total from 320 to 380 minutes while keeping both answers unchanged. The incorrect answer's arithmetic verdict switched from pass to fail; the correct answer's switched from fail to pass. With one ballot per cell, this exposes a reference error without establishing repeatability or collection-wide validity.
