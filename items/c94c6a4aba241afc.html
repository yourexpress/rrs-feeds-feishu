Leveraging their inherent sparse event-driven computation, spiking neural networks (SNNs) offer a promising path toward energy-efficient large language models (LLMs). Time-to-first-spike (TTFS) coding generates at most one spike per neuron within a time window, yielding extremely low firing rates. However, conventional TTFS SNNs are restricted to specific structures, making it challenging to encode certain blocks in LLM -- such as layer normalization and matrix multiplication --using TTFS. To overcome this limitation, we introduce a reference-based strategy specifically to encode the four core LLM components: embedding layers, layer normalization, attention-related operations and dropout. We construct a fully TTFS-based SNN architecture and train it end-to-end. Experiments on modern LLMs like BERT and GPT-2 demonstrate that our approach achieves performance comparable to ANN counterparts on natural language understanding and common-sense reasoning, while a clear gap remains on language modeling perplexity.
