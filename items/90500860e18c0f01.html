Why Kubernetes Cannot Contain a Full Lazy-Pulling Cache: Two Impossibility Results and a Bounded, Honest Design for Model Serving

arXiv cs.DC · 2026-11-09 · 论文PDF

Lazy container-image pulling makes cold starts of autoscaled model-serving fleets on Kubernetes nearly independent of model size, but it moves the model's bytes into a node-level cache that the platform neither bounds nor accounts for. This article establishes, by experiment, two impossibility results for that cache. A monitoring-side remedy cannot catch its failure: a sentinel readiness probe stays green while 272 of 280 files are unreadable. A platform-side remedy cannot contain it: kubelet, given full disk-pressure visibility, frees 0 bytes and evicts 26 bystander pods, because its reclamation actuator is keyed to an accounting that does not know the cache exists. The article then eliminates the failure class inside the component that owns the bytes, with no change to kubelet or the container runtime interface, by two design rules: a byte budget whose pressure signal is keyed to filesystem truth, so that a never-blocking, lossy accounting still enforces a hard bound (held against about 301,000 dropped updates), and pass-through semantics under which a failed cache write never becomes a failed read, with the coupling traced to a single io.MultiWriter.
