Asymmetric Multimodal Learning with Decomposed JEPAs

arXiv cs.LG · 2026-11-12 · 论文PDF

Cross-modal alignment is the standard objective for building multimodal representations. We show that alignment as a \emph{sole} objective is lossy wherever modalities are informationally asymmetric---where neither can express what the other carries. Visual question answering is an obvious case: a question is a selective relational operator probing a dense visual field whose fine-grained features no query recovers, so what alignment preserves is not what the task requires. We prove an \textbf{Alignment Redundancy Bound}: alignment-only Dirichlet energy minimization retains at most the redundant information atom $\Red(Z; X_1, X_2)$, annihilating private modality-specific structure $\Unq(Z; X_m)$ and cross-modal synergy $\Syn(Z; X_1, X_2)$. We introduce \textbf{Decomposed JEPA (D-JEPA)}, which factorizes latents into an aligned shared core and private modality-specific fibers. The shared core retains most of the structure alignment targets; the private fibers recover what alignment discards, via contextual masked prediction and asymmetric cross-subspace independence.
