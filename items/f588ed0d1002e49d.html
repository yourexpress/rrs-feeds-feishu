Acceleration of Sparse and Irregular Workloads through Energy-Efficient Stream Extensions

The slowdown of Moore's law and Dennard scaling has motivated computer architects to uphold performance scaling through architectural specialization. So far, most efforts have focused on highly regular workloads, which exhibit good data locality, ample reuse, and a data-independent execution flow. As this specialization hits diminishing returns, attention shifts towards sparse and irregular workloads, which are essential to applications like sparse neural networks, multiphysics simulations, and graph analytics. However, they lack the convenient properties of highly regular compute that modern architectures are optimized for, resulting in very low (<10%) functional unit utilization on traditional CPUs and GPUs. The most promising approaches to their general hardware acceleration leverage memory streams, which decouple data movement from execution. In this thesis, we explore energy-efficient hardware stream extensions that significantly accelerate sparse and irregular workloads in general-purpose systems without specializing to specific application domains or compromising on regular-workload performance.
