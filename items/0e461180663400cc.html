Pretraining Representation Lottery Determines Fine-tuning Generalization

arXiv · 2026-11-21 · 论文PDF

Large scale pretraining (PT) followed by fine-tuning (FT) is the central paradigm of modern deep learning, yet how PT shapes FT generalization remains poorly understood. We use a synthetic setup in which the ground-truth concept geometry of entities is known, as a controlled testbed to study how representational geometry affects model behavior and how PT representations determine FT generalization. We first show that models differing only in training seed can closely match in PT loss yet vary substantially in FT generalization. We hypothesize that a representational lottery underlies this phenomenon: the random seed dependent geometry formed during PT determines what FT can reach. We confirm this causally. First, injecting the ground-truth concept geometry into the model yields near-ceiling FT generalization, showing that good representations directly drive generalization. Then, distilling a lucky seed's learned representation into an unlucky seed unlocks FT generalization, while distilling the same model's behavior does not.
