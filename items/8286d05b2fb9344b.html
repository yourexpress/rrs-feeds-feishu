An Empirical Evaluation of Quantum Kernel Stability in Network Intrusion Detection

arXiv cs.LG · 2026-11-09 · 论文PDF

Quantum kernel methods have been proposed for network intrusion detection, yet their performance limitations are often attributed to noise in current quantum hardware. This study evaluates that assumption through a controlled analysis of Pegasos-QSVC on the NSL-KDD and ToN-IoT datasets. We perform 56 quantum experiments using one exact noiseless statevector backend and three noise-simulated superconducting backends while keeping preprocessing, PCA features, data splits, and classical baselines identical. XGBoost achieves accuracies of 98.94% on NSL-KDD and 99.41% on ToN-IoT, compared with 91.80% and 82.98% for Pegasos-QSVC. The quantum model also exhibits substantially greater seed-dependent variability. Eight runs collapse into degenerate classifiers, including four recall-collapse cases and four trivial-positive classifiers. Notably, two failures occur on the noiseless backend, demonstrating that hardware noise is not a necessary condition for classi-fier collapse. Kernel Gram matrix analysis further reveals low effective rank and severe ill-conditioning even in the absence of noise, indicating intrinsic fragility in the learned quantum representation.
