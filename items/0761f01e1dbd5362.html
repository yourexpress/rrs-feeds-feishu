Optimistic Hedge, a multiplicative weight update with one step of extrapolation, is one of the most natural regret-minimizing dynamics for games, and characterizing its regret has been a longstanding open question. Daskalakis, Fishelson and Golowich proved that if all $m$ players in a finite general-sum game follow Optimistic Hedge with a fixed learning rate, each player's regret is polylogarithmic in the horizon $T$. Subsequently, Ha showed that an appropriate choice of learning rate makes each player's regret bounded independently of $T$. This bound, however, is non-constructive and its dependence on the number of actions $n$ is not explicit, leaving open whether Optimistic Hedge can achieve a constant regret bound which is polynomial in the size of the game. We show that the answer hinges on the number of players. In two-player games, there is a learning rate for which Optimistic Hedge guarantees $O(n\log n)$ regret independent of the horizon.
