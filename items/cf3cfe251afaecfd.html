Semantic Alignment of EEG and Text: A Contrastive Learning Framework for Decoding Imagined Speech

Imagined speech decoding from EEG offers a promising route toward naturalistic brain-computer interfaces. Traditional closed-set classifiers treat semantic categories as independent labels and do not explicitly exploit relationships among linguistic intents, limiting output flexibility. We adapt a multimodal contrastive learning framework to imagined speech, aligning EEG trials with language representations in the embedding space of a frozen pretrained text encoder. An EEG encoder is trained with a composite objective combining semantically reweighted EEG-sentence alignment and text-prototype-based class discrimination, encouraging semantic structure while preserving separability among 39 intent categories. We evaluate the approach on the Chisco imagined speech dataset across five subjects, reporting Top-k accuracy, macro-F1, and balanced pairwise matching accuracy (2v2). We also investigate maximum EEG-text cosine similarity (MaxCosine) as an intrinsic indicator of decoding reliability and probe semantic structure beyond category labels using five evaluation-time semantic axes and a qualitative Semantic Gallery Probe (SGP) with previously unseen candidate sentences.
