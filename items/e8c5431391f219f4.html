Correct First, Fast Second: What a Local 7B Code Model Can and Cannot Do for GPU Kernel Optimization

arXiv cs.LG · 2026-11-09 · 论文PDF

We study how much the interaction strategy around a fixed, locally hosted 7B-parameter code model (Qwen2.5-Coder-7B-Instruct) affects its ability to replace PyTorch operators with custom CUDA kernels. On 30 KernelBench tasks (levels 1-3, first 10 each, evaluated on a V100), we compare four regimes of increasing structure: zero-shot prompting, profiling-guided prompting, iterative refinement on execution feedback, and a multi-agent loop (analysis, retrieval, generation, evaluation, feedback triage) in which the same model fills four roles around a non-LLM evaluator. Three findings stand out. (1) Correctness, not speed, is the binding constraint: even correct generated kernels average 0.97-0.99x the speed of eager PyTorch, because a 7B model cannot out-write cuBLAS/cuDNN on dense GEMM and convolution; the only real wins are fused memory-bound epilogues. (2) The best strategy depends on difficulty: profiling-guided prompting is strongest overall (43% correct, 27% faster than baseline), but the agent loop is the only method that solves any level-2 fusion problem (3/10 vs. 0/10 for all others).
