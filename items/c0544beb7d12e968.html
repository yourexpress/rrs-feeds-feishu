SOTA or Luck? The Winner's Curse in LLM Leaderboards

arXiv cs.LG · 2026-11-17 · 论文PDF

LLM 排行榜的赢家诅咒实证

Public LLM leaderboards guide model evaluation and adoption, yet the reported performance of top-ranked models can be systematically overstated. This leaderboard winner's curse arises because ranking noisy benchmark scores rewards sampling luck alongside genuine ability. The same selection effect can distort scores throughout the leaderboard, a phenomenon we call the rank-induced bias. We develop an $m$-out-of-$N$ bootstrap to correct for this bias. It assumes a fixed model pool with independent, identically distributed bounded tasks while allowing arbitrary within-task correlation among models. Under exact population tiers separated by positive gaps, we prove that the bootstrap reduces $Θ(N^{-1/2})$ ranking bias to expected residual bias $o(N^{-1/2})$. Across 14 benchmarks from 5 benchmark families, the rank-1 adjustments range from approximately 0.09 to 3.86 percentage points. In our SWE-bench Verified snapshot, the tied leaders receive an adjustment of 0.90 percentage points. Such overstatement is large enough to make the apparent winner statistically fragile.
