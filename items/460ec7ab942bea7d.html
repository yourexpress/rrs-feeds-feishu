A richness-sensitive Acoustic Biodiversity Index from species-level detections: classifier benchmarking, false-positive control and validation against standard soundscape indices in an African savanna and an urban zoo

arXiv (cs.LG) · 2026-10-30 · 论文PDF

Acoustic indices are widely used as rapid proxies for biodiversity, but they summarise the distribution of sound energy rather than the species producing it and often track richness poorly. We revise and test an Acoustic Biodiversity Index (ABI) built from species-level detections. Its primary form, ABI_E, is Shannon diversity of detections normalised by the regional species pool expected at the site and season (H / ln S_ref); temporal stability T is reported as a separate component. We first benchmarked four classifiers on a strongly labelled tropical dataset (1,216 calls, 24 classes) with recording-grouped cross-validation. BirdNET embeddings + logistic regression performed best (macro-F1 0.750 +/- 0.029), and random frame-level splits inflated accuracy from 0.41 to 0.70. We then applied BirdNET v2.4 with a location and season filter to recordings from a riverine savanna in the Zambezi Valley, Zimbabwe, and from three habitats at Emirates Park Zoo and Resort, Abu Dhabi, United Arab Emirates.
