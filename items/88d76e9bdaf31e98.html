What Environment Invariance Identifies: Minimal Invariant Sets in Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Spaces

arXiv · 2026-11-23 · 论文PDF

Invariant causal prediction identifies the direct causes of a target $Y$ by searching for covariate sets whose conditional law of $Y$ is stable across environments. We state that criterion at the level of operators, calling a set $S$ invariant when the conditional mean embeddings of $Y$ given $X_S$ agree across environments, and characterise the resulting family exactly: under two conditions on the environments, which we state separately because they fail independently, the invariant sets are precisely those containing $PA(Y)$ and no descendant of $Y$. Two consequences follow. Invariance does not identify $PA(Y)$ uniquely, since every descendant-free superset is invariant as well; and $PA(Y)$ is the unique minimal invariant set. In finite samples, recovery requires the non-invariant sets to be separated from invariance by a margin, and the sample size splits into a screening term logarithmic in the ambient dimension and a term inherited from conditional mean embedding estimation, which is the binding one.
