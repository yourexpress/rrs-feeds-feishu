Architectural Principles for Parameter-Efficient Unitary Transformations in Coherent Optical Neural Networks

arXiv cs.LG · 2026-11-16 · 论文PDF

Coherent optical neural networks based on interferometric architectures offer a promising route toward high-throughput, energy-efficient computation by exploiting the multiple degrees of freedom of light, including spatial, temporal, and spectral modes. However, practical implementations are constrained by the number of tunable parameters due to fabrication complexity, device footprint, and control overhead. In such parameter-constrained settings, achieving high expressive power remains a central challenge. We identify two key architectural requirements for maintaining high expressive power under these constraints: (i) statistical uniformity in the implemented transformations and (ii) strong mixing capability per parameter, which enables rapid and unbiased redistribution of information across modes. To realize these requirements, we develop a logarithmic-depth butterfly-topology architecture whose fundamental building block is a single phase-shifter--beam-splitter pair, in contrast to conventional rectangular mesh architectures based on Mach-Zehnder interferometers.
