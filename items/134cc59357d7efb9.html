Top-Level Operational Equilibrium: When is the response of a distributed system predictable from its own telemetry?

arXiv cs.DC · 2026-11-16 · 论文PDF

研究分布式系统响应能否从其自身遥测预测的顶层运行平衡问题

Distributed systems deployed across the edge-cloud continuum are adapted continuously: scaled, re-routed, given more or less capacity. Each adaptation rests on an expectation of how the system will respond, yet operators cannot tell from the telemetry they already collect whether that response is predictable. We define top-level operational equilibrium (TLOE), a notion of equilibrium for a running system, stated relative to the controls its operator declares. TLOE has three conditions: stationarity with time-scale separation, sufficiency of a top-level aggregate for the controls, and time-reversibility of that aggregate. We prove that an aggregate satisfying the last two exists if and only if the minimal sufficient statistic of the controls is time-reversible, so the coarsest admissible aggregate is derived rather than chosen. Under the first two conditions, the response to a small control change follows from passive telemetry through the steady-state fluctuation-response identity, with a conjugate observable fixed by the type of control: the total work in progress for a change of load, the occupancy of a stage for a change of its capacity.
