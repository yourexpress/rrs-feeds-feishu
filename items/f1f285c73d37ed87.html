Hybrid Spark: Adaptive Speculative Decoding That Lets the Content Choose the Drafter

arXiv · 2026-11-05 · 论文PDF

Speculative decoding (SD) speeds up large language model (LLM) serving, but its gain depends on the workload's \emph{shape}, such as batch size and context length, and on its \emph{content}. A model-based drafter proposes a short block on any content, while a model-free drafter such as n-gram lookup proposes long copies at almost no cost when the output repeats its input, as in code editing. Adaptive verification spends a batch-wide verification budget on the draft tokens most likely to be accepted, but it supports only one model-based drafter. Model-free drafters provide no per-token confidence, so they cannot take part in budget allocation, and deployments must either fix one drafter or split their GPUs between drafters. We present \sys, a framework that runs a model-based and a model-free drafter on every step and gives each request the better of their chains, within adaptive verification and entirely on the GPU, which keeps asynchronous scheduling intact. The key idea is a price per verified draft token: the goodput that slot would earn elsewhere in the batch.
