Contention Resolution with a Fast-but-Fickle Channel

arXiv cs.DC · 2026-11-09 · 论文PDF

The contention-resolution problem studies how $n$ devices share a communication channel without centralized coordination. Classical models typically assume a single continuously available channel and focus on minimizing total completion time. However, modern wireless systems increasingly rely on shared spectrum, where access may be abruptly revoked by incumbent users. Motivated by this setting, we study contention resolution with two channels: a reliable slow channel and a faster channel that can be intermittently disabled by unpredictable eviction periods. Moving a batch of packets from the slow channel to the fast channel incurs a unit cost, while evictions from the fast channel impose a larger per-packet penalty. Our goal is to achieve asymptotically optimal latency while minimizing switching overhead relative to an optimal offline algorithm that knows the eviction schedule. We present Randomized Adaptive Channel Exploitation (RACE), a randomized algorithm that exploits the fast channel when available while maintaining progress on the slow channel during disruptions.
