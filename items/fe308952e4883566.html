Clinical forecasting papers typically report a single best architecture. We show that on multivariate ICU physiology this question conceals variable-specific differences: no architecture is best on every variable, and which model wins changes from variable to variable. Across five ICU databases (MIMIC-III, MIMIC-IV, eICU, HiRID, and UMM (University Medicine Mannheim), a German single-center ICU) we forecast 94 channels over a 24-hour horizon with a pool of 14 models, and combine them with weights fitted per variable. On test, the combination beats the best single model on all five databases by 6.1-11.0% mean squared error (mean 8.7%). The gain is concentrated where clinical data is thinnest: the sparsest third of channels improves by 11.9% and laboratory values by 7.5%, against 3.1% for the densest third. Rank-based harmonic weighting of the best five models per variable, with no fitted weights at all, recovers about four-fifths of the gain.
