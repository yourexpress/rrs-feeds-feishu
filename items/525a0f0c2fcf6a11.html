Functional verification of Register-Transfer Level(RTL) designs has become a critical bottleneck in modern semiconductor development, often consuming the majority of project resources. Debugging, in particular, remains an especially time-consuming task, requiring careful analysis of simulation traces or counterexamples. Recent work has explored applying Large Language Models (LLMs) to RTL debug and repair with great promise. However, the existing approaches typically are for single-line bugs, operate in open-loop, rely heavily on prompts or retrieval, lack scalability, and often produce incorrect fixes. In this paper, we propose a novel automated RTL bug repair methodology that combines LLMs, assertions, and a model checker to provide scalable, trustworthy semantic grounding and formal guarantees. Concretely, the methodology leverages failing assertions to guide the LLM in generating patches for multi-line bugs. Each candidate patch is formally verified using a model checker (JasperGold), ensuring only valid repairs are accepted.
