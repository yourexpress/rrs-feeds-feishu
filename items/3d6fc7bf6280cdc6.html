While quantum technologies are rapidly evolving, near-term applications heavily rely on hybrid quantum-classical paradigms. Among these, the classical shadows framework has gained a lot of traction, not only for quantum information science, but also for the development and verification of quantum hardware. It allows to efficiently classically estimate many properties of quantum states from randomized measurements alone, a task that is otherwise intractable due to the destructive nature of measurements in quantum systems. There are, however, many properties of interest that are, due to their very problem formulation, not efficiently estimable. In this work, we identify a computational kernel occurring in estimators of polynomial functions, a class of problems that are in general computationally difficult to solve. We show that this structure allows a row-wise partition that, together with a custom SIMD vectorization, enables massive parallelization and can reduce the computational cost significantly.
