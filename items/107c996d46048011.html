Do Embedding-Map Distances Guide LLM Ideation? Four Tests Against What Came Next

arXiv cs.LG · 2026-11-16 · 论文PDF

嵌入映射距离能否引导LLM构思

A popular ideation recipe treats an embedding map of existing work as a guide: pick points far from everything and decode them into ideas. I test four ways such distances could guide ideation against what appeared later. (1) Far random directions are not text: no later item reaches a raw cosine above about 0.10 with one (after centring or whitening, 22 of 24 cells stay out of reach), and an LLM decoder shown a target's neighbours, with score-only feedback, writes those neighbours in all six target sets. A neighbour-free search writes coherent text at median cosine 0.57 and 0.47 to interior gaps, beating the nearest existing item on 15 and 14 of 20, but only 0.07 and 0.06 to far points. (2) On k-means cells of a past-only map, the registered hole score mostly tracks cell size and position; three density-based gap scores forecast which cells grow fastest at or below chance (AUC 0.30 to 0.66) and add nothing to momentum in Steam and arXiv, with patents inconclusive; more isolated new Steam games are slightly better reviewed.
