Zero-knowledge proofs (ZKPs) have become a fundamental building block for privacy-preserving computation, blockchain systems, and verifiable machine learning. Modern ZKP protocols span two distinct families: curve-based protocols, which rely on large finite-field arithmetic, and hash-based protocols, which operate over native 64-bit fields. Existing hardware accelerators are specialized for one protocol family because they are built on operand-scanning Montgomery multiplication, resulting in fixed-width datapaths, poor utilization across heterogeneous operand widths, and limited scalability. This paper presents \emph{ZKPU}, a Zero-Knowledge Processing Unit for heterogeneous ZKP workloads. ZKPU introduces a new compute paradigm by co-designing finely integrated product scanning (FIPS) Montgomery multiplication with a reconfigurable Twin Systolic Fabric (TSF), enabling modular multiplication at arbitrary widths in 64-bit increments. Combined with a fine-grained Deque buffer, on-chip twiddle-factor generator, and memory-efficient MSM preprocessing strategy, ZKPU provides a unified architecture for both curve-based and hash-based protocols.
