Ziv Meets Sumcheck: Zero-Knowledge Proofs of Floating-Point LLM Inference

arXiv · 2026-11-22 · 论文PDF

Zero-knowledge proofs can show that a neural network produced a given output without revealing the network, but current systems for language models prove integer approximations of the model rather than the floating-point arithmetic that is actually deployed. Proving floating-point matrix multiplication has been regarded as hard, because rounding after every addition makes the result depend on the order of the additions. We avoid this obstacle by proving the correctly rounded product, in which every dot product is computed exactly and rounded once. This result is independent of the hardware and at least as accurate as standard kernels. We give an interactive proof for this relation whose cost is close to that of integer matrix multiplication, with roughly a thousand times fewer committed values than per-operation floating-point circuits need constraints, extend it to every layer of a transformer, and give a privacy construction for the complete argument whose zero knowledge we prove under explicit hypotheses.
