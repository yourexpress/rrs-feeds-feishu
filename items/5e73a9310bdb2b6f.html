What Does the Effective Dimension of a Hybrid Quantum Neural Network Actually Measure?

arXiv · 2026-11-06 · 论文PDF

Hybrid quantum neural networks (QNNs) pass features from a classical backbone into a variational quantum circuit, and many studies log a scalar called the effective dimension as a sign of capacity or model quality. The effective dimension is usually defined as the participation ratio (PR) of the quantum Fisher information matrix (QFIM), but studies in practice log a PR of cheap parameter sensitivities instead, and the meaning of that scalar is rarely tested. We show that the logged scalar equals the PR of Pauli-Z output sensitivities, that each sensitivity is at most n times its QFIM diagonal entry on n qubits, and that a PR of the QFIM diagonal overstates the full-matrix PR by an exact factor set by the off-diagonal mass. We then test the scalar on CIFAR-10 and EuroSAT with four frozen backbones, one of them a random projection, over 4 to 16 qubits, 1 to 4 layers, three entangling patterns, and three seeds. At a fixed circuit, CIFAR-10 accuracy spans 23.14% to 86.44% while the scalar stays between 6.63 and 9.13, and its correlation with accuracy changes sign across seeds in 7 of the 14 configurations.
