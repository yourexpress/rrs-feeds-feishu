Understanding the mechanism behind why diffusion models memorize their training data remains an open question in generative modeling. In this paper, we examine this question from two perspectives: (a) the model-level perspective concerning the onset of memorization in a model during training, and (b) the sample-level perspective concerning the proclivity of an individual sample to be memorized by a given model. We show that the so-called input loss curvature, or the Frobenius norm of the Hessian of the diffusion model's training loss, is an effective metric for quantifying both perspectives on memorization. In particular, we empirically demonstrate that input curvatures averaged across training and validation samples can identify when a model is likely to start regurgitating its training data when tracked over training time. However, this metric is not sufficient for measuring an individual sample's likelihood of being memorized. To address this perspective, we introduce a counterfactual definition of memorization in diffusion models, extending the analogous notion from supervised learning proposed by [Feldman, 2019].
