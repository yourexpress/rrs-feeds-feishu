Bauer et al. (2026) showed that in low-data reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards, training GRPO on mixed-difficulty prompts gives up to 5x the sample efficiency of easy-only prompts. They did not test whether this benefit is specific to GRPO, or whether another post-training method given the same data would obtain it too. We test this on their procedural counting task with Qwen3-4B-Base and LoRA, comparing GRPO to rejection-sampling fine-tuning (RFT) on the same prompts, verifier and token cap. Six primary arms cross the two methods with three prompt sets (Easy, Mixed, and a Curated subset of prompts the base model sometimes solves, which makes GRPO's implicit filter explicit). We use three seeds per arm. Hypotheses and falsifiers were registered before training. To rule out a poorly tuned baseline, RFT receives a 27-run hyperparameter sweep, while GRPO uses Bauer et al.'s recipe unchanged. GRPO outperforms RFT at every prompt set: by 10.9 points on held-out test problems with curated prompts, and, in post hoc comparisons with matched rollout budgets, by 8.4 and 9.2 points on the easy and mixed prompts.
