RB-LDC: Redundancy-Balanced Latent Coding for Noise-Robust Diffusion Latents

arXiv cs.LG · 2026-11-10 · 论文PDF

Latent diffusion models decode approximately correct latents, making decoder sensitivity to perturbations relevant to image quality. After whitening, forward diffusion corrupts every direction at the same signal-to-noise ratio (SNR), despite unequal decoder sensitivities. We study an invertible linear layer between the encoder and diffusion process that shifts each direction's log-SNR. Its linear minimum mean-square-error (LMMSE) recovery risk has an exact spectral expression for any whitened latent distribution. Under an energy budget and a floor ensuring invertibility, minimizing decoder-sensitivity-weighted LMMSE risk integrated over the diffusion schedule yields a unique optimal allocation. The layer aligns with the decoder's expected pullback metric and equalizes marginal risk reduction across directions, computed by a deterministic spectral solver. At large budgets, the allocation follows a power law in sensitivity whose exponent is determined by the low-SNR tail of the loss weighting: one half for noise prediction and two thirds for data prediction under the cosine schedule, with a crossover on truncated schedules.
