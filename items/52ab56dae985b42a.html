Cooling Noisy Population Protocols -- Vanishing-Gain Averaging and Localisation under Persistent Errors

We study noise-tolerant coordination in anonymous population protocols whose agents interact in uniformly random pairs, maintain real-valued labels, and have no global clock. Each agent cools its updates using a gain $γ_k=(k+1)^{-p}$ computed from its own local interaction counter. First, for pairwise averaging with independent communication errors drawn from a fixed centred finite-variance distribution, we prove convergence with probability 1 to a common finite random value for every $1/2<p\le1$, and derive convergence-rate exponents under the same assumptions. Second, for one-dimensional localisation with fixed non-degenerate Gaussian displacement errors, we show that the one-sided RightAlign rule rectifies centred noise and cannot stabilise; a signed update based on the relative-position discrepancy restores convergence to the true agents locations up to one finite random translation. Third, we analyse the setting in which both communication errors and displacement-measurement errors depend on the physical distance between the interacting devices.
