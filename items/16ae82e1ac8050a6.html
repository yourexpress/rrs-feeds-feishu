Can LLMs predict the outcomes of machine learning experiments?

arXiv · 2026-10-15 · 论文PDF

Recent advances in AI make Recursive Self-Improvement (RSI) increasingly plausible.

Recent advances in AI make Recursive Self-Improvement (RSI) increasingly plausible. Large Language Models (LLMs) can already propose, select, and run Machine Learning (ML) experiments. However, the space of possible design choices in ML is vast. An LLM that proposes an experiment needs a reliable intuition of whether it is worth running given the timing constraints of long training runs. In this work, we measure such intuition in current frontier LLMs by evaluating how accurately they predict the impact of self-generated ML experiment ideas before these are put into training runs. To this end, we construct a dataset of 288 LLM-proposed ideas on eight MLE-bench tasks. We then prompt five frontier LLMs (GPT-6 Astra, Claude Opus 5.5, Claude Fable 5.1, Kimi K3, and GLM-5.3) to predict the impact of each idea and compare their predictions with the actual outcomes after training. We report four main findings. First, LLM predictions are substantially better than random chance but far from accurate: even the best model, Claude Opus 5.5, explains only half of the variance in outcomes (R^2 = 0.49).
