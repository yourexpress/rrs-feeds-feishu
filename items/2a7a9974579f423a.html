Heterogeneous systems-on-chip (SoCs) increasingly rely on domain-specific hardware accelerators (HAs) to deliver performance and energy efficiency gains beyond what general-purpose processors can provide. However, operating systems (O/S), such as Linux, assume that processors are the Compute Elements (CEs) and HAs are peripheral devices rather than specialized CEs. As a result, HA execution incurs substantial software (SW) overheads that reduce the effective benefits of hardware (HW) acceleration. This work demonstrates that significant inefficiencies arise from ``O/S peripheral device taxes'' (O/S taxes), including SW stack-accelerator communication, invocation, interrupt handling, and scheduling. We built MEERKAT, a Field-Programmable Gate Array (FPGA)-based framework for configurable multicore systems that include HAs; it allows the user to migrate Linux O/S support from SW to HW and identify, study, and quantify the impact. Here we study four major sources of O/S tax and introduce techniques to mitigate them.
