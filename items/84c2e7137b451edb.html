Attention Temperature Confounds Zero-Shot Context Extension

arXiv · 2026-11-23 · 论文PDF

A training-time attention change is usually judged for zero-shot context extension against a dense baseline beyond the training length at the default temperature, $τ= 1$. The outcome depends on how miscalibrated the baseline is there: the share of perplexity it recovers when its logits are sharpened by one scalar. Under the standard recipe (fp32 master weights), an 8k-trained baseline is 8-15% miscalibrated at 30k in three seed-paired 365M twins; in one of them this makes the block-sparse-trained twin look 7% better, which calibration removes. In pure-bf16 training without master weights the miscalibration is several times larger: an AdamW step ($β_2 = 0.95$) on a gain initialised at 1.0 is below half the bf16 spacing at peak learning rates under $1.7\times10^{-3}$, so gains never move (22 checkpoints), the baseline is 37-58% miscalibrated, and block-sparse pretraining, attention dropout and ALiBi look 6-35% better. Calibrating one temperature per model and length removes all three advantages, leaves ALiBi 5% behind RoPE and keeps part of the gain of a positive control, NTK-aware scaling.
