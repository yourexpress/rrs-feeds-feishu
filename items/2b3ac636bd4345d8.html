Ptychography is a computational imaging method that reconstructs a sample from overlapping diffraction measurements. This technique relies on these overlapping measurements to provide the redundancy information needed for robust reconstruction, but dense scanning increases acquisition time and data volume. Reducing scan overlaps can improve measurement efficiency but weakens the physical constraints available for reconstruction. Diffusion models provide strong learned prior information, offering a promising approach to compensate for the loss of measurement redundancy. However, effectively utilizing this prior information under weak measurement constraints remains a challenge. We propose a progressive coupling posterior sampling (PCPS) method, which allows for a broader exploration of the solution space under high uncertainty while maintaining stronger diffusion-guided consistency as the reconstruction becomes more refined. Experiments on real X-ray ptychographic data at various scan overlaps demonstrate that PCPS consistently improves reconstruction accuracy compared to traditional iterative methods and existing diffusion posterior samplers, especially at low overlaps.
