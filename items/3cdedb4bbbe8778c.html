Inference of phylogenetic trees from sequences plays a central role in understanding evolutionary relationships, with applications ranging from tracking pathogen spread to reconstructing the history of life. Conventionally, practitioners obtain posteriors over trees using likelihood-based methods like MCMC. However, likelihoods are only available for simple evolutionary models with restrictive assumptions. For realistic evolutionary models, inference using conventional methods is prohibitively expensive, inaccurate, or even impossible. In practice, practitioners settle for using as flexible a conventional method as possible, even though they expect it is misspecified. In principle, we could use simulation-based inference: simulate tree and sequence data from an evolutionary model and train a neural network to predict the posterior over trees given sequences. However it is a mathematical challenge to represent and train complex posterior distributions over trees. Here we present a flexible family of conditional generative models over trees.
