HPC clusters are shared among many projects, each holding an allocation of node-hours, and ensuring fairness is becoming harder. Foundation models workflows and automated pipelines have made job submissions bursty and concentrated among a few heavy users, so occasional users absorb queueing delays created by frequent ones, with no indication of how long their jobs will wait. Existing schedulers either forget past usage, protect only the job at the head of the queue, or assume that running jobs can be preempted. We propose HyDRA, a Hybrid Deadline and Ranking-Aware scheduler that enforces fairness among users while bounding the queue wait of every job. HyDRA assigns each job a deadline on arrival based on its size, the priorities of its project and user, the current backlog, and how far its project and user are from their entitled share. It ranks waiting jobs by a priority score that grows with waiting time, serves jobs close to their deadlines first, and backfills a job only when no deadline, or fairness limit is violated.
