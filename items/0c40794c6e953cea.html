Mitigating catastrophic risks from large language models requires excising hazardous operational capabilities, such as cyberwarfare exploitation and pathogen enhancement, without degrading benign scientific utility. In these dual-use domains, legitimate defensive science and malicious attacks share vocabulary, syntax, and internal model representations, causing standard unlearning methods to induce severe collateral damage (concept bleeding). In this work, we present Orthogonal Boundary Unlearning (OBU), a mathematically grounded framework designed to eliminate collateral damage on benign dual-use capabilities. OBU extracts a domain-matched capability subspace from paired calibration queries via singular value decomposition on empirical activation covariances, and restricts parameter updates strictly to the orthogonal null space. This guarantees that benign representations experience zero parameter perturbation along the spanned calibration subspace.
