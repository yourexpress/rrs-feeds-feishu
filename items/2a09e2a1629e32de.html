Most multimodal classifiers assume that all useful evidence is available upfront, whereas clinical inquiry is sequential. We present a synthetic evaluation framework separating objective multimodal inference from active inquiry. An objective model maps synthetic tongue, bilateral multipressure pulse, and acoustic observations to eight syndrome families. A provenance-bearing Knowledge Core and training-only answer model support Bayesian narrowing over 33 fine terms using 25 typed questions, while seven safety questions remain outside the diagnostic posterior. Weak shared symptom factors limit fine-term influence on objective phenotypes, and an objective-only probe tests leakage. In a preregistered 600-case synthetic evaluation, objective family accuracy was 87.0%. Cost-sensitive expected information gain (cost-EIG) raised family accuracy to 95.5% and exact fine-term accuracy to 42.5%, versus 92.7% and 33.0% for an unoptimized fixed-order baseline. Selective family accuracy reached 96.9% at 95.3% coverage. However, cost-EIG asked more questions on average than fixed order (9.795 versus 9.352).
