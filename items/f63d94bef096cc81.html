Rethinking How to Leverage Cheap Physics for Data-Efficient Neural Operators

arXiv cs.LG · 2026-11-06 · 论文PDF

Neural operators learn mappings from PDE inputs, such as initial conditions and coefficient fields, to the corresponding solutions. Once trained, they can rapidly predict solutions for new inputs. However, obtaining input-solution pairs for supervised training often requires expensive numerical simulations, whereas PDE inputs and governing equations provide inexpensive physical information. This raises a question: how can cheap physical information reduce the need for labeled solutions? A natural approach is to use physics to select inputs for labeling, but our acquisition experiments show no consistent advantage over random sampling. This observation motivates exploring an alternative: using cheap physics to improve neural operator initialization before supervised training. We propose Physics-Informed Initialization (PI-Init), a simple yet effective method that pretrains neural operators on unlabeled inputs using physics-informed losses and retains the learned parameters for supervised fine-tuning on the labeled dataset.
