Where Generated Floor Plans Stop: Calibrated Sample-Level Checks Against a Built Baseline

arXiv · 2026-11-06 · 论文PDF

Floor plan generators are judged mainly by Compatibility, a graph edit distance to the requested adjacencies that is near saturation and blind to room shape. Our sample-level protocol calibrates each threshold no standard states at a declared false-rejection rate on real samples and grades them with the same checks. It reports each sample at its first failure, counts samples without a verdict apart, and attributes a stop to the generator or to its representation where a control exists. We apply it, with a production layout solver's checks, to four published generators against two built corpora, RPLAN and Modified Swiss Dwellings (MSD). A squareness check set per room type refuses 5 per cent of RPLAN apartments and 33.7 per cent of House-GAN++ plans. The gap survives resolution, tracing and orientation controls. Pooled over rooms, a Wasserstein distance on squareness orders the generators nearly as the check does but separates House-GAN++ from the built corpora by far less. Taken per plan on the check's worst-room margin, it separates them about fivefold to sevenfold. Within the GAN generators, Compatibility barely predicts which plans pass.
