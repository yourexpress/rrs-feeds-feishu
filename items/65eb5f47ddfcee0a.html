PsyDrift: Healthy-Reference Latent Diffusion for Anomaly Scoring in Autism Spectrum Disorder

arXiv · 2026-11-23 · 论文PDF

Individual heterogeneity and acquisition-site variability complicate neuroimaging-based characterization of autism spectrum disorder (ASD). We propose PsyDrift, combining ASD/healthy-control-supervised functional-connectivity representations with healthy-reference latent diffusion. An encoder produces 128-dimensional representations, and a denoising diffusion probabilistic model (DDPM) learns exclusively from healthy training latents. Distance from a diffusion-denoised reconstruction provides a continuous deviation score. We evaluate 1,102 subjects from all 20 ABIDE-I sites using five-seed leave-one-site-out cross-validation. The classifier and DDPM achieve mean-site AUROCs of $0.798 \pm 0.003$ and $0.718 \pm 0.013$, respectively; uncertainties denote seed standard deviations. Matched healthy-reference scorers on the same representation achieve 0.68-0.71; the statistical significance of diffusion's modest numerical advantage remains unestablished. Within ASD, scores show weak associations with ADOS Total (Spearman $ρ= 0.108$, $p = 0.038$) and full-scale IQ ($ρ= -0.138$, $p = 0.002$), without adjustment for age, sex, site, or head motion.
