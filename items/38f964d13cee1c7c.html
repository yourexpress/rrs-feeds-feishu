Rollout decoding affects both which trajectories are used for reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) and how many completion tokens are spent collecting them. We study whether candidate-token support should remain fixed or widen during training in the context of rollout sampling efficiency. Our main schedule moves from top-K=10 to K=20 and then to full support as the attempted-token budget increases. In a 4B code-RLVR setting, the schedule has a higher interpolated 150M-token validation value than its matched fixed-K=10 continuation in all three runs, with a mean gain of $1.80{\pm}0.42$ points. The schedule also performs comparably to a separately trained fixed-K=20 baseline, the strongest fixed-support setting in our sweep. Follow-up experiments suggest that late widening to full support is the most promising variant, although the evidence remains noisy, and mixed-reward-group diagnostics do not clearly explain the gain. An exploratory single-run test replay yields identical point estimates for the schedule and fixed $K=10$.
