Predicting the runtime of noisy quantum circuit simulations is important for scheduling, resource allocation, and performance optimization. However, accurate prediction is challenging because backend-aware transpilation can substantially alter the original circuit structure, while the backend-derived noise model and simulator execution behavior can introduce additional runtime variation. We study the effectiveness of graph neural networks (GNNs) and conventional regression methods in predicting Qiskit Aer simulation runtime measured after transpilation. We construct a dataset from a benchmark pool of 1,402 unique circuits spanning 22 circuit families, two Qiskit fake-backend configurations, and four transpiler optimization levels. Specifically, we compare a source GNN using original circuit information, hybrid GNN combining source-level graph with post-transpilation features, and transpiled GNN using only transpiled circuit information, along with five regression models.
