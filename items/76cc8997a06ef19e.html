Prescribed Bayesian Efficiency under Lifetime Adjustment Costs

A Bayesian learner may retain an issued action while updating its posterior, trading predictive loss against adjustment charges. We assume population curvature, boundary control, an order-$1/n$ lower bound on expected posterior uncertainty about the optimal action, and an observable unbiased gradient witness whose squared norms are uniformly integrable. Sample losses may be unbounded or nonconvex. Cumulative expected excess measures the loss increase relative to current predictive Bayes actions. For bounded nonnegative nondecreasing charges $c$ with $c(0)=0$ and nondecreasing profiles $G: \mathbb{N} \to [1,\infty)$ with $G(J)=o(J)$, we characterize joint attainability of cumulative expected excess $O(G(J))$ at horizons $T=2^J$ and finite expected lifetime charge. Here $J$ indexes logarithmic time. The criterion is finiteness of a prefix-constrained allocation of tracking-error allowances across action-distance scales, equivalently its decreasing-weight dual. Adaptive timing and private randomization do not enlarge the attainable region.
