Monotone Viability Learning for Offline Safe Reinforcement Learning

arXiv · 2026-11-06 · 论文PDF

Offline safe reinforcement learning requires reliable long-horizon safety estimates from fixed data. Learning Hamilton-Jacobi (HJ) viability in this setting calls for semantic preservation, convergence, and numerical stability. To address these challenges, we propose Monotone Viability Learning (MoViL), which learns viable sets from offline transitions through monotone finite-horizon set recursion. The recursion preserves finite-horizon safety semantics and converges to the infinite-horizon viability kernel. Binary supervision maintains a fixed target scale across horizons, avoiding discount-induced numerical attenuation. We further establish sufficient conditions for recovering behavior-supported viable subsets. The learned viability estimates are used to certify actions at deployment. Dubins car experiments validate the numerical accuracy of the learned viable sets. Evaluations on safe RL benchmarks demonstrate state-of-the-art reward-cost performance. MoViL satisfies the average cost constraint on every task and achieves the lowest average cost among the compared methods.
