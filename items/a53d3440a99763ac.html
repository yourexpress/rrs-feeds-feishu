When Does Sharing Work? Scaling a Shared SigLIP2 Across Image, Video, Audio, and Audio-Visual Representations

arXiv · 2026-11-05 · 论文PDF

Contrastive pretraining has advanced image-text understanding, yet image, video, and audio encoders are still trained independently, with cross-modal alignment realized mainly through post-hoc binding to a frozen anchor space. We present Omni-Encoder, a general multimodal encoder that processes image, video, audio, and audio-visual inputs within a single SigLIP-2 Vision Transformer with fully shared parameters. Unlike anchor-centric binding or per-modality adapters, all modalities are optimized jointly end-to-end, and the framework is organized from input to training. A modality-native input stage first maps each signal into the shared embedding space, including a 1D-convolutional audio tokenizer that converts mel-spectrograms into tokens aligned with visual patches. A multimodal rotary position embedding parameterized by absolute timestamps then places visual frames and audio segments in a unified spatiotemporal coordinate system. Finally, alternating-modality training interleaves image, video, audio, and temporally aligned audio-visual batches and accumulates their gradients into one update, which we find central to stable joint optimization.
