Exploration in physical control environments requires agents to identify actions whose consequences reveal previously unexplored aspects of the underlying dynamics. However, many intrinsic motivation methods rely on immediate prediction errors, state novelty, or local physical changes, which may overlook informative outcomes that emerge across extended temporal horizons. We investigate whether learning structured representations of reachable futures can provide a more effective basis for exploration. To this end, we introduce Hamiltonian Temporal Contrastive Curiosity (HTC$^2$), a framework that combines multi-horizon temporal representation learning with Hamiltonian phase-space structure. A horizon-conditioned contrastive critic learns temporal relationships from future-state pairs sampled within the same episode. The distance between positive-pair representations is then used as an intrinsic reward, encouraging the agent to explore futures that remain insufficiently organized in the learned representation.
