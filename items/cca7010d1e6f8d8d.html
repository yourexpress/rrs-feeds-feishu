Instruction-Aware Transfer of GPU Implementation Crossovers: a Tensor-Product Discontinuous Galerkin Operator on GB200, H100, and A100

arXiv cs.PF · 2026-11-11 · 论文PDF

We compare up to six implementations of an FP64 tensor-product discontinuous Galerkin operator at p = 7-1023 on GB200, H100, and A100 with fixed numerics, input, and code. The fastest path is machine dependent: at p = 511, a fused-epilogue GEMM path is 5.0% faster than a fused Tensor Core kernel on GB200 and 6.9% slower on A100. Nominal-peak scaling reproduces the A100 ranking but fails on H100, where the cost of FP64 arithmetic in the profiler-defined FP64 budget depends on the instruction. We therefore price each issued instruction at its architecture-dependent cost in that budget, using a calibration-side counter profile and one derived calibration factor per kernel, with no fit to the target. A retrospective leave-one-GPU-out evaluation, holding out target timings and counters but not the model form, transfers the p >= 511 path times with 1.9% median error at the common MMA shape, and the direction of the crossover at the H100 shape. Restricting the streaming multiprocessors of one A100, with nothing fitted, reproduces the A100-to-GB200 reversal. At roduction-relevant orders the model does not resolve the fastest fused path; the result identifies the crossover mechanism.
