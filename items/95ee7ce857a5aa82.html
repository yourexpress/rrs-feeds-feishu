Language models can produce similar probabilistic predictions after observing outcomes, reading summaries, receiving instructions, or undergoing activation interventions. Whether these routes induce equivalent belief states remains unclear: agreement in current predictions does not establish how a model will respond to subsequent evidence. We investigate this distinction in a Bernoulli inference task with an analytic Bayesian reference. Across three open-weight language models, we prepare states through four textual routes and two activation-level interventions, measure both next-outcome continuations and explicit probabilistic estimates, and append identical new evidence from each prepared state. We quantify the persistence of the initial belief through its effective evidence weight, the number of new observations it is worth. We find that the format of evidence decides which readouts express its content: outcome sequences primarily affect continuations, whereas summaries and stated rates primarily affect explicit estimates.
