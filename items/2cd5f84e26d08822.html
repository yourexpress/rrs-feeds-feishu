Gradient-based data attribution has become an important tool for high-quality data selection in the instruction fine-tuning of large language models (LLMs), as it estimates the contribution of training examples through their influence on model optimization. However, we find that existing global gradient attribution methods suffer from surface-form bias: minor changes to semantically irrelevant details, such as wording or formatting, can produce highly inconsistent attribution scores even when the underlying content remains unchanged. To address this issue, we propose Content-Invariant Gradient Attribution (CIGA), a robust framework for instruction data selection. CIGA first identifies layers whose gradients are both influential for attribution and stable under semantics-preserving surface-form perturbations, thereby reducing formatting noise in gradient features. It then dynamically fuses these attribution signals with semantic representations of the input, combining optimization-level influence with content-level information.
