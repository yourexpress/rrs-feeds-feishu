Failure Modes and Data-Efficient Remedies for Transformer-Based Prediction of Viscoplastic Flows

arXiv · 2026-11-20 · 论文PDF

Fast and accurate prediction of viscoplastic flows remains a challenge in computational rheology due to yield surfaces, discontinuous gradients, and strongly nonlinear transitions between yielded and unyielded regions. Transformer-based neural operators such as RheOFormer have recently shown strong potential for surrogate modeling of non-Newtonian flows. However, their applicability to viscoplastic systems and their efficacy in recovering realistic flows remain poorly understood. In this work, we present the first systematic investigation of RheOFormer applied to steady Bingham channel flow. By isolating the problem to a simple geometry and constitutive model, we examine how dataset structure, numerical value range, and input representation govern the model's ability to interpolate and extrapolate within the parameter space. The data regimes in which RheOFormer remains accurate and those in which errors emerge are identified, particularly for plug-dominated flows and for conditions outside the velocity scales or flow structures represented during training.
