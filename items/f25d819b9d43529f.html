Challenge-Aware Knowledge Recruitment with Meta-Agent Supervision for Medical Multi-Agent Reasoning

arXiv · 2026-11-05 · 论文PDF

Large language models perform strongly on standard medical QA but degrade on harder reasoning tasks, where multi-agent debate often fails to beat strong single-model prompting. We argue that a key limitation is that existing systems select experts and retrieve knowledge from the question alone, rather than from gaps that emerge during reasoning. We propose challenge-aware adaptive recruitment: an external CHALLENGER inspects the evolving reasoning trace and identifies specific factual or expertise gaps. These signals trigger targeted knowledge retrieval or mid-discussion specialist recruitment. On the MEDAGENTSBENCH hard subset (862 questions across nine biomedical datasets), upfront retrieval provides little benefit, while challenge-aware in-between retrieval performs better; on a multi-specialist backbone it improves accuracy on 8 of 9 datasets and adds 6 percentage points over Challenger Only. Ablations indicate benefits from both retrieval timing and gap-specific query signals, and a live web-search experiment shows that the timing advantage persists across retrieval backends.
