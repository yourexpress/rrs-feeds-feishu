A Sparsity Analysis of Traffic Forecasting Architectures: How Much Missing Data is Too Much?

arXiv · 2026-11-19 · 论文PDF

交通预测架构对传感器缺失数据的稀疏性分析

Traffic forecasting models are typically evaluated under ideal conditions with complete data. This evaluation does not reflect the frequent sensor failures and communication outages common in real-world deployments. This paper presents a comprehensive analysis of multivariate traffic prediction models to evaluate their performance under varying levels of sensor data loss during inference. We benchmark several architectural paradigms, including graph neural networks, attention-based Transformers, and selective state-space models, across sparsity levels ranging from zero to complete sensor loss, using two widely used multi-channel traffic benchmarks. To ensure consistent comparison across traffic variables, we use normalized metrics and define a usability threshold to identify when predictions become unreliable. Our results show a clear trade-off between baseline accuracy and robustness: models with the highest accuracy under full-data conditions often degrade most sharply as data becomes sparse, while less accurate models maintain more stable performance.
