Background: Large language models (LLMs) show promise for extracting information from clinical free-text documents, but their outputs are often unstructured and lack traceability, complicating validation and adoption in clinical workflows. In this work we introduce SIFTING, an LLM-based framework designed to address these shortcomings. Methods: SIFTING combines the language comprehension capabilities of LLMs with segment-level processing and structured prompts with strict output control, linking findings to the source text to enable both accurate and transparent information extraction. To demonstrate its capabilities, we applied the framework to the task of extracting tumor T-stage information from 130 lung cancer radiology reports (SIFTING-T-stage). A compact 4-bit quantized version of the open-source LLM Llama-3.3-70B (35 GB) was used in a fully self-hosted setup, providing full control over data and model. Performance was evaluated against a reference standard created by four clinical experts and compared with a range of LLMs as used in a conventional single-prompt approach, using bootstrap resampling to estimate confidence intervals.
