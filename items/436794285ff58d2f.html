The Price of Not Planning: Parallel Sampling Depth Peaks at the Reconstruction Threshold

arXiv cs.LG · 2026-11-16 · 论文PDF

不规划的代价：并行采样深度在重建阈值处达峰

Masked diffusion models sample the tokens of one round independently. Planned decoders first generate a coarse plan, then fill in the tokens in parallel. We ask when a plan saves rounds, how many it saves, and how fine it must be. We study this on broadcast trees: the leaves are the tokens, the internal nodes are the plan, and the edge correlation $θ$ sets how strongly tokens depend on the plan. Errors are measured by the forward Kullback-Leibler divergence $\varepsilon$, and the lower bounds hold for every deterministic value-adaptive rule, also with learned conditionals. We show that a decoder without the plan needs of order $\min\{n,(n/\varepsilon)^α\}$ rounds below the reconstruction threshold, where $α=\log b/(4\log(1/θ))$ rises to $1/2$ at the threshold, and that the exponent stays below $1/2$ above it (binary channel: $0.02\leθ\le0.98$). With the plan, $\log_b n+1$ rounds suffice. In the binary model below the threshold, a plan with units of $m$ tokens needs of order $\log_b(n/m)+\min\{m,(n/\varepsilon)^α\}$ rounds, so it gives logarithmic depth exactly when $m$ is logarithmic.
