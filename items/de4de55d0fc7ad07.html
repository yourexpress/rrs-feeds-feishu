Ask, Don't Equip: Question-Driven Coding Agents for Deep Kernel Optimization

arXiv (cs.LG) · 2026-11-02 · 论文PDF

LLM agents for GPU kernel optimization sit at two ends of a structure-freedom tradeoff. Scripted agents fix what the model does at each step, via a learned policy, an optimization menu, or fixed roles, and are sample-efficient where the recipe applies and stall where it does not. Open-ended agents let a self-directed model mutate a population of kernels and pay for the freedom in trials: thousands of evaluations, repeated edits, and wrong candidates. Both respond to hard targets by equipping the agent with more: parsed profiler output, disassembly analysis, retrieval over documentation and libraries, and stores of lessons. We argue that both lack direction, a hypothesis about where the remaining gap is, and that a short question asked at the right moment supplies it without narrowing what the agent may do next. We present KernelSeek, a question-driven agent that makes an existing CUDA kernel faster with no operator-specific knowledge and no faster implementation to imitate: a general-purpose coding agent, a minimal harness that enforces a verifiable goal, and questions at decision points, first from a human, then from a bank the agent applies to itself.
