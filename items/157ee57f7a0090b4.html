A Methodologically Rigorous Benchmark of Linear Regression,RNN, and LSTM for S&P 500 Forecasting:Identifying Sources of Performance Inflation in Financial DeepLearning

This study examines one-day-ahead forecasting of the S&P 500 closing level using a 2006-2025 dataset that includes fundamental price data, macroeconomic indicators, and engineered technical features. We benchmark three model families, namely linear regression, vanilla recurrent neural networks (RNN), and long short-term memory networks (LSTM), within a unified preprocessing and chronological evaluation pipeline. A central contribution of this work is a methodological audit of common practices in the deep-learning-for-stock-prediction literature. We identify three sources of result inflation that frequently appear in published methodologies: wavelet-denoised evaluation targets, the absence of a naive persistence baseline, and best-of-N replicate reporting. We then re-evaluate all model families under a corrected protocol that controls for each. Performance is reported using root mean squared error, mean absolute percentage error, and the Pearson correlation coefficient, averaged across N = 30 independent replicates per configuration.
