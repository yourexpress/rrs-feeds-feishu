Delirium affects a large fraction of intensive care unit (ICU) patients and foreshadows longer stays, lasting cognitive decline, and higher mortality. Flagging the patients who will become delirious within the first day of admission would give clinicians a window to intervene. Yet state-of-the-art predictors rely only on the structured electronic health record (EHR) of vital signs, laboratory values, and medications, while overlooking the free-text clinical notes written in the same window, which carry information the numbers cannot encode. To this end, we present DeliMF, a multimodal framework that fuses the structured time series with clinical text. It encodes the structured signals with a temporal Transformer and the time-stamped radiology reports with a clinical text encoder, and fuses the two through a gated cross-attention module. The gate learns a per-patient weight on the text; when a patient has no note the gate closes and DeliMF reduces to its structured-only form, giving robustness to missing text by construction.
