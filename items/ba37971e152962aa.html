Provenance-Isolated Epistemic Quorums: Execution-Bound Evidence Certification and Plausible-World Safety Gating in Agentic Control Planes

arXiv · 2026-11-24 · 论文PDF

Agreement among large language models is not physical evidence for mutating production infrastructure. Because generative models share pretraining priors and conform to peer rationales during deliberation, voting ensembles correlate errors instead of providing independent fault tolerance; yet attaching rigid deterministic checks to unconstrained agent outputs paralyzes incident remediation. We present Provenance-Isolated Epistemic Quorums (PIEQ), an execution-bound certification layer that keeps every language model strictly outside the trusted computing base. PIEQ unifies assumption-based truth maintenance, physical provenance isolation, control-theoretic plausible-world safety shielding, and linearizable concurrency governance around three distinct obligations: certifying diagnostic preconditions from disjoint physical failure domains, gating candidate mutations across all unrefuted fault worlds while falling back to read-only diagnostic probes under ambiguity, and binding admission certificates to target execution through snapshot barriers, revision checks, authorization freshness, trusted temporal-precondition policies, and capability-tiered enforcement.
