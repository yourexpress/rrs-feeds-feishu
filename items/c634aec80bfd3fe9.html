Adversarial attacks on graph neural networks (GNNs) have been studied as a model-centric optimization problem driven by gradients, learned policies, or meta-learning, with the graph treated as a passive substrate. This view overlooks a defining feature of GNNs: their predictions are produced by structured message passing along the graph, so their adversarial vulnerabilities are encoded as much in the geometry of the graph as in the learned weights. We develop a structure-driven framework for feature attacks in which the attack is derived from the spectral properties of the normalized adjacency operator. Its central result is a separation principle: under a natural relaxation of the attack objective, the optimal feature perturbation is rank one and factorizes into a graph-dependent component (degree-proportional spatial allocation, depending only on the graph) and a weight-dependent component (top singular vector of the composed weights, depending only on the weights). Node degree thus emerges as the fundamental graph quantity governing adversarial vulnerability to feature perturbations.
