Escaping Latent Traps by Remembering: Training-Free Episodic Kernel Repulsion for World-Model Planning

arXiv cs.LG · 2026-11-17 · 论文PDF

免训练情节核斥力逃离世界模型规划潜陷阱

Latent world models plan towards a goal image by minimising the distance between predicted and goal latents. This distance is not geodesic: it has local minima, latent traps, behind walls, at corners and in dead ends, and memoryless receding-horizon replanning keeps the agent in them, revisiting states far from the goal. We propose Episodic Kernel Repulsion (EKR), a training-free cost term that penalises candidate plans by a top-$k$ Gaussian kernel to a novelty-gated memory of the latents the agent has actually visited, rescaled by the current base cost so that its strength is comparable across models. Across two JEPA world models and four benchmarks, EKR raises average success from 31.8% to 46.7% (LeWM) and from 54.7% to 64.0% (H-JEPA) with the same model, planner and budget. Same-memory counterfactuals show that, in most settings, EKR pushes plans away from visited states, in navigation, often at a higher base cost; stuck episodes and revisits drop accordingly. In seven of eight settings it matches or exceeds $4\times$ samples at $0.8$--$1.5\times$ compute.
