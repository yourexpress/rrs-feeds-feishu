Protein motif scaffolding benchmarks assess whether generative models can build scaffolds around functionally important residues. Their conclusions depend on which motifs are tested and which criteria define success. Existing benchmarks typically use small hand-curated motif sets and static refolding criteria, but protein function often depends on conformational ensembles and transitions. Here, we address both benchmark scope and success criteria. We introduce BILBY-PRO, a systematically derived benchmark of structurally conserved functional motifs from PROSITE-linked experimental structures, comprising 220 cases from 174 PROSITE motif-pattern entries. Evaluating six recent scaffold-generation models reveals distinct patterns in target coverage and unique-solution yield: RFDiffusion and Proteina solve more targets, whereas ESM3 produces more unique successful scaffolds on the targets it solves. We further introduce BILBY-MD, a reference-based all-atom molecular dynamics evaluation framework.
