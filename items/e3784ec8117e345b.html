Before the Word: Name Information in Description-Derived States of Small Language Models, With and Without Naming Failure

arXiv cs.LG · 2026-11-14 · 论文PDF

描述派生状态中小语言模型的名称信息

The tip-of-the-tongue (TOT) state, knowing an object but not its name, motivates this study as an analogy only. We ask whether small language models form a name-predictive state from a name-free description, and whether it differs when naming fails but description selection succeeds. In an internally pre-registered study (a frozen document, no external registry) of Qwen2-0.5B, SmolLM2-1.7B and Phi-3-mini with 200 items, we combine a similarity read-out, a Patchscopes-style read-out and activation patching with matched controls. H1 (name information in the state) was supported in all nine model-by-description-type combinations; the agreement rule was met by the similarity measure together with patching, while Patchscopes stayed near chance. Post-hoc robustness checks after internal review (logit lens, single-position patch, manipulation check) temper this reading: name information in middle layers is clearest under the multi-position patch and the similarity measure, and weak under single-position patching at 50% depth.
