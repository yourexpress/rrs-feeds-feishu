Time-Aware Urban Flood Routing with Sticky Edge Closure on Forecast Depth: A Hanoi Case Study

arXiv (cs.DC) · 2026-11-02 · 论文PDF

During urban flash floods, commuters and guiding agencies require awareness towards which roads remain passable when a vehicle actually arrives, rather than relying solely on which links appear open at initial departure. Most existing flood-routing tools apply a single static snapshot to each route; meaning that corridors that appear clear initially can become flooded before arrival. This paper presents a routing engine that ingests a two-hour water forecast, renewed every 15 minutes, and plans each journey around roads that the forecast closes for that specific vehicle class. Under this formulation, any road that closes mid-trip remains closed for that query, which keeps route calculation fast enough for live requests while operating inside a production GraphHopper routing server. Testing on a recorded storm in western Hanoi revealed that a router checking only departure conditions sent 7.8% of routes into roads flooded upon arrival. In comparable trips, the proposed rule produced longer routes in 3.95% of cases, requiring a median of only 9.2 s of additional travel time.
