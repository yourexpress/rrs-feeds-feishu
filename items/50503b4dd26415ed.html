Time-Series Anomaly Detection (TSAD) is a fundamentally challenging task: it is unsupervised, requires high local precision capabilities, and suffers from limited labelled data. Recent work has explored adapting Large Language Models (LLMs) to time-series via cross-modal transfer. However, existing approaches are poorly suited to Multivariate TSAD (MTSAD), as tokenisation often obscures fine-grained temporal and variables structures, and most methods optimise auxiliary objectives such as forecasting or reconstruction. We posit that these surrogate objectives become unreliable as model capacity increases. Highly expressive models risk reconstructing or forecasting anomalies, undermining the foundations of both strategies. Instead, we propose to use LLMs purely as feature extractors and directly analyse their induced feature spaces. In this work, investigate the ability of LLMs to provide useful representations for MTSADs in a zero-shot setting through classification. To this end, we study the separability of the extracted features, under the assumption that anomalous features should be isolated from normal ones.
