Large vision-language models (LVLMs) exhibit strong multimodal in-context learning (ICL) capabilities, yet this ability degrades substantially as model size decreases. Knowledge distillation offers a natural way to bridge this gap, but existing methods primarily align output distributions or hidden representations directly. Such alignment teaches the student what the teacher predicts without revealing which evidence in the complex context causally supports that prediction. Consequently, a student can imitate the teacher's answer while continuing to rely on language priors, prompt structure, or other spurious cues. To address this limitation, we introduce Multimodal Causal Distillation (MCD), a distillation framework that transfers how a strong teacher uses multimodal evidence during ICL. MCD uses structure-preserving token interventions to identify and verify causal evidence, then transfers how the teacher responds when that evidence is retained or removed. This design connects distillation to the causal patterns by which the model uses contextual evidence during multimodal ICL.
