Auditing black-box clinical prediction models with a large language model through queries alone: a blinded test with planted flaws

arXiv · 2026-11-18 · 论文PDF

仅通过查询审计黑盒临床预测模型的盲测

Large language models (LLMs) are used to explain clinical prediction models, on the promise that they can say what a model computes, find its faults and even reveal new knowledge in it. We tested the three promises where the answers could be checked. From the International Stroke Trial we trained four kinds of model (logistic regression, explainable boosting machine, gradient-boosted trees, neural network) for two outcomes on patients randomised earlier, planted one flaw in each of 24 copies (an under-predicted subgroup, a reversed risk factor or an implausible step) and mixed them with the 8 clean models. GPT-6 Astra audited each model three times, blind, through a tool that only returned the model's predictions; the judging rules were committed before any run and applied by program. Its surrogate models, with a median of 128 terms, reproduced the audited models' log-odds with a mean $R^2$ of 0.976 (0.959 for an additive surrogate of 42 terms fitted by an analyst). It found the planted flaw in 69 of 72 runs (96%; 95% CI 80% to 99%); a script written for the purpose found all 24.
