Autoregressive electronic health record (EHR) foundation models predict the next event token in patient trajectories: sequences of structured medical events. A typical pretraining strategy is to concatenate all trajectories into a global stream and sample token windows from it. This gives patients with longer sequences more training exposure and allows tokens to attend across patient boundaries. We argue that these choices create a misalignment between pretraining and downstream clinical tasks. To explore this, we investigate how the composition of pretraining sequences affects clinical prediction performance. Using the global-stream approach as a baseline, we propose alternative sequence composition methods that control how patient trajectories contribute to pretraining sequences. Across four MIMIC-IV v2.2 tasks, our best method improves macro AUROC, AUPRC, F1, and balanced accuracy, with relative gains of 9.3% in F1 and 2.7% in AUPRC under a fixed training recipe. We find that these gains generalize across four additional evaluation settings, including the INSPIRE and eICU-CRD datasets, with improvements in macro AUROC, AUPRC, and F1 score.
