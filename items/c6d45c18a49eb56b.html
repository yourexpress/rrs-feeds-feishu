On the Difficulty of Sequence-Level Supervised Learning for Autoregressive Language Models

arXiv cs.LG · 2026-11-12 · 论文PDF

During the supervised learning phase, autoregressive language models are typically trained by minimizing the negative log-likelihood of the training data, which decomposes as a sum of token-level cross-entropies via teacher forcing. This objective is simple and efficient but potentially suffers from so-called exposure bias: a mismatch between the way the model is trained and the way it is used at inference time. Can we instead design sequence-level loss functions that incorporate inference-time decoding procedures, such as greedy decoding or autoregressive sampling? We show that two natural attempts lead to loss functions that are fundamentally flawed: unbounded below. We further show that these two flawed loss functions are intimately connected: using autoregressive Gumbel tricks, the sampling based loss is the expected greedy loss under Gumbel perturbations. Since the pathology stems from the dependence of the decoded sequence on the model parameters, we analyze the update direction obtained by applying a stop gradient on the sampling procedure, leading to a moment matching direction.
