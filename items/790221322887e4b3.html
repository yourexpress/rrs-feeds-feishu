A common indication that a fine-tuned model has forgotten a deleted record is a membership-inference score near chance (ROC-AUC $\approx 0.5$). This reading is sound only if the comparison population represents how the record should behave after a valid deletion---a counterfactual that is rarely validated. We test this premise in a controlled $2\times2$ study pairing two membership-inference benchmarks, ArxivMIA and BookMIA, with two language models, Llama-3.2-1B and Qwen2.5-1.5B. Exact retraining validates the membership reference: it lies within the prespecified chance band on ArxivMIA but reaches $0.122$ on BookMIA, causing the same metric to reject an empirical deletion reference. A post-hoc BookMIA/Qwen diagnostic replaces benchmark label-0 records with same-pool, never-fine-tuned records and moves the reference from $0.257$ to $0.523$, supporting population mismatch in that cell. Each approximate-unlearning checkpoint is judged jointly on membership removal, deletion-targeted shift beyond non-deletion variation, and utility.
