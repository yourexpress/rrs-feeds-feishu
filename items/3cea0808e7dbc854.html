M3Former: Fine-Grained Cross-Layer Mixing for Transformers

arXiv (cs.LG) · 2026-11-02 · 论文PDF

Residual connections give each Transformer block access to all earlier layers only through one fixed sum, broadcast to all of the block's inputs. This broadcast bottleneck imposes three constraints: the query, key, value and residual inputs of a block, which we call its roles, read the same vector; all hidden dimensions within a role share one set of weights over earlier layers; and every earlier layer has the same fixed weight for every token. We propose the Multi-Role Multi-Group Mixing Residual (M3), which removes all three: every role, and every group of hidden dimensions within a role, reads its own token-dependent mixture of all earlier layers. M3 starts as an exact residual connection and adds as little as 1.4% parameters and 1.8% FLOPs. From 100M to 1.4B parameters, M3Former outperforms the Transformer and coarse-grained cross-layer connections at every size, matching a Transformer 1.19-1.34x the size of the one it modifies, trained with 1.4-1.8x the compute. Relaxing only some of the constraints recovers only part of this gain. M3Former also improves sparse mixture-of-experts models of up to 4.6B parameters and trains more stably in deeper networks.
