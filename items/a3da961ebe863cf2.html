Automated polysomnography after ischemic stroke is challenging because of altered sleep-related neural activity and concurrent respiratory disturbances. Existing stroke-specific approaches mainly address sleep-stage classification, leaving joint analysis of sleep architecture and respiratory events underexplored. Moreover, adapting pretrained EEG encoders introduces a representation inconsistency when downstream models are trained on embeddings from previously seen patients but evaluated on unseen individuals. We propose MM-Net, a multimodal multi-task framework that resolves this inconsistency through cross-fitting and ridge-based embedding alignment. Sleep-adapted EEG-EOG representations and engineered neural descriptors are fused with learned cardiorespiratory features through cross-attention and shared temporal modeling for simultaneous sleep staging and respiratory-event detection. On iSLEEPS (99 patients with subacute ischemic stroke), ten-fold patient-independent evaluation achieved 76.3% staging accuracy (Cohen's $κ$ = 0.656) and 0.825 respiratory-event AUC, outperforming the evaluated baselines under matched protocols.
