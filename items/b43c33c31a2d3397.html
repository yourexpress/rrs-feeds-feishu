When the Cheapest Feasible Router Fails: Auditing Deployment Commitments in Cost-Aware LLM Routing

arXiv cs.LG · 2026-11-16 · 论文PDF

最便宜可行路由器失效时：成本感知LLM路由部署承诺审计

Cost-aware LLM routing often selects the cheapest policy meeting empirical risk and coverage constraints. Yet a router that passes an audit can violate its fixed service contract on new requests, even when candidate policies are constructed independently. We study this deployment-commitment decision with disjoint construction, audit, and test samples. Across 19 RouterBench task families, the cheapest audit-feasible router deploys in 91.82% of opportunities but violates the held-out contract in 32.22% of deployments. We introduce Menu-Aware Routing Commitment (MARC), which qualifies policies using audit evidence and a serving-cost tolerance to retain the apparent winner, deploy a supported alternative, or defer. Its practical policywise variant (MARC-P) reduces observed conditional failure to 0.73% at 85.79% deployment, with higher serving cost; fixed-margin and formal risk-control procedures provide competitive alternatives. Nested audits show how added evidence changes deployable policy sets, and learned semantic routing exhibits the same commitment failure.
