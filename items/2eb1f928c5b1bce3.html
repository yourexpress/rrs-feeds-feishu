Scaling Graph Similarity Learning with Graph Neural Networks

arXiv cs.LG · 2026-11-06 · 论文PDF

Graph similarity computation is an important task in graph mining with many real-world applications, such as chemical compound analysis, program dependence comparison, and social network analysis. The problem is computationally challenging: Graph Edit Distance (GED) and Maximum Common Subgraph (MCS), which quantify the distance between graphs and identify their largest shared subgraph, respectively, are NP-hard to compute exactly. To address these computational challenges, Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) have been applied to this problem. However, existing GNN-based approaches have primarily focused on small-scale graph datasets, while scalability to larger scales remains underexplored. In this work, we propose FiLSim, a scalable GNN-based framework, and investigate whether a simplified GNN pipeline can maintain competitive performance on standard benchmarks while scaling to large graphs. We additionally propose a sequential graph-editing strategy to construct large-scale graph datasets with reliable GED labels to evaluate scalability.
