Predicting which scientific connections will form next can point researchers toward the most promising leads. One successful approach learns representations by sampling paths through publication networks, represented by a hypergraph, that connect scientific entities, concepts, and researchers, approximating how knowledge and expertise move through the literature. Sampled paths are then used to train an embedding model to forecast scientific connections such as a property of a material. Performance, however, depends strongly on how many paths are sampled: too little sampling yields incomplete representations, while further sampling can eventually weaken prediction quality. The best sampling budget is therefore often identifiable only after future discoveries are known. We introduce Query--Anchor Agreement (QAA), a diagnostic requiring no future labels or separately trained model. QAA can be used to select a sampling budget, and refine or re-rank the resulting forecast to improve forecasting scientific connections. QAA compares the candidates a model predicts for a target with those most similar to the target's known historical examples, and identifies when these two views converge. …
