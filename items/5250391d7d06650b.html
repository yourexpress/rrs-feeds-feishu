Interaction Retention and Output-Likelihood Training in Parallel Sampling

arXiv · 2026-11-21 · 论文PDF

Parallel samplers such as masked diffusion language models generate several coordinates per step from conditional marginals and randomize which coordinates are generated together, so even exact conditionals return a distorted distribution. We give an exact local account of this distortion. Around any full-support product law, the Gram operator of the sampler's response is diagonal in the interaction decomposition, and its eigenvalues are retention probabilities: the probability that an interaction has a unique latest-revealed coordinate. This retention spectrum determines the exact frontier between output accuracy and conditional calibration, its regularization path, and the population response of finite-mixture likelihood training. For pairwise interactions it yields a sharp dichotomy. Under homogeneous retention, output-likelihood training is equivalent to a single context gain; heterogeneous retention opens correction directions beyond any scalar, with an advantage bounded by an explicit heterogeneity gap.
