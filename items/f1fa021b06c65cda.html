Where Is the "Self" of a Large Language Model? -- From Self-Index to Self-Reference

arXiv · 2026-11-06 · 论文PDF

Whether a large language model has a self is usually argued without a way to measure it. We measure a functional self with two levels familiar from biology: a self-index, which marks material as the model's own, as animals tag self-generated signals with a corollary discharge, and self-reference, the ability to recognize what is one's own, as in mirror self-recognition. Our main finding is that language models mark their own messages in their internal representations and that recognizing these messages depends on this mark: no model we tested recognizes its own messages without it, and in the three models we edited, changing the mark removes or creates the recognition. We place the same text in an Assistant, a User, or a System message of the model's own chat template. In Qwen3.8-27B, the representation separates the model's own message from both external sources at every one of its 64 layers and in every held-out text. Asked who wrote a message, the model answers "you" with probability 0.77 for its own messages and at most 0.07 for the others.
