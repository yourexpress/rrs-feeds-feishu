Loop Before You Leap: What Recurrent Depth Can and Cannot Buy in Data-Limited Pretraining

arXiv · 2026-11-06 · 论文PDF

Multimodal models have often scored below text-only baselines in the BabyLM Challenge (Klerings et al., 2024; Takmaz et al., 2025). We test whether additional recurrent computation helps close this gap. Recurrence increases computation by reusing layers without adding parameters or training data. Using a single implementation, we compare standard and looped Transformers under a 100M-word budget, with three seeds per main condition. Each image is represented by one feature vector from a frozen DINOv2 encoder, used as a prefix token. Caption words count toward the budget, so multimodal training also changes the language data. In a pre-registered text-only comparison, a 12-layer stack repeated four times exceeds a standard 48-layer Transformer by 5.4 BLiMP percentage points at matched inference compute. However, multimodal training lowers BLiMP accuracy by about 3.8 points in both architectures, with no clear mitigation from recurrence. An exploratory loop-side no-image control, trained with the multimodal language data and batching schedule, scores 5.7 points below the text-only baseline.
