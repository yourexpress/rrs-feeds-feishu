GNN-Based Deep Reinforcement Learning for Coastal Emergency Response under Extreme Water Levels

arXiv cs.LG · 2026-11-15 · 论文PDF

When a storm surge approaches a coastal town, the emergency plan must be put into action quickly using water-level forecasts, flood extent, road access, vulnerable residents and shelters. Bringing this information together under time pressure is slow and depends on the duty officer's expertise. This paper is one step in a wider research line from data to safe decisions: machine learning to understand the hazard, reinforcement learning and planning to choose actions, large language models (LLMs) to explain and cross-check them, and an assurance layer that keeps people in charge. Here we address the action step on a real place. We ask whether a policy can learn the timing of emergency actions from decades of real events. Lido di Venezia is represented as a heterogeneous graph built from public elevation, road, building and census data and driven by hourly tide-gauge records from 1983 to 2024, with seven actions taken from the municipal emergency plan. A PPO policy is trained on this graph and compared, on storm windows unseen in training, with a rule-based system simulating the officer's discretionary decisions.
