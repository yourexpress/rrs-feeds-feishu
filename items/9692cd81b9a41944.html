Pain Reassessment from Pulse Waveforms with Language Model Explanations

arXiv · 2026-11-22 · 论文PDF

Routine photoplethysmography (PPG) offers a continuous physiological signal for pain reassessment between self-reports. Whether prior recordings provide an individualized physiological reference beyond care-process timing remains unresolved. Across 52,082 report-linked assessments from 21,514 patients in MIMIC-III critical care and MC-MED emergency care, we evaluate a workflow that pairs longitudinal PPG representations with local evidence-linked language interpretation. Among 27 methods, source-selected full-history models achieved average precision of 0.824 from critical to emergency care and 0.822 in reverse. Removing historical pulse-run representations while retaining reassessment availability and elapsed time lowered critical-to-emergency four-hour within-patient concordance from 0.718 to 0.534. This comparison supports the contribution of prior pulse-run representations to within-patient ordering beyond timing cues. Across 2,905 test patients, a local Qwen model interpreting frozen predictions achieved a 94.7% patient-equal pass rate on automated evidence checks after at most one revision.
