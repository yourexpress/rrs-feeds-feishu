Attention Is Only as Good as Its Witnesses: When Higher-Order Layers Help, and When They Do Not

arXiv cs.LG · 2026-11-09 · 论文PDF

Transformers stop learning graph search as graphs grow, and learn the card game SET only after many distinct questions, although attention can represent both. We read these failures, and the uneven record of higher-order attention, from what a layer sums over: each layer we study computes the partition function of a family of witnesses and reads it at a temperature. Two laws follow. The depth law is a known result of logic, which we state for these layers: L layers over two-step walks reach exactly 2^L - 1 steps, just as a logical statement with L nested clauses of the form "there is a vertex such that" sees exactly that far. We build a construction that reaches this far, checked in Lean, and, when vertices carry no names, no such network reaches further. The size law is the cost of reading at a temperature, which logic does not have: existence is read correctly at every size, in the worst case, if and only if inverse temperature times margin is at least the logarithm of the number of candidates.
