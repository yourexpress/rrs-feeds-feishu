Training models on their own outputs has attracted widespread attention as a possible route to advance AI capabilities. One prominent approach is self-distillation, where a model is trained to match the token-level predictions it would have produced when given access to additional context, such as a cue, feedback, or instruction. However, the safety properties of this training procedure are poorly understood. In this paper, we present a surprising safety failure of recursive self-distillation, where each self-distilled model becomes the teacher for the next round of training. We observe that when a safe model is recursively self-distilled on its own (safe) generations while receiving an additional non-harmful cue, it becomes increasingly misaligned across a diverse set of harmful queries; we call this Progressive Misalignment. We show this failure mode in both the Qwen3 and Phi-4 model families on SORRY-Bench and StrongREJECT. Progressive misalignment also occurs in a more realistic setup, where the fixed cue is replaced by feedback from a simulated user with benign intent.
