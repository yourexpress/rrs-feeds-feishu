Verifier Noise Is a Temperature: Testing Tilt Predictions in Finite-Budget LLM-Guided Search

arXiv cs.LG · 2026-11-06 · 论文PDF

LLM-guided search loops propose a modified candidate, score it, and keep it if it scores better. When both candidates are re-scored at every comparison, as in the minibatch acceptance gates of prompt optimizers, keep-if-better is a reversible Markov chain if the score difference is logistic and the mutation operator is reversible, and its stationary law is the mutation equilibrium tilted by exp(beta f). For any continuous symmetric noise difference the same tilt is the stationary law of a small-step diffusion limit, with beta = 4F'(0); for a minibatch gate beta grows with the square root of the minibatch size. Caching the incumbent's score instead inflates the recorded score. We test the tilt as a prediction for LLM-guided program search, committing each prediction from calibration data before the search runs. The level after 150 steps rises with the minibatch size in six of seven settings, but its predicted value fails: on bin packing, search attains 62-88% of the predicted gain when the proposer edits the parent and 109-119% when it writes new heuristics.
