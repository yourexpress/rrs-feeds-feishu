Tree-based samplers offer a compelling alternative for scalable inference-time search, bypassing the per-step reward and gradient dependencies that introduce systematic bias into SMC-style frameworks. Despite this advantage, contemporary tree samplers fail to achieve broad exploration. Their reliance on learned, DDPM-like transitions inherently couples neighbor states in a trajectory, thereby restricting child-parent feature divergence in the tree sampler. Consequently, these samplers suffer from restricted coverage of the target marginal distribution, severely hindering the exploration critical for efficient search under sparse reward. To overcome these limitations, we introduce a simple yet effective Bootstrap Stochastic Interpolant Tree (BSIT) sampler that decouples child node generation from explicit parent dependencies. By expanding the degrees of freedom during child node sampling, BSIT encourages diverse trajectory rollouts and unlocks broader path-space diversity-the exact catalyst needed for efficient exploration.
