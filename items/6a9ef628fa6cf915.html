The continuous expansion of digital infrastructure, especially data centers, has imposed unprecedented, geographically concentrated stress on existing power grids. However, rigorous causal evidence quantifying how these facilities reshape local power-infrastructure supply remains limited. This study builds a comprehensive county-level US panel dataset spanning 2010-2024, uniquely integrating individual data center building footprints and power-plant additions. To mitigate endogeneity and high-dimensional confounding, this study deploys Double Machine Learning with five-fold cross-fitting, conditioning on county and year fixed effects alongside regional socioeconomic covariates. Baseline estimation identifies a significant positive causal effect robust across multiple rigorous specifications. The result holds following 1%-99% winsorization that downweights mega-clusters including Loudoun County: growth in data-center floor area drives meaningful increases in nearby power-generation capacity (ATE=1.982, P=0.000, CI: [1.25, 2.72]).
