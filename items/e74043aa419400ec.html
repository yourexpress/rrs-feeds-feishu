Efficient Conformal Calibration for Paired Recommendation Decisions

arXiv (cs.LG) · 2026-10-31 · 论文PDF

Risk control can suppress useful changes to a frozen recommendation rule. We study how to recover valid activation from the same observed fractional losses. Two specified internal calibrators can lose efficiency by compressing losses into their sum or clipping auxiliary inverse counts before averaging. We propose Averaged Conformal Risk Calibration, or ACRC, which averages before clipping. A product identity proves expected-risk validity, while pointwise bounds characterize the additional activation. For fixed populations sampled without replacement, we connect activation to signed utility under heterogeneous users and switching constraints. Near the budgets, positive population mean gain and the stated nondegeneracy conditions give positive leading-order utility increments despite sampling dependence. Across six datasets, changing only calibration improves discounted net utility by $0.010226$ over raw and $0.008337$ over RB. A separately frozen Jester confirmation gives $0.011025 \pm 0.001082$ over raw with the rule and filter fixed, using a five-seed approximate 95 percent paired interval.
