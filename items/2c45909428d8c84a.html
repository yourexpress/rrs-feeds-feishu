Across social and behavioral research, measuring latent constructs can be challenging when target populations are small or difficult to reach. Recruiting additional respondents may be costly or infeasible, leaving researchers with limited data for estimating questionnaire factor structures. Yet item wording contains structural expectations before data collection, and whether these can improve small-sample estimation without replacing empirical validation remains unclear. We introduce the Semantic-Empirical Updating Framework (SEUF), which converts large language model paraphrases and embeddings, aligned with hypothesized item-factor assignments, into prespecified priors for Bayesian confirmatory factor analysis. We evaluated SEUF using a 38-item survey of 617 industrial-park stakeholders and two public surveys, FEVS and RIASEC. Semantic structure persisted after wording controls across all three instruments. With 50 respondents, SEUF achieved loading precision comparable to conventional CFA with 70-82 respondents, equivalent to 29-39% fewer respondents at the specified precision benchmark.
