How Deep is the Signal? Time-Context up to 30 Years for Colorectal Cancer Prediction in a Primary-Care EHR Model

arXiv · 2026-11-06 · 论文PDF

EHR foundation models read medical events within a fixed context window, and long context has so far been measured only in tokens. How far back in time a model must look to capture the earliest predictive signal is unknown. We introduce time-context, the calendar lookback window before the prediction point, and provide, to our knowledge, the first systematic evaluation of decade-scale time-context for an EHR foundation model. We pretrained a decoder-only transformer (34.6M parameters) on 1.4B coded events from 3.6M primary-care patients in North West London and fine-tuned it to predict incident colorectal cancer under a 12-month pre-diagnostic buffer. A frozen cohort of 1,008 cases and 9,582 matched controls, all registered for at least 30 years, was evaluated at 15 nested lookback windows from 2 to 30 years, so that every truncation is compared within the same patients. Discrimination rose from an AUROC of 0.551 at 2 years to 0.590 at 18 years, then plateaued, with about 80% of the gain reached within 10 years; an XGBoost baseline converged to the same level (peak AUROC 0.589).
