Mixed-Size Placement Initialization via Stiefel-Manifold Sequential Subspace Optimization

Mixed-size global placement simultaneously optimizes the locations of large macros and millions of standard cells in modern very-large-scale integration (VLSI) physical design. State-of-the-art analytical placers achieve strong scalability, but their nonlinear objectives and density handling make them sensitive to the initial placement. This paper presents a geometry-driven framework that formulates placement initialization as a Quadratically Constrained Quadratic Program (QCQP) with first-order centroid and second-order spreading constraints. Explicit mixed-size spreading targets are derived from the die geometry and object sizes, to obtain stable constraint values even under extreme size variation. A utilization-aware rescaling of the constraint matrix prevents pathological spreading when fixed objects occupy significant die area. The QCQP is then transformed into a Stiefel-manifold optimization through metric equalization and centering projection, making the underlying geometric structure explicit.
