Generalizing from small graphs to larger ones can require computation beyond the depth of a neural model used during training. We study target tasks that admit a common local iterative computation and whose effective dependency radius grows with graph size. The central idea is to learn a local update rule through GNN pretraining, then repeatedly apply the same learned processor beyond the training depth to extend information reach on larger graphs without retraining. Calling such parameter-shared architecture a \emph{looped GNN}, we categorize six schemes for looped GNNs to separate the effects of depth extension, fine-tuning, and freeze-and-steer adaptation. Our analysis establishes fixed-depth information limits and sufficient conditions under which accurate iterative reuse overcomes them. To improve beyond unadapted reuse, we introduce \emph{Loop-Freeze-Steer} (LFS), which freezes the pretrained processor and learns a small controller to steer its repeated application, using much fewer trainable parameters than full fine-tuning.
