Embedding-level scaling expands the capacity of language models through lookup-based memory rather than by increasing model depth, width, or dense computation. Extending this idea to vision-language models (VLMs) is challenging because visual features are continuous, spatially structured, and query-dependent, unlike discrete text tokens that naturally support n-gram lookup. We introduce V-LUMEN, a visual lookup memory for VLMs. V-LUMEN discretizes visual features with a frozen codebook, builds spatially aware memory keys through spatial aggregation-based hashing, and modulates them with text-conditioned relevance signals; all addressing is non-parametric, and the retrieved embeddings are fused into the backbone through a gated residual pathway. On five reasoning-intensive benchmarks, V-LUMEN substantially improves a frozen Qwen3-VL-2B backbone and transfers zero-shot to three benchmarks whose images are disjoint from the training data. At a matched parameter budget, moving part of a LoRA or MoE adapter's capacity into V-LUMEN preserves accuracy on two backbones, indicating that external visual memory is an independent capacity axis rather than a stronger adaptation method.
