Reinforcing Diffusion Models by Instantaneous Trajectory Distribution Matching

arXiv · 2026-11-23 · 论文PDF

Forward diffusion reinforcement learning (RL)---enabling deterministic ODE sampling without trajectory backpropagation---has grown increasingly popular for efficiently aligning diffusion models. However, we reveal that existing methods essentially rely on signed reward-weighted regression (RWR), regressing the \emph{trainable policy} velocity onto rollouts from a \emph{behavior distribution}. Unbiased baseline subtraction implicitly assumes that the policy velocity equals the behavior marginal velocity---an idealized assumption violated under classifier-free guidance (CFG) rollouts, offline/off-policy updates, and imperfect online self-consistency. This \emph{behavior--policy mismatch} breaks baseline invariance and injects \emph{baseline-induced gradient drift}, destabilizing training. To resolve this issue, we propose \textbf{I}nstantaneous \textbf{T}rajectory \textbf{D}istribution \textbf{M}atching (\textbf{iTDM}), taking the infinitesimal limit of Trajectory Distribution Matching toward a time-local reward-guided target via the posterior-score identity to yield a sample-level gradient anchored at the behavior velocity without learning an additional reward-tilted target score.
