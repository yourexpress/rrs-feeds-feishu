Microdroplet Generator Surrogate Enhancement and Inverse Design Through an Uncertainty-Guided Framework

arXiv · 2026-11-05 · 论文PDF

Microfluidic droplet generation is critical for many applications, yet efficient device design in high-dimensional parameter spaces remains computationally prohibitive due to complex multiphase flow dynamics. This work presents a unified methodology that couples adaptive surrogate enhancement with simulation-driven inverse design. High-fidelity Volume-of-Fluid simulations provide ground-truth data, while neural network ensembles predict flow regimes and droplet diameters. Ensemble-based uncertainty quantification identifies epistemic uncertainty using consensus levels for regime classification and the coefficient of variation for diameter prediction, revealing that uncertainty concentrates near regime transition boundaries where physics is highly sensitive to parameter perturbations. A physics-informed enrichment strategy targets these critical zones and addresses class imbalances by deploying additional targeted simulations and retraining the surrogates forming the ensembles. A framework that integrates Particle Swarm Optimization for constrained inverse design, balancing accuracy with predictive confidence and physical consistency is therefore proposed.
