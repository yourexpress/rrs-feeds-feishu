Unlike autoregressive large language models (AR-LLMs), diffusion LLMs (dLLMs) generate text by iteratively denoising many tokens in parallel, offering a promising path to efficient inference. Existing serving frameworks, however, schedule dLLM requests with policies designed for AR models, namely first-come-first-served (FCFS) admission and prefill priority. We find that both policies are ill-suited to dLLMs, wasting much of their speed. FCFS ignores that a dLLM request's decoding cost is highly heterogeneous and unknown at arrival, causing severe head-of-line blocking. Prefill priority, widely used in AR-LLM serving to keep time-to-first-token low, sharply cuts dLLM efficiency, as rounds that run only prompts leave most of the batch idle. We present DWISE, a Denoising Workload-Informed Scheduling Engine for efficient dLLM serving. DWISE schedules requests by their remaining work, measured in a block-level unit shared by prefill and denoising, so both phases share a round.
