Federated learning (FL) under differential privacy (DP) is typically evaluated on large client cohorts, where subsampling and cross-client averaging amplify privacy and smooth injected noise. Personal biosignal applications such as wearable EEG instead involve small federations of a handful of subjects, a regime whose interaction with formal client-level DP is largely uncharacterized. We study differentially private federated class-incremental learning in this small-cohort regime, combining a public EEG dataset with a custom four-channel MUSE 2 recording set into a K=5-client federation, and give a masked per-class training protocol that avoids degenerate gradients and missing negative supervision under class-incremental streaming. We propose FedEEG, combining Synaptic-Intelligence consolidation with a lagged adaptive FedProx term, and show it reduces catastrophic forgetting relative to FedAvg, FedProx, and a federated Learning-without-Forgetting baseline; on-device balanced-batch replay reduces mean forgetting by a further 56.7%.
