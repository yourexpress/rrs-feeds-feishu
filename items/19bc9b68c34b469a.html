KINDER: Kernel-based Independence for Fair Representation Learning via Prototype-space Erasure

arXiv (cs.LG) · 2026-10-30 · 论文PDF

Concept erasure is a prominent approach to achieving fairness in machine learning through the removal of sensitive attributes from learned representations. Prior concept erasure methods typically define debiasing indirectly through the failure of a chosen adversary, probe, or fairness penalty, which makes the target of erasure dependent on a particular decoder family or optimization setup. We introduce KINDER, which directly removes sensitive-attribute information from intermediate representations with respect to a chosen family of prototype functionals, and is compatible with any deep model containing an intermediate feature space, independent of the architecture, task type, or training paradigm. KINDER operates in a random Fourier feature space, where it estimates a sensitive subspace from protected group prototypes and projects representations onto its orthogonal complement. Thus, it forms an explicit representation cleaning mechanism distinct from prior fairness control studies.
