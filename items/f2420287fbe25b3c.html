Plurality Consensus for $h$-Majority via First-Repetition Rules

arXiv cs.DC · 2026-11-11 · 论文PDF

In the h-Majority dynamics, each of n agents holds one of k opinions and, in every synchronous round, observes the opinions of h agents chosen independently and uniformly at random and adopts a most frequent one among them, breaking ties uniformly at random. Let M_0 be the initial number of agents holding the most frequent opinion, and Delta_0 its bias, its lead over the second most frequent opinion. We show that, for every sample size h >= 3, if Delta_0 >= L sqrt(M_0 log n) max{1, n/(h^2 M_0)} for a sufficiently large constant L, then h-Majority reaches consensus on the initially most frequent opinion within O((1 + n/(h^2 M_0)) log n) rounds with high probability. This is the time bound proved for the DejaVu dynamics with h samples (arXiv:2604.03648); our bias condition is that of DejaVu when h >= sqrt(n/M_0), and larger by a factor sqrt(n/(h^2 M_0)) otherwise. Our main contribution, the analysis of one round, rests on a comparison principle: among all rules that read the same observations and treat opinions symmetrically, plurality maximizes the difference between the probability of outputting the most frequent opinion and that of outputting any fixed competitor.
