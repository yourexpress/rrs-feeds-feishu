SparseForge: Beyond Spike Sparsity toward Hardware-Exploitable Neuron Pruning for Efficient SNN Acceleration

arXiv (cs.AR) · 2026-11-01 · 论文PDF

Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) provide an event-driven paradigm for energy-efficient edge computing. However, operation-level sparsity in SNNs does not necessarily eliminate the hardware costs associated with redundant output neurons, including neuron-state maintenance, partial-sum (psum) accesses, and on-chip storage requirements. This paper presents SparseForge, a hardware-aware sparse SNN acceleration framework that jointly optimizes neuron pruning, sparse dataflow, and accelerator microarchitecture. At the algorithm level, we introduce a gated leaky integrate-and-fire (G-LIF) neuron to learn differentiable neuron masks with binary spike semantics. A dual regularization scheme is further developed to align pruning with both synaptic-operation reduction and psum buffer capacity reduction. At the dataflow level, we design a sparse Column-Wise Product (CWP) dataflow with a variant of incremental spike encoding to improve weight-access regularity and reduce sparse-index overhead under dynamic spike activity.
